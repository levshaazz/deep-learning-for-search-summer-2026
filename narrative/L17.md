## Thesis
Information is surprise: I(x) = −log₂ p(x). Entropy H = −Σ p log₂ p is *expected* surprise — and the
hard floor of compression (source coding theorem). English carries ~1 bit per letter (Shannon's
guessing game), far below the 4.7-bit naive estimate, because language leans on context (Markov).
Cross-entropy is the bill paid *above* the floor — exactly what a language model's loss measures —
and IDF from L3 is self-information wearing a retrieval uniform.
