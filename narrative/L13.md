## Spine position
Sits squarely in **Territory III — The Bridge (Rank)**, but in its **engine room**: not *which* order, but how
to deliver that order **at galaxy scale, forever**. L10 built the neural cascade and L12 the Alliance — both still
assume you can *sweep* the corpus. **L13 is the border crossing from "correct ranking" to "ranking that survives
10⁹ vectors under a latency budget":** approximate nearest neighbor (HNSW / IVF), vector compression (PQ), and
the production serving that wraps them (FAISS, vector DBs, caching, the SLA). The Ship part it builds is the
**navigation system** — the jump-gate network and the compressed star-charts that let the Bridge issue an order
in tens of milliseconds instead of seconds. The hook is the **death of linear scan**: everything before L13
quietly assumed `O(N)` was affordable; here it stops being affordable, and the Wraith (L2) is the reason why.

## Per climb — worked toy examples, numbers, data schema, generator, gating
> **Lesson honored (L12):** map each worked-computation slide to the RIGHT `data-type`. The graph/scatter/encode
> figures are `archflow`/`viz`/`e2e`; **every by-hand arithmetic slide is `data-type="formula"`** (L12 regressed 7
> slides by typing `e2e` where `formula`'s 280% formula-stage was needed).
> **Coverage-guard mechanics (get this right):** the signature `\d+\.\d{2,}` matches a decimal with **≥2 fractional
> digits**. So decimals with ≥2 places (recall **0.6667**, distances **0.7071 / 1.58 / 2.55**) **are** caught and
> **MUST** be gated `[C]`. But **single-decimal recalls (1.0, 0.0) are NOT caught** — the guard sees no ≥2-digit
> decimal, so they slip through silently and each needs an **explicit [C]-claim** (or a `1.00`/`0.00` render). Integers
> (bytes 32/4, ms 89, k=256, tokens) are likewise un-caught and still get `[C]` claims for drift safety.

### Climb 1 — `climb-hnsw` (greedy search on a tiny small-world graph) · deck `archflow` + `formula`
- **Toy:** 6 nodes in 2-D so distances are hand-computable. `n0(0,0) n1(2,0) n2(4,0) n3(1,3) n4(3,3) n5(5,2)`,
  query `q(4.5,0.5)`. Base-layer edges (M=2): n0–n1, n0–n3, n1–n2, n1–n3, n2–n4, n2–n5, n3–n4, n4–n5; entry =
  n0 (upper layer). Greedy: at n0 (dist 4.53) → nearest neighbour n1 (**2.55**) < n3 (4.30) → move; at n1 → n2
  (**0.707**) < n3 → move; at n2 → neighbours n4 (2.92), n5 (**1.58**) both > 0.707 → **local min, stop. NN = n2.**
- **Numbers it produces:** brute-force NN = n2 at **0.7071**; greedy path n0→n1→n2 = **2 hops**, **3 nodes on the
  descent path**; **recall@1 = 1.0**. **This toy shows the MECHANISM — greedy descent — not a speedup:** on only 6
  nodes greedy still evaluates distances to most nodes (every visited node's neighbours), so there is **no eval saving
  here** and we make **no count-the-evals claim**. The speed win is **asymptotic** and is made on **`climb-latency`** and
  the **"now 10⁶ vectors"** zoom — there the graph reaches the NN in **O(log N)** hops instead of scanning **O(N)**.
  Second scenario (the trap): a query where ef=1 stops at a local min → **recall@1 = 0.0**, ef=3 keeps a candidate list
  → **recall@1 = 1.0** (the ef knob).
- **`data/l9-hnsw.json` schema:** `{coords:{nodes:[[x,y]…]}, edges:[[i,j]…], query:[x,y], bruteForce:{nn,dist},
  greedy:{path:[…], hops, nodesVisited, recall}, trap:{ef1:{recall}, ef3:{recall}}, knobs:{M, ef}}`.
- **Generator:** `_research/gen_l9.py` (stdlib): Euclidean dist, deterministic greedy, fixed coords → byte-stable.
- **Gating:** `[C]` deck+book claims on distances 0.7071 / 1.58 / 2.55 (≥2-dp → coverage-gated) and recall 1.0/0.0;
  `provenance_l9` recomputes greedy from coords and asserts `path`/`hops`/`recall` match (cross-file/structural).

### Climb 2 — `climb-ivf` (recall@k vs nprobe) · deck `archflow`/`viz` + `formula`
- **Toy:** 9 points in 2-D, **nlist=3** cells with frozen centroids `c0,c1,c2` (precomputed k-means, committed).
  Query `q` lands in cell c0; its true **3-NN** are 2 points in c0 and 1 point that sits just across the border in
  c1. **nprobe=1** → probes c0 only → finds **2/3** → recall@3 = **0.6667**; **nprobe=2** → also probes c1 →
  **3/3** → recall@3 = **1.0**.
- **Geometry must be COMMITTED and CHECKED (or the lesson doesn't hold):** the **9 point coordinates + the 3
  centroids** are frozen in `l9-ivf.json`, and the build **asserts the geometry actually produces the lesson** —
  **exactly 2 of the 3 true NN fall in c0, the 3rd falls in c1, AND c1 is the *second-nearest* cell to q** (by
  centroid distance). If any of these fails — e.g. the 3rd NN lands in c2, or c1 is not the 2nd-nearest cell — then
  nprobe=2 would *not* reach the 3rd NN and recall would not climb 0.6667→1.0, breaking the climb. `provenance_l9`
  pins all three conditions, not just the recall arithmetic.
- **`data/l9-ivf.json` schema:** `{points:[[x,y]…], assign:[cellIdx…], centroids:[[x,y]…], query:[x,y],
  trueNN:[idx,idx,idx], cellRankByDist:[c0,c1,c2], probe:{"1":{cells,found,recall}, "2":{cells,found,recall}}}`.
- **Generator:** `gen_l9.py` (stdlib): assign each point to nearest centroid; recall = |trueNN ∩ probed| / 3;
  emit `cellRankByDist` (cells ordered by centroid-distance to q) so the 2nd-nearest-cell condition is checkable.
- **Gating:** `[C]` on **0.6667** (≥2-dp → coverage-caught) and **1.0** (single-decimal → **NOT** coverage-caught,
  needs an explicit [C]-claim or a `1.00` render); `provenance_l9` recomputes assignment + recall from
  points/centroids **and** asserts the committed geometry (2-in-c0 / 1-in-c1 / c1 = 2nd-nearest cell).

### Climb 3 — `climb-pq` (subvector / codebook + memory math) · deck `formula` + `e2e`
- **Toy (by-hand, exact):** D=8 float32 vector → **m=4** subvectors of d\*=2 dims → each subvector → nearest of
  **k=256** centroids → index fits in **1 byte** → **4 bytes/vector**. Float32 baseline = 8 × 4 = **32 bytes**.
  **Compression = 32/4 = 8×.** ADC: split the query into m subvectors, precompute an m×k distance table, sum
  lookups for an approximate distance.
- **Scale-up (representative, cited):** a 768-d embedding → m=96, k=256 → **96 bytes** vs **3072 bytes** = **32×**;
  a 128-d vector → m=8 → **8 bytes** vs **512** = **64×** (the bytes/vector quoted in vector-DB docs).
- **Numbers:** 32 → 4 bytes (8×); 3072 → 96 (32×); 512 → 8 (64×). A **representative recall** for PQ-m4 vs exact
  (≈ 0.70 recall@1) is a *cited/frozen* number (see frontier), **not** presented as the hand-computed value.
- **`data/l9-pq.json` schema:** `{toy:{D, m, dStar, k, bytesFloat32, bytesPQ, compression}, scale:[{dim, m, k,
  bytesFloat32, bytesPQ, compression}…], adc:{tableShape:[m,k]}, recallRepresentative:{m4:…, source}}`.
- **Generator:** `gen_l9.py` (stdlib): pure integer byte arithmetic; the recall figure is cited verbatim with its
  source string in `l9-bench.json` (not computed here).
- **Gating:** bytes/compression are integers (coverage-exempt) but `[C]`-claimed; the representative recall
  decimal is `[C]`-gated against `l9-bench.json`; `provenance_l9` pins `bytesPQ = m`, `compression = bytesFloat32/bytesPQ`.

### Climb 4 — `climb-latency` (serving budget) · deck `sequence` (REUSE the L1 slide-type)
- **Toy (exact sum):** client→API **+3** · embed query **+8** · **ANN search** over 10⁶ quantized vectors **+12**
  · rerank top-100 (the L7/L8 cross-encoder, now *behind* ANN) **+60** · cache+log (async) **+2** · response **+4**
  → **total 89ms < 200ms SLA**. Contrast hop: *exact* scan of 10⁶×768-d ≈ **>500ms** — the search hop alone blows
  the budget. Cache-hit path: repeated query ≈ **5ms** total.
- **`data/l9-latency.json` schema:** `{sla:200, budget:[{step,from,to,label,lat,kind?}…], total, exactScanMs,
  cacheHitMs}` (the L1 `sequence` shape).
- **Generator:** `gen_l9.py` (stdlib): `total = sum(lat)`; the per-hop values are *representative serving numbers*
  (like L1's), the **sum is the exact gated computation**.
- **Gating:** `[C]` claim that the displayed total == `sum(budget[].lat)` (the L1 `sequence` total pattern);
  `provenance_l9` asserts `total == Σ lat` and `total < sla`. Per-hop ms are integers (coverage-exempt).

## Frontier + lab
- **Frontier — fold into `climb-pq` (after the taught PQ baseline):** **TurboQuant: Online Vector Quantization
  with Near-optimal Distortion Rate** — Amir Zandieh, Majid Daliri, Majid Hadian, Vahab Mirrokni, **arXiv:2504.19874**
  (2025, Google Research; cs.LG/cs.DB/cs.DS/cs.AI). *Why it's here:* it removes PQ's biggest production pain — **codebook
  training** — by being **data-oblivious and online**: randomly rotate the vector (inducing a concentrated **Beta**
  distribution on coordinates), then apply a **per-coordinate optimal scalar quantizer**; a two-stage **MSE + 1-bit
  QJL on the residual** gives an **unbiased inner-product** estimate. It matches the information-theoretic distortion
  lower bound within a small (**≈2.7×**) constant, and on NN search **outperforms existing PQ techniques in recall
  while reducing indexing time to virtually zero**. **VERIFICATION (cross-checked against the arXiv abstract):**
  the verified claims are *data-oblivious / online / no codebook*, *random-rotation → Beta-concentration →
  per-coordinate scalar quantizer*, *≈2.7× from the lower bound*, *better recall with near-zero indexing time*. **Two
