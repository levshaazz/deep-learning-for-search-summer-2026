ncd-rag, three steps, and it is here to say the three things the block diagram on the previous slide structurally cannot. Same loop, same stages — a different claim. Step zero, retrieve. Look at the left: the corpus is a dashed REGION and it ENCLOSES the N documents. That is the point. A block diagram draws the corpus as a box with an arrow coming out of it, as if the corpus were a thing you fetch from; in the circuit it is the AXIS the query is broadcast over — the query is scored against every one of the N documents, and a region is how this notation writes "for all of these". Each document is a pentagon, an element: encoded offline, only looked up now. The wires bundle into the cheap bi-encoder, and the query comes into that same retriever from above — a second axis in. Then the hexagon. In the L06 grammar a hexagon is a reindex or a slice, and top-k is exactly a slice, so the narrowing N to K is a hexagon and nothing else. That is the recall stage: cast a wide net cheaply, get an ordering that is only roughly right. Step one, rerank. A second hexagon, a second slice: K to k. Two hexagons — and that is the entire cascade from lecture seven, in shape. Watch the ledger: the work line goes from N cheap dot products to plus K full BERT passes, and the reason you can afford that at all is that the first hexagon already threw K's complement away. The reranker never looks at the corpus. Step two, generate, and this is the slide's real payoff. Follow the dashed arc: the query enters a SECOND time, straight into the top of the generator. Everybody says RAG grounds the answer in the retrieved evidence; almost nobody draws it. The equation two slides back already told you — answer equals G of q and R sub k of q — and q appears twice in that expression. The circuit is that expression: two wires into the generator, the evidence and the question the evidence was fetched for, and one answer out. The whole loop is one funnel — N to K to k to one — and every narrowing in it is a hexagon. N, K and k here are shapes, not measurements: this figure prices the loop in axes, not in milliseconds.
