The mechanism behind the act's claim that flat retrieval "cannot assemble the chain" and that single-hop recall is zero on the anchor question. The problem slide states the failure; this shows why it is structural rather than a matter of tuning. Top-k scores every document independently of every other, so the returned set is the k best singletons. What the multi-hop question needs is the best SET: neither d1 nor d2 answers it alone, but together they do, so usefulness is superadditive and no ranking by solo score can express that. Worse, d2's score is computed against a query that does not contain the bridging entity — the very fact that links them is latent at query time, so d2 competes on accidental similarity only. This is also the answer to the obvious objection: yes, raising k eventually sweeps d2 in, but only alongside everything else that resembles the query, and with no guarantee at any particular depth — you pay in precision for a chance, not a mechanism. Two escapes, and they are the shape of the rest of the course: make the entity explicit by querying twice (the agentic loop of L16), or precompute the edge so the link is stored rather than rediscovered — the entity graph the next act builds. Deliberately symbolic: d1, d2 and the anchor question are the ones already on the previous slide.
