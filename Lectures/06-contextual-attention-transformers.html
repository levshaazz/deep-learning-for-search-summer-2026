«bank»: один вектор, два смысла“bank”: one vector, two meanings

«river bank» и «bank account» делят один статический вектор из L5 — его координаты усредняют оба смысла. Контекста в нём нет.“river bank” and “bank account” share one static L5 vector — its coordinates average both senses. There is no context in it.

State the problem with the canonical example. In the static map of Lecture 5, the word bank has exactly one vector, fixed before we ever see a sentence. But river bank and bank account are different things. The single point is forced to sit somewhere between the two senses, averaging them, which is wrong for both. No amount of training a static embedding fixes this — the limitation is structural, one word, one vector. What is missing is context: the vector should depend on the sentence the word appears in. That is the whole motivation for contextual embeddings, and attention is how we build them.

The wish, stated as a definition. We want to upgrade the embedding from a function of the word alone to a function of the word and its context. Then the same surface word bank produces two distinct vectors when its neighbours differ — river pulls it one way, account the other. The question is how a model can let neighbours influence a token's vector, in a way that is learnable and parallel. The answer is the attention mechanism, and we build it next.
Часть 01Part 01

A Vector per Context

Не координата на слово — координата на употребление:Not a coordinate per word — a coordinate per use: одно слово, разные точки в разных предложениях.the same word, different points in different sentences.

The turn. We are moving from a static map to a dynamic one. In the static world each word was a fixed star; in the contextual world a word's position is computed fresh for each sentence. The same token can land in two different places depending on what surrounds it. This single change is what splits the two banks apart, and it is also what makes a sentence vector finally meaningful — because the parts now know about each other. The mechanism that computes those context-aware positions is attention.

Make the contrast concrete. A static embedding, like word2vec or GloVe, is a lookup table — you index in with the word string and read out a fixed vector. A contextual embedding, as in ELMo in early 2018 and then BERT, is the output of a neural network that has read the whole sentence, so the vector for bank genuinely differs between river bank and bank account. The price is computation: a lookup is instant, but a contextual vector requires running the sentence through a stack of attention layers. The rest of this lecture builds that stack, one mechanism at a time.
Часть 02Part 02

Let Each Token Attend

Каждый токен — советник:Every token is a councillor: его новый смысл — взвешенная смесь соседей, к которым он прислушался.its new meaning is a weighted blend of the neighbours it heeded.

The attention idea, before any matrices. Picture each token as a councillor at the round table. To update its own meaning, it does not shout — it listens. It asks every other token how relevant they are to it, gets a weight for each, and then forms its new representation as a weighted average of what those tokens contain. A token surrounded by river will heed river; the same token surrounded by account will heed account. That weighted blend is the entire intuition. Next we make it precise with three roles — query, key, value — and a dot product.

The three roles, with the search analogy you already know. Every token produces three vectors. The query is what this token is looking for. The key is what each token advertises about itself. The value is what a token actually contributes if it is attended to. The attention weight between two tokens is the similarity of one's query to the other's key, so a token with a matching key gets a high weight. The output for a token is the weighted sum of all the values, using those weights. It is exactly soft retrieval, but inside one sentence: the query matches keys, and the matching values are blended in. The picture shows one token's query lighting up its most relevant neighbours.

Метафора заработана — теперь точные числа:The metaphor is earned — now the exact numbers: \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\) на «the cat sat».\(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\) on “the cat sat”.

The formula, read left to right. Stack the queries, keys and values as matrices whose rows are tokens. Q K transpose gives a T by T matrix whose entry i j is the dot product of query i with key j — the raw match score. Divide every entry by root d k; with a key dimension of four that divisor is exactly two. Apply softmax along each row, which turns that row of scores into a probability distribution that sums to one — the attention weights. Finally multiply by V, so each output row is the weighted average of the value rows. Three matrix multiplies and a softmax; that is the whole mechanism. Next we run it on real numbers.

Зачем делить на \(\sqrt{d_k}\)?Why divide by \(\sqrt{d_k}\)?

не косметика — спасает градиентыnot cosmetic — it saves gradients
Что думают студентыWhat students think

«Деление на \(\sqrt{d_k}\) — просто нормировка для красоты».“Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) is just cosmetic normalisation.”

Нет. Скалярное произведение двух случайных векторов размерности \(d_k\) имеет дисперсию \(\propto d_k\): при больших \(d_k\) скоры огромны, softmax насыщается (один вес \(\to 1\), остальные \(\to 0\)), а его градиент \(\to 0\) — обучение глохнет. Деление на \(\sqrt{d_k}\) возвращает дисперсию к \(\approx 1\).No. The dot product of two random \(d_k\)-vectors has variance \(\propto d_k\): for large \(d_k\) the scores are huge, softmax saturates (one weight \(\to 1\), the rest \(\to 0\)), and its gradient \(\to 0\) — training stalls. Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) rescales the variance back to \(\approx 1\).

Урок.Lesson. \(\sqrt{d_k}\) держит скоры в зоне, где softmax «мягкий» и градиент течёт. Здесь \(d_k=4\Rightarrow\sqrt{d_k}=2.0\).\(\sqrt{d_k}\) keeps scores in the range where softmax stays soft and gradients flow. Here \(d_k=4\Rightarrow\sqrt{d_k}=2.0\).

A short but important honesty beat — the scaling is not decoration. If queries and keys have roughly unit-variance components, their dot product over d k dimensions has variance proportional to d k, so for a realistic key size of sixty-four the raw scores swing into the tens. Feed scores that large into softmax and it saturates: one weight goes to one, the rest to zero, and right there the gradient of softmax collapses to nearly zero, so no learning signal flows back. Dividing by root d k pulls the variance back to about one, keeping softmax in its soft, differentiable regime. In our toy example d k is four, so the divisor is exactly two — small, but the principle is the same one that matters at scale.

Self-Attention

\(Q\), \(K\), \(V\) — из одной последовательности:\(Q\), \(K\), \(V\) all come from the same sequence: каждый токен запрашивает каждый токен, включая себя.every token queries every token, including itself.

A precise naming. In our worked example the queries, keys and values all came from the same three tokens — that is self-attention. Every token both asks and answers; every token attends to every token, itself included, which is exactly why cat could put weight on cat. This is the variant inside an encoder like BERT. Later, in encoder-decoder models, you also see cross-attention, where the queries come from one sequence and the keys and values from another — but the arithmetic is identical. For building contextual embeddings, self-attention is the workhorse.

Attention is Order-Blind

«dog bites man» = «man bites dog»“dog bites man” = “man bites dog” — без позиции внимание видит мешок токенов. Вернём порядок.— without position, attention sees a bag of tokens. Give order back.

The order problem, and it is a real one. Look back at the attention formula: it is a sum over tokens, and a sum does not care about order. Permute the input tokens and you simply permute the output rows — the actual computation is identical. That means dog bites man and man bites dog would, to a pure attention layer, be the same bag of tokens with the same representations. For language that is fatal; word order carries meaning. So before the first attention layer we add a positional encoding to every token, a signal that says where in the sequence this token sits. The sinusoidal scheme is next.

Make the invariance explicit, because it surprises people. Attention computes, for each token, a weighted sum over all tokens; permute the rows of the input and the set of pairwise scores is unchanged, so the outputs simply permute along. Formally the layer is equivariant to permutation and carries no inherent notion of first, second, third. Recurrent networks got order for free because they processed tokens one at a time; a Transformer processes them all at once, in parallel, which is exactly why it is fast but also why it is order-blind. The remedy is to add a positional encoding to each input token before the stack — position is injected, not emergent.

The exact positional encoding from the original Transformer. For a token at position pos, dimension number two i gets the sine, and two i plus one gets the cosine, of pos divided by ten thousand to the power two i over d. The effect is that each embedding dimension is a sinusoid with its own wavelength: the lowest dimensions cycle quickly, encoding fine local order, while the highest cycle very slowly, encoding coarse absolute position. A quick trade-off so you know the landscape. The sinusoidal encoding has zero parameters because it is a closed-form function, and it can in principle handle positions longer than any seen in training, since you just evaluate the sinusoids further out. A learned positional encoding instead stores a lookup table of one vector per position up to some maximum length — flexible, but it cannot extrapolate past that maximum and adds parameters. The original Transformer paper reported the two performing about equally and chose sinusoids for the extrapolation property. In practice BERT and GPT-2 used learned tables; and modern models often use relative schemes like RoPE or ALiBi, which encode the offset between tokens rather than absolute position. The principle, though, is constant: position must be injected.

The block compressed to two lines, which is worth memorising. H is the layer-norm of X plus multi-head attention of X — the mixing sublayer with its residual. The structural point in the callout is what makes a Transformer a Transformer: input and output are both T-by-d matrices, the shape is preserved, so you can feed Z straight into an identical block and repeat N times. Train the Space for Search

Готовые векторы не заточены под поиск:Off-the-shelf vectors aren’t tuned for search: тяни релевантное вместе, отталкивай нерелевантное. Сэр Косинус — линейка потерь.pull relevant together, push the rest apart. Sir Cosine rules the loss.

The second turn of the lecture. We have a contextual encoder, but its raw vectors are not organised for retrieval. Contrastive learning fixes that by training directly on the thing we care about: relative similarity. For an anchor — say a query — we define a positive that should be close, and negatives that should be far, and we adjust the encoder to pull the positive in and push the negatives out. The ruler inside this objective is cosine similarity, so Sir Cosine, who has ridden every lecture since L2, now powers the loss itself. This is the step that finally makes the space a search space.

The trick that makes contrastive training cheap and scalable. Naively you would have to go hunting for negative examples for every anchor. In-batch negatives skip that entirely: take a batch of B query-positive pairs, and for any one query, its own positive is the answer while the other B minus one positives in the batch are perfectly good negatives — they belong to different queries. So a batch of B pairs yields B squared comparisons, and every negative is free, no mining required. Bigger batches give more negatives and a sharper signal, which is one reason dense-retrieval training likes large batches. This is exactly how DPR, from Karpukhin and colleagues in 2020, trains its dense passage retriever; harder mined negatives are then layered on top for the difficult cases.

A short conceptual beat that ties the loss back to the ruler. Why not just use the raw dot product of two contextual vectors? Because the dot product mixes two things: the angle between the vectors, which carries meaning, and their magnitudes, which often just track word frequency or sentence length. Normalising every vector to unit length removes the magnitude, and the dot product becomes pure cosine — the angle alone. Training contrastively on cosine arranges all the embeddings on the surface of a unit sphere, where being near means being similar in meaning, which is exactly what retrieval wants. It is the same Sir Cosine ruler from Lecture 5, now baked into the training objective rather than only used at query time.

Сырые контекстные векторы лгут о близости:Raw contextual vectors lie about nearness: они сбиваются в узкий конус. Обратный вызов из L2.they crowd into a narrow cone. A callback to L2.

The catch beat, P7, and it is a callback to the high-dimensional pathologies of Lecture 2. Here is the surprise: take a raw, untuned BERT and embed a pile of random sentences, and their vectors do not spread out over the space — they bunch into a narrow cone, all pointing roughly the same way. This is called anisotropy, and it means even unrelated sentences have high cosine similarity, so cosine stops discriminating. It is exactly why raw BERT [CLS] underperformed GloVe. The trap, made precise, with the L2 callback. Anisotropy means the embedding cloud is not spread evenly — it occupies a thin cone, so any two vectors, even for unrelated sentences, share a small angle and therefore a deceptively high cosine, around zero point six or more on random pairs. Cosine cannot rank well when everything looks similar, and this is the same curse-of-dimensionality pathology the Wraith embodied back in Lecture 2 — distances concentrate, contrast vanishes. The cure is twofold and is precisely what we just built: contrastive training actively pushes negatives apart, spreading the cone, and normalisation onto the sphere restores a usable geometry.
