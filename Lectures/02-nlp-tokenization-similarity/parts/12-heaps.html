Heaps' law: the vocabulary never saturates

The argument for subwords

As you read more text \(N\), the number of distinct types \(V\) keeps growing as \(V \propto N^{\beta}\) — sublinear, but unbounded. Measured on 3.66 M tokens of 20 Newsgroups: \(\beta \approx 0.59\), still 94 287 distinct types and climbing (\(R^2=0.998\)). No finite word list ever covers everything.

Heaps and Zipf are two views of the same fact. The punchline — say it loud: a fixed WORD vocabulary can never cover real text. New names, typos, compounds, hashtags, code identifiers arrive forever. That is the direct, non-negotiable argument for SUBWORD tokenization: bound the vocabulary, and rebuild any unseen word from pieces. Everything in Part 2 follows from this slide.
