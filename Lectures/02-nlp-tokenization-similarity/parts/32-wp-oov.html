Жадное совпадение по самому длинному переиспользует куски из обучения, затем откатывается к отдельным символам (Greedy longest-match reuses pieces from training, then falls back to single characters (quicker → → quick ##e ##r); слово схлопывается в ); a word collapses to [UNK] только когда один из его символов ни разу не встречался в обучающем алфавите ( only when one of its characters was never in the training alphabet (café).).

This contrasts WordPiece with byte-level BPE. WordPiece's base alphabet is the characters of the training data, not all bytes — so any word whose letters all appeared in training decomposes to known pieces plus single-character fallbacks, and never becomes [UNK]. Same corpus as the previous slide, "the quick brown fox". Note the partial win: "quicker" wasn't seen, yet the learned piece "quick" (built on the previous slide) is reused, then e and r fall back to character pieces ##e ##r. [UNK] is reserved for genuinely out-of-alphabet characters — here the accented é in "café", which never appeared in the training text (nor did the letter a). (Unlike byte-level BPE, which has no [UNK] at all because every byte is in its alphabet.)
