Compounds & non-concatenative morphology

German · Arabic

German compounds

Donaudampfschifffahrts­gesellschaftskapitän

→ Donau ##dampf ##schiff ##fahrt ##gesellschaft ##kapitän

One orthographic word, six morphemes ("Danube steamship company captain"). Subwords recover the parts a word vocabulary would miss.

Arabic: root + pattern

The triliteral root k-t-b ("write") interleaves with patterns:

  • ·kataba — he wrote
  • ·kattaba — he made (someone) write
  • ·iktataba — he subscribed

Meaning is woven through the root, not appended — so simple left-to-right merging fits poorly.

Two stress tests for subword merging. German concatenates freely, so words can be arbitrarily long — subwords handle this gracefully by recovering morphemes. Arabic is non-concatenative: the same root k-t-b appears with vowels and affixes woven inside it, so the "shared piece" is discontinuous and contiguous-merge tokenizers can't capture it cleanly. This is a known weak spot for BPE/WordPiece on Semitic languages.
