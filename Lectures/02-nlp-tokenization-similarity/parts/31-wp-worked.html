overview
Walk the three scores aloud: (q,##u) = (1·29)/(1·1) = 29; after merging, the vocabulary grows by one so the factor becomes 30, giving (qu,##i)=30; then (qui,##c)=31. The word "quick" assembles piece by piece. Inference is different from training: it is greedy longest-match, left to right — the first piece is bare, every continuation gets ##. A word becomes [UNK] only if it contains a character outside the training alphabet; otherwise it falls back to known pieces plus single-character pieces (unlike byte-level BPE, which has no [UNK] at all). See the next slide.
Because q, u, i, c are each unique here, the real criterion freq(AB)/(freq(A)·freq(B)) equals 1 for every pair — a tie. The ×|V| factor on screen is only a legibility crutch so the steps read as distinct numbers; it is not part of real WordPiece and does not change which pair would win. On a real corpus the marginal counts differ and the unscaled score genuinely separates the pairs.
