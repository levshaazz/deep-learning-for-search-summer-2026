Score & merge
corpus = "the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog"

In this small corpus \(\texttt{q}\), \(\texttt{u}\), \(\texttt{i}\), \(\texttt{c}\) each occur once; the vocabulary size factor works out to \(29\).

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{q},\texttt{\#\#u}) = \frac{1\cdot 29}{1\cdot 1} = 29$$

Highest score → merge into qu.

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{qu},\texttt{\#\#i}) = \frac{1\cdot 30}{1\cdot 1} = 30$$

Vocabulary grew by one, so the factor is now \(30\). Merge into qui.

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{qui},\texttt{\#\#c}) = \frac{1\cdot 31}{1\cdot 1} = 31 \to \texttt{quic} \to \texttt{quick}$$

Continue until the whole word quick is one token.

$$\texttt{quickness} \to \texttt{qu}\;\texttt{\#\#ick}\;\texttt{\#\#ness}$$

Encoding is greedy longest-match, left → right; the first piece is bare, the rest get ##.

overview
Walk the three scores aloud: (q,##u) = (1·29)/(1·1) = 29; after merging, the vocabulary grows by one so the factor becomes 30, giving (qu,##i)=30; then (qui,##c)=31. The word "quick" assembles piece by piece. Inference is different from training: it is greedy longest-match, left to right — the first piece is bare, every continuation gets ##. If no piece matches, the whole word becomes [UNK] (unlike byte-level BPE).
Because q, u, i, c are each unique here, every numerator pair-count is 1 and the score is just the vocab factor — a clean demonstration of the formula.
