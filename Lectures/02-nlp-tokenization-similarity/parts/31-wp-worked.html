Score & merge
corpus = "the quick brown fox"

In this tiny corpus \(\texttt{q}\), \(\texttt{u}\), \(\texttt{i}\), \(\texttt{c}\) each occur once, so the real criterion gives \(\tfrac{1}{1\cdot 1}=1\) for every pair — a tie. To make the steps legible we display a pedagogical re-scaling (numerator \(\times|V|\), here \(|V|=29\)); it does not change which pair WordPiece picks — greedy assembly of quick proceeds the same way regardless.

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{q},\texttt{\#\#u}) = \frac{1\cdot 29}{1\cdot 1} = 29$$

Highest score → merge into qu.

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{qu},\texttt{\#\#i}) = \frac{1\cdot 30}{1\cdot 1} = 30$$

Vocabulary grew by one, so the factor is now \(30\). Merge into qui.

$$\mathrm{score}(\texttt{qui},\texttt{\#\#c}) = \frac{1\cdot 31}{1\cdot 1} = 31 \to \texttt{quic} \to \texttt{quick}$$

Continue until the whole word quick is one token.

$$\texttt{quicker} \to \texttt{quick}\;\texttt{\#\#e}\;\texttt{\#\#r}$$

Encoding is greedy longest-match, left → right; the first piece is bare, the rest get ##.

overview
Walk the three scores aloud: (q,##u) = (1·29)/(1·1) = 29; after merging, the vocabulary grows by one so the factor becomes 30, giving (qu,##i)=30; then (qui,##c)=31. The word "quick" assembles piece by piece. Inference is different from training: it is greedy longest-match, left to right — the first piece is bare, every continuation gets ##. A word becomes [UNK] only if it contains a character outside the training alphabet; otherwise it falls back to known pieces plus single-character pieces (unlike byte-level BPE, which has no [UNK] at all). Because q, u, i, c are each unique here, the real criterion freq(AB)/(freq(A)·freq(B)) equals 1 for every pair — a tie. The ×|V| factor on screen is only a legibility crutch so the steps read as distinct numbers; it is not part of real WordPiece and does not change which pair would win. On a real corpus the marginal counts differ and the unscaled score genuinely separates the pairs.
