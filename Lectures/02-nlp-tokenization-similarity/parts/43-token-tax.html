The multilingual "token tax"

fertility = tokens per word

Same content, more tokens in many languages. Train a 16k BPE on 20 Newsgroups (mostly English) and the word «привет» (Russian "hello") takes 12 byte-level tokens; English "tokenization" takes 3. \(2\times\) fertility → ~\(2\times\) cost in the typical (FFN-dominated) regime, approaching \(4\times\) only once attention's \(O(n^{2})\) term dominates (very long sequences), plus ~\(2\times\) API price and latency, and a smaller effective context window.

This is a fairness story, not just an efficiency one. Fertility is tokens-per-word. A tokenizer trained mostly on English is cheap for English and expensive for Hindi, Telugu, Turkish — and Russian. The fertility numbers in the caption are not hand-waved: `_research/data/bpe_merges.py` trains a 16k-vocab byte-level BPE on 20 Newsgroups and reports the exact token-counts for English vs Russian demo strings. The Token Tax (2025) work: each extra token/word costs roughly 8–18 accuracy points and explains 20–50% of cross-lingual accuracy variance. Attention cost — which we define in the transformers lecture (L6) — grows with the square of sequence length, but for typical model sizes total compute is dominated by the linear feed-forward and projection layers, so 2× fertility is ~2× training compute in that regime, approaching ~4× only when the O(n²) attention term takes over at very long sequences; at inference it is ~2× the API bill, ~2× latency, and less room in the context window. Low-resource speakers literally pay more for worse service on identical information.
