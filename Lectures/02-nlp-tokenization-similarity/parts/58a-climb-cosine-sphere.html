Единичная сфера мирит косинус и ЕвклидаThe unit sphere reconciles cosine and Euclid

шаг (←/→): два вектора → линейка Евклида → угол → нормировка → одна формулаstep (←/→): two vectors → Euclid’s ruler → the angle → normalize → one formula

Соотношение с прошлого слайда — теперь его можно прокрутить. Линейка Евклида называет два сонаправленных вектора далёкими, косинус — одинаковыми; спроецируй оба на единичную сферу — и спор исчезает. После нормировки два взгляда связаны одной формулой: вот почему векторные базы позволяют себе подменять одно другим.The relationship from the previous slide — now you can turn it by hand. Euclid’s ruler calls two same-direction vectors far apart; cosine calls them identical; project both onto the unit sphere and the disagreement vanishes. After normalization the two views are tied by one formula — which is why vector databases allow themselves to swap one for the other.

The geometric climb of act 4, right after the how-the-three-relate slide. Vectors and values come from data/l2-cosine.json. Steps: (0) the same two vectors as the compute climb — deliberately, so the geometry maps onto arithmetic students just did; (1) Euclidean distance disagrees loudly; (2) the angle says identical; (3) normalize — both land on the same point of the unit sphere, DWELL here; (4) the identity linking squared distance and cosine for unit vectors. Students stumble on step 4’s formula — anchor it visually: on the sphere, walking apart IS opening the angle. This is also the quiet setup for the FAISS slide at the end of the act (inner product on normalized vectors) and for distance concentration in the high-D act.
