Ġ is that attached leading space. What glues together first is not words but frequent little fragments; only later does a merge combine earlier merges — that is how a sub-word grows into a whole word. Nobody wrote this list by hand; frequency dictated it.

Act 2’s payoff climb, mounted after the byte-level slide because that slide is what makes the ledger readable: the Ġ marker is the leading space the pre-tokenizer keeps attached. The toy loop earlier in this act showed HOW merges are chosen; this one shows WHAT a real tokenizer actually chose, read from data/l2-bpe.json. Steps: (0)–(3) reveal the early ranks one at a time — the payoff is the students’ surprise that the top of the list is whitespace and letter fragments, not words; (4) DWELL: the merge that combines two EARLIER merges is the recursion, and it is why the pieces grow; (5) the generalisation claim — enough such rules and any word, including one never seen, splits into known pieces, which is the [UNK] promise this act opened with. Do not read the ranks as English words — read them as evidence that nothing here was hand-designed.
