Quick check

Audience question
Why is raw text fundamentally harder for ML than images?
ASymbols are discrete, length varies, vocabulary is open.
BText files are larger than image files.
CImages come pre-labelled, text never does.
Reveal explanation
A. Three structural facts behind the kernel claim: discreteness (no built-in metric on symbols), variable length (inputs aren't a fixed-size grid), and an open vocabulary (Zipf's steep tail, Heaps' never-saturating \(V \propto N^{\beta}\)). Images are continuous, fixed-grid, interpolatable; text isn't. B is about storage, not learnability; C is false (text labels exist, just at a different granularity).
Let them commit. The point is to consolidate Part 1: the difficulty is structural, not about file sizes or hardware. These three facts are exactly what tokenization and embeddings exist to fix.
