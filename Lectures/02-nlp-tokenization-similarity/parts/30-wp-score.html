Деление на маргинальные частоты благоприятствует парам, встречающимся вместе чаще случайного — частый кусок вроде ##s штрафуется, если только не липнет к одному конкретному соседу. Оригинальный WordPiece выбирает слияние, сильнее всего увеличивающее правдоподобие корпуса при униграммной LM; это отношение — распространённое практическое приближение той цели.Dividing by the marginal counts favours pairs that co-occur more than chance — a common piece like ##s is penalised unless it sticks to one specific neighbour. The original WordPiece picks the merge that most increases corpus likelihood under a unigram LM; this ratio is the common practical approximation of that objective.

Read it as pointwise mutual information without the log. A pair of two very common pieces (say "th" + "e") has a big numerator but an even bigger denominator, so its score is modest. A pair where a rare piece always follows a specific piece scores high. This is why WordPiece tends to keep meaningful morphemes together. Note: real WordPiece uses exactly this freq(AB)/(freq(A)·freq(B)) ratio with no |V| factor. On the next slide's toy corpus every pair-count is 1, so the honest score is 1 for all pairs (a tie); the ×|V| we display there (29, 30, 31) is a legibility-only re-scaling, not a real variant.
