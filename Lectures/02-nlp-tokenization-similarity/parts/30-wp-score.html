WordPiece merge criterion

Likelihood, not raw frequency
$$ \mathrm{score}(A,B) = \frac{\mathrm{freq}(AB)}{\mathrm{freq}(A)\cdot \mathrm{freq}(B)} $$

Dividing by the marginal counts favours pairs that co-occur more than chance — a common piece like ##s is penalised unless it sticks to one specific neighbour.

Read it as pointwise mutual information without the log. A pair of two very common pieces (say "th" + "e") has a big numerator but an even bigger denominator, so its score is modest. A pair where a rare piece always follows a specific piece scores high. This is why WordPiece tends to keep meaningful morphemes together. Note: some textbook variants multiply by |V|; with our toy corpus |V| works out so the headline scores are 29, 30, 31.
