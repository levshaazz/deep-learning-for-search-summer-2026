Granularity on one sentence

"unhappiness is unbearable"

Same sentence, three ways to cut it. Watch vocabulary size and sequence length trade off.

01
Character level
u·n·h·a·p·p·i·n·e·s·s·… — tiny vocabulary, very long sequence. No OOV ever.
vocab \(\sim 10^{2}\)
02
Word level
unhappiness · is · unbearable — short sequence, but a huge vocabulary, and any unseen word becomes [UNK] (the OOV problem).
vocab \(\sim 10^{6}\), OOV
03
Subword (BPE / WordPiece)
un · happiness, or un · happ · iness — the middle ground. Common words stay whole; rare words decompose into known pieces.
vocab \(\sim 3\times10^{4}\)
→
Verdict
Subword wins for an open vocabulary: bounded size, reasonable sequence length, graceful handling of unseen words.
✓ subword
4/4
Walk it one row at a time. Character-level: trivially no OOV, but a 20-word sentence becomes 100+ tokens and the model must learn spelling. Word-level: clean and short, but you can never enumerate every word, and morphology ("unhappiness" = un + happy + ness) is invisible. Subword is the practical compromise nearly every modern model uses.
