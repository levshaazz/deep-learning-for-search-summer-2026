The stakes slide — motivates the whole course before the objectives. Two order-of-magnitude figures (illustrative, not precise): the web ≈ 10^12 pages, and Google has long quoted ≈15% of daily queries as brand-new (never seen). Plan A — pre-compute every answer — dies on the unseen 15% (tomorrow's questions don't exist yet). Plan B — scan the whole corpus per query — dies on the clock: even at 1 µs/doc, 10^12 docs is over a week of compute, far past the ~250 ms a user will wait. Neither brute force works; that tension — too much data, too little time — is the entire course in one breath. Mirrors the Book stakes-scale beat.

А вот и сама машинаAnd here is the machine

шаг (←/→): пулинг → индекс → воронкаstep (←/→): pooling → the index → the funnel

Тот слайд — маршрут. Этот — механизм. Пулинг — единственное место, где ось \(n\) умирает. Индекс — не вторая машина, а тот же кодировщик, прогнанный по \(N\) документам заранее. Дальше — только сужение, и каждая стрелка стоит денег.That slide was the route. This one is the machine. Pooling is the one place where the axis \(n\) dies. The index is not a second machine — it is that same encoder, run over \(N\) documents ahead of time. After it, only narrowing — and every arrow costs money.

The map slide before this one is the itinerary — four territories, one route. This one is the machine, and it is deliberately a different kind of picture: a neural circuit diagram, where a wire is a tensor axis and a box is an operation. Do NOT teach the grammar here — that is L7's job, and it gets three slides there. Here you are only pointing at shapes. Read the ledger down the right-hand side as a column: n tokens, n by m, n by m, m, N by m, K, k, one. That is the entire course in eight rows. Step zero, the encoder row, and the most important contraction we will meet: text arrives as n tokens, the embedding table makes each one a vector, position gets added, L transformer blocks refine them without ever changing the shape — and then POOLING kills the axis n. n token-vectors collapse into ONE vector. That single move is what turns a document from a sequence into a point, and a point is the only thing you can put in an index. Every lecture from two to six is one box in that row. Step one is the move that makes search possible at all, and it is worth saying slowly: the index is not a second machine. It is the SAME encoder — not a copy, not a different model — run over all N documents ahead of time. That is the dashed violet wire. Search works for exactly one reason: the expensive part can be paid once, offline. Take that away — make the score depend on query and document together — and the dashed wire vanishes and you are running a neural network N times per query. We will spend L10 on exactly that trade. Step two is the rest of the field: everything after the index is narrowing. N to K to a handful to one answer. Every arrow costs money, and that is the whole reason a cascade exists — cheap and wide first, expensive and narrow last. Close on the promise: by the end of the course you will have built every box on this page.
