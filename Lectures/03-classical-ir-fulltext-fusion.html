Задача поискаThe retrieval problem

запрос → какой из \(N\) документов?query → which of \(N\) documents?

Поиск = по запросу вернуть небольшое релевантное подмножество из \(N\). Релевантных — горстка; корпус — миллиарды. Вся суть в том, чтобы найти эту горстку, не осматривая миллиарды.Retrieval = given a query, return the small relevant subset out of \(N\). The relevant set is a handful; the corpus is billions. The whole game is finding the handful without inspecting the billions.

State the task cleanly. A query names a tiny relevant subset hidden in an enormous corpus. The naive approach scores every document and keeps the best — correct, but unaffordable. The art of classical IR is to reach the relevant handful while looking at as few documents as possible, which is exactly what an inverted index buys.

Сканировать каждый документ безнадёжноScanning every document is hopeless

Препятствие · перебор не масштабируетсяObstacle · brute force does not scale

Линейное сканирование стоит \(O(N)\) на запрос. При \(N\approx 10^{9}\) и ~1 мкс на касание это ~1000 с — около 17 минут на запрос при бюджете ~100 мс. Промах на четыре порядка.A linear scan costs \(O(N)\) per query. At \(N\approx 10^{9}\) and ~1 µs per touch that is ~1000 s — about 17 minutes per query, against a ~100 ms budget. Off by four orders of magnitude.

The arithmetic kills brute force. A billion documents at a microsecond each — one µs is a realistic figure for a single in-cache comparison — is roughly a thousand seconds, about seventeen minutes, per query. The budget is about a hundred milliseconds. You are off by four orders of magnitude, and no faster CPU saves you, because the cost grows linearly with the corpus. The only way out is to not look at most documents at all, which is exactly what an index buys.

The budget is not arbitrary. Within that hundred-millisecond wall, first-stage retrieval gets only a slice — so it has to be sublinear in N. That requirement is the whole motivation for the inverted index.

Часть 02Part 02

The Inverted Index

Я доказал, что грубая сила безнадёжна — теперь я переворачиваю карту: вместо документ → слова я храню слово → в каких документах.I've proved brute force is hopeless — now I flip the map: instead of doc → words, store word → which docs.

My reveal. A normal document maps doc to its words. My catalog flips it: for every term, I store the list of documents that contain it — a postings list, one catalogue card per word. Now a query term is a single lookup, not a scan. This is the inverted index, the foundation of every full-text search engine for fifty years. The theoretical foundation. The Probabilistic Ranking Principle states that if you order documents by their probability of relevance to the query, you maximise the expected usefulness to the user — it is provably optimal under reasonable assumptions. The practical move is to rank by the odds of relevance rather than the probability directly, because the odds factorise into per-term contributions once you apply Bayes' rule and an independence assumption. That factorisation is what produces a sum over query terms, the shape every scorer in this part shares.

The Binary Independence Model represents a document as a set of present-or-absent terms and, under independence, the odds of relevance factorise into a sum of per-term weights. With no relevance judgments to estimate from, that weight reduces to the Robertson–Spärck-Jones form — the probabilistic relative of idf. Note the subtlety: the raw RSJ weight can go negative when a term sits in more than half the corpus (and on a tiny collection like cat/dog/mouse it would), so in practice — and in every worked example in this deck — we use the +1-clamped form from slide 32, ln(1 + (N−n+0.5)/(n+0.5)), which stays non-negative. This is BM1. Its limitation is glaring once stated: it only asks whether a term is present, never how many times — so it cannot tell a document that mentions "shuttle" once from one that is entirely about shuttles. Fixing that is the next step.

Документ либо о \(t\) (тема повторяется), либо просто упоминает его (мимоходом). Лучший вес, отличающий одно от другого, растёт с tf, но выходит на плато — принципиальный источник насыщения. A document is either about \(t\) (the topic recurs) or merely mentions it (in passing). The best weight that tells the two apart rises with tf but flattens — the principled origin of saturation. (Формально: моделируем tf как смесь 2-Poisson, по одной компоненте на режим — модель 2-Poisson — это Harter 1975; прочтение через элитность/насыщение — Robertson et al. 1981.)(Formally: model tf as a 2-Poisson mixture, one component per regime — the 2-Poisson model is Harter 1975; the eliteness/saturation reading of it is Robertson et al. 1981.)

This is the theory the old deck never gave: why saturation. Robertson's eliteness idea says each document is either elite for a term — genuinely about that concept, so the term recurs — or not, where it appears only incidentally. Model the term count as a mixture of two Poisson distributions, one per class — the 2-Poisson model itself goes back to Harter 1975 — and the optimal weight from the likelihood ratio is an increasing but bounded function of tf. In other words, the saturating shape is not an engineering hack; it falls out of a principled probabilistic model. BM25's tf factor is a cheap, robust approximation to that curve.

The parameter k1 selects a curve from a family: smaller k1 saturates sooner, larger k1 stays linear longer. This matches intuition — once a document is clearly about a topic, more repetitions tell you little — and it is the single most important difference between the two scorers.

Регулятор The \(b\): длинные против коротких документов dial: long vs short documents

\(b=0\) игнорирует длину · ignores length · \(b=1\) штрафует полностью penalizes fully

При \(b=0\) вклад терма не зависит от длины. При \(b\to1\) документ длиннее среднего штрафуется сильнее, а более короткий усиливается — всё поворачивается в точке \(\mathrm{len}=\mathrm{avgdl}\). Значение по умолчанию \(0{,}75\) — сильный, но не полный штраф.At \(b=0\) a term's contribution is independent of length. As \(b\to1\), a document longer than average is penalized more and a shorter one boosted — all pivoting at \(\mathrm{len}=\mathrm{avgdl}\). The default \(0.75\) is a strong-but-not-total penalty.

The companion picture to saturation. Hold term frequency fixed and vary document length. At b zero the line is flat — length is irrelevant. As b rises toward one, documents longer than the average lose score and shorter ones gain, and all the lines pivot at exactly the average length, where the bracket equals one regardless of b. PageRank

Мой секстант оценивает текст, но не доверие — поэтому я задаю новый вопрос: на какие звёзды указывают другие звёзды?My sextant scores text, but not trust — so I ask a new question: which stars do other stars point to?

My BM25 scores how well a document's text matches a query, but it says nothing about how trustworthy or important the document is. PageRank, Page, Brin, Motwani & Winograd 1998, supplies the missing query-independent signal from the link graph: a page is important if important pages link to it. In my star metaphor, stars vote for each other along their links, and the most-endorsed star shines brightest regardless of any particular query.

Ссылка — это голос, взвешенный по голосующемуA link is a vote — weighted by the voter

авторитетность, не зависящая от запросаquery-independent authority

Treat each inbound link as a vote for the target. But not all votes are equal: a link from an authoritative page is worth more. Authority is therefore recursive — you are important if important pages point to you.

The core intuition. The web's link structure encodes human judgment: linking to a page is an implicit endorsement. A naive count of inbound links is gameable and crude, so PageRank weights each vote by the importance of the voter — a single link from a highly authoritative site outweighs many links from obscure pages. That makes the definition recursive: your rank depends on the ranks of the pages linking to you, which depend on theirs. Resolving that recursion is what the power iteration does, two slides on.

Когда слияние не срабатываетWhen fusion fails

  • ·У всех ранжировщиков одна и та же слепая зона → слияние ничего не добавляет.All rankers share the same blind spot → fusing adds nothing.
  • ·Один ранжировщик намного сильнее → слабые вносят шум; взвесь или выбрось их. RRF использует только ранги, поэтому уверенность на отдельных запросах теряется — взвешенный RRF или слияние с учётом оценок её возвращают.One ranker is far stronger → the weak ones add noise; weight or drop them. RRF uses only ranks, so confidence on a few queries is lost — weighted RRF or score-aware fusion recovers it.
  • ·Сильно скоррелированные списки → помогает разнообразие, а не избыточность.Highly correlated lists → diversity, not redundancy, is what helps.
The reason RRF is everywhere today is hybrid search. The standard production retriever runs BM25 and a dense embedding model in parallel and fuses with RRF, getting lexical exactness and semantic recall in one list without ever calibrating the two score scales — which is why it ships as the default in Elasticsearch, OpenSearch, Weaviate, and most RAG pipelines. But fusion is not magic. If every ranker shares the same blind spot, fusing them just reproduces it. If one ranker dominates, the weak ones inject noise and should be down-weighted or dropped. Fusion pays off only when the rankers are genuinely diverse — they must disagree usefully.

BM25 сопоставляет точные термы, а не смысл. Запрос couch получает ноль на документе, где написано лишь sofa — та же концепция, другая строка, нет пересечения. Это и есть словарный разрыв: классический IR слеп к синонимам, перефразированию и вариантам написания.BM25 matches exact terms, not meaning. A query for couch scores zero on a document that only says sofa — same concept, different string, no overlap. This is the lexical gap: classical IR is blind to synonyms, paraphrase, and spelling variants.

Лексический Гремлин.The Lexical Gremlin. Он вбивает стену между couch и sofa, чтобы ни один список вхождений их не связал. Стемминг и расширение запроса латают отдельные случаи, но стена стоит. Чтобы её снести, нужны эмбеддинги — векторы, где couch и sofa стоят близко, хотя строки различаются. Это следующая лекция.It wedges a wall between couch and sofa so no postings list ever connects them. Stemming and query expansion patch a few cases, but the wall stands. To tear it down we need embeddings — vectors where couch and sofa sit close even though the strings differ. That is the next lecture.

My catch beat. I over-trusted BM25 too, because it works so well on exact terms. But it is a string matcher with clever weighting — it has no notion of meaning. Couch and sofa share no characters, so they share no postings, so my BM25 scores the match at zero. The Lexical Gremlin is the cartoon villain who keeps the two words walled apart in my catalog. Classical patches — stemming, synonym dictionaries, query expansion — help at the margins but do not generalize. The real fix is dense embeddings, where semantically close words have close vectors regardless of spelling. That is precisely why I move to embeddings next, and it is the headline weakness every neural retriever is designed to beat.
