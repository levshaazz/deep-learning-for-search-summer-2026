Quick check — the score went up

reading an evaluation result correctly
Your team optimised the answer-generation prompt against an LLM-judge and the average score rose from 4.0 to 4.25. The new answers are noticeably longer. What is the safest conclusion?
AQuality improved by 6% — ship it; the judge is reference-free so the number is objective.
BThe judge is broken — discard LLM-judging entirely and only use humans.
CThe rise may be verbosity bias (the judge prefers longer answers); re-check with length-normalised, order-swapped judging and a human-labelled sample before trusting it.
Reveal explanation
C. A rising judge score with longer answers is the exact signature of the verbosity bias we measured (longer won 10/10 trials). The number isn't objective (A) — it's a proxy you just optimised against, which is Goodhart. But the judge isn't worthless (B) either — it caught the planted hallucination on grounding. The disciplined move is to control for the bias (length-normalise, swap order) and anchor to a human-labelled sample before concluding anything.
