“If the judge score went up, the answers got better”

Goodhart the Trickster — the metric becomes a target
What students think

“We optimised our system against the LLM-judge score and it rose from 4.0 to 4.25 — so the new answers are better.”

Watch the flip. An honest judge prefers the genuinely better answer: A scores 4.3333, the padded answer C only 4.0. Now over-weight “completeness” — a stand-in for length — and C climbs to 4.25 while A sits at 4.0: the worse, verbose answer wins. Nothing about C got truer; we just rewarded length, and the system learned to pad. The moment a measure becomes a target, it stops measuring quality. Goodhart isn't a hypothetical — he is what every leaderboard climb risks becoming.

A rising score is necessary, not sufficient. Before you trust the climb, prove the judge wasn't the thing you optimised.
