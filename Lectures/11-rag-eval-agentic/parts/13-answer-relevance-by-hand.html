Answer relevance, by hand

reverse-engineer questions from the answer · mean cosine to the original
$$ \text{answer relevance} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \cos\!\big(q,\ \tilde{q}_i\big), \qquad \tilde{q}_i = \text{question the answer seems to answer} $$ $$ \cos\text{ to original } q:\quad [\,0.92,\ 0.88,\ 0.31\,] $$ $$ \text{answer relevance} = \operatorname{mean}[0.92, 0.88, 0.31] = \frac{2.11}{3} = \mathbf{0.7033} $$

where q = the original question, q̃i = the i-th question reverse-engineered from the answer, n = the number of such reverse-questions, cos = cosine similarity between their embeddings.

Generate the questions the answer could be answering, embed them, and average their cosine to the real question. The first two paraphrases stay on-topic (0.92, 0.88); the third — born of the hallucinated Harvey claim — drifts to 0.31 and drags the mean down. Relevance measures “does the answer address the question?”, not whether it is true. It reads only the question and the answer — never the retrieved context — so it is blind to grounding; and RAGAS additionally penalises noncommittal answers (an evasive “I don’t know” yields off-topic reverse-questions).
