Faithfulness, by hand

supported claims ÷ total claims · here, with 4 claims, one hallucination costs a quarter (1/N in general)
$$ \text{faithfulness} = \frac{\#\{\text{claims supported by the context}\}}{\#\{\text{claims in the answer}\}} $$ $$ \text{4 claims: pumps blood }\checkmark,\ \text{contracts }\checkmark,\ \text{systole ejects }\checkmark,\ \text{Harvey 1628 }\times $$ $$ \text{faithfulness} = \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{0.75} $$

where #{·} = the count of items in a set (supported claims over total claims), N = the number of atomic claims in the answer (here 4), so one unsupported claim costs 1/N.

Break the answer into atomic claims, then ask of each: is it entailed by a retrieved passage? Three of four are; the fluent “William Harvey, 1628” is not — nothing we retrieved says it. Here, with four claims, one unsupported sentence drops the score a full quarter (1/N in general). This is the metric that catches hallucination directly.
