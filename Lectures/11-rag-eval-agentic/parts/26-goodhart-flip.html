The Goodhart flip, by hand

re-weight one criterion and the winner inverts
$$ \text{honest mean: }\ A = \operatorname{mean}[5, 5, 3] = \mathbf{4.3333} \;>\; C = \operatorname{mean}[4, 3, 5] = \mathbf{4.0} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{winner } A $$ $$ \text{over-weight completeness } \times 2\ (\text{a proxy for length}): $$ $$ A = \frac{5 + 5 + 2\cdot 3}{4} = \mathbf{4.0} \;<\; C = \frac{4 + 3 + 2\cdot 5}{4} = \mathbf{4.25} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{winner } C $$

Honest judging picks A (4.3333 > 4.0). Now treat “completeness” as twice as important — a stand-in for rewarding length — and C, the padded answer, wins (4.25 > 4.0). Nothing about C got truer; we just changed what the score rewards. The metric became a target — Goodhart in three lines of arithmetic.
