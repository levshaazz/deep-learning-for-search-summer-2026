Verbosity bias — the longer answer wins, every time

the killer empirical finding · llama3.1:8b
$$ \text{verbosity-preference rate} = \frac{\#\{\text{picks the longer answer}\}}{\#\{\text{length-matched pairs}\}} = \frac{10}{10} = \mathbf{1.0} $$ $$ \text{across } 5 \text{ paired prompts} \times 2 \text{ orderings} = 10 \text{ trials},\ \text{the longer answer won } \textbf{every} \text{ time} $$

Held content quality fixed and only varied length: the real judge preferred the longer answer in 10 of 10 trials — a perfect 1.0. This is Goodhart made measurable: optimise against this judge and your model learns one lesson — write more. Padding is a free win. It is the single most dangerous bias because it rewards exactly the behaviour that erodes a good search answer.
