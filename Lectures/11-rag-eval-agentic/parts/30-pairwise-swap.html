The order-swap fix, by hand

judge both orderings, keep only the agreements
$$ \text{single ordering on ties: } \text{follow-slot-1 rate} = \frac{4}{6} = \mathbf{0.6667} \quad(\text{position bias}) $$ $$ \text{judge }(A,B)\text{ and }(B,A);\ \ \text{verdict} = \begin{cases} \text{the agreed winner} & \text{if both agree} \\ \text{tie / abstain} & \text{if they disagree} \end{cases} $$

Run the judge twice with the answers in both orders. When position bias would have decided it, the two runs disagree — so we call it a tie instead of crowning slot 1. The biased 0.6667 on ties collapses toward an honest coin flip, at the cost of one extra judge call. The cheapest defence is symmetry.
