Pointwise scoring, by hand

average the rubric criteria · then call the winner
$$ \text{score}(x) = \frac{1}{3}\big(\text{relevance} + \text{grounding} + \text{completeness}\big) $$ $$ A = \operatorname{mean}[5, 4, 3] = \frac{12}{3} = \mathbf{4.0} \qquad B = \operatorname{mean}[4, 2, 2] = \frac{8}{3} = \mathbf{2.6667} $$ $$ A\ (4.0) \;>\; B\ (2.6667) \;\Rightarrow\; \text{pairwise winner} = \mathbf{A} $$

Each answer is judged alone, criterion by criterion, then averaged. A is strong across the board (5/4/3); B trails on grounding and completeness (4/2/2). The pointwise gap (4.0 vs 2.6667) and the side-by-side pairwise verdict agree: A wins. Clean and decisive — which is exactly why we are about to distrust it.
