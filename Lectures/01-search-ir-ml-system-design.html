Чем мы на самом деле занимаемсяWhat we are actually doing

ОпределениеDefinition
ОпределениеDefinition
Информационный поискInformation Retrieval IR · Manning, Raghavan & Schütze, 2008

Поиск материала — обычно неструктурированного (текста), — который удовлетворяет информационную потребность, внутри больших коллекций.Finding material — usually unstructured (text) — that satisfies an information need from within large collections.

Система хранит документы; у пользователя есть потребность; единственный мост между ними — короткий запрос с потерями. IR — это дисциплина о том, как хорошо построить этот мост.The system stores documents; the user holds a need; the only bridge between them is a short, lossy query. IR is the discipline of building that bridge well.

Read the definition slowly. Three nouns matter: documents (what we have), need (what the user actually wants — which we never directly observe), and query (the few words they typed). The whole subject lives in the gap between need and query. "Unstructured" is doing work too: contrast with a database, where the data is typed and the query is exact — that contrast is two slides away.

потребность → запрос — это сжатие с потерямиneed → query is lossy compression

Средний веб-запрос ≈ 2.3–2.9 словаAverage web query ≈ 2.3–2.9 words

Система никогда не видит потребность — только два слова. Ранжирование — это попытка восстановить потребность из сигнала с потерями.The system never sees the need — only the two words. Ranking is the attempt to reconstruct the need from a lossy signal.

This is the single most important framing of the IR half. The user compresses a rich, context-laden need into ~2.5 words and throws away deadline, intent, reading level, language. Search must reconstruct all of that. Ask the room: "if you only had the two words, which of those five documents would you put first?" — there is no objectively correct answer, which is exactly why relevance is hard.

Скользкое слово: релевантностьThe slippery word: relevance

ОпределениеDefinition
ОпределениеDefinition
РелевантностьRelevance does this document satisfy the need?

Бинарная или градуированная — релевантно / нет, либо 5-балльная шкала (Perfect · Excellent · Good · Fair · Bad), которая питает \(\text{nDCG}\).Binary vs graded — relevant / not, or a 5-point scale (Perfect · Excellent · Good · Fair · Bad) that feeds \(\text{nDCG}\).

Тематическая или ситуативная — документ может быть идеально по теме, но бесполезен: не тот уровень, устарел, за пейволом, не тот язык.Topical vs situational — a document can be perfectly on-topic yet useless: wrong level, outdated, paywalled, wrong language.

Релевантность динамична (свежесть), персональна и ситуативна. Именно поэтому офлайн-метка редко равна настоящему счастью пользователя.Relevance is dynamic (freshness), personal and situational. This is precisely why an offline label rarely equals real user happiness.

Relevance is not a property of the document alone — it is a relation between document, need, and context. The topical/situational split is the seed of the whole offline–online gap we hit in Part 03: a human judge marks topical relevance against a label, but the user lives in a situation the judge never saw. Graded relevance is what makes \(\text{nDCG}\) possible — keep that thread.

Почему мы ранжируем, а не фильтруемWhy we rank instead of filter

Градуированная релевантность · 5-балльная шкалаGraded relevance · the 5-point scale

Фильтрация говорит да/нет; ранжирование говорит насколько хорошо и в каком порядке — а это совпадает с тем, как пользователи читают сверху вниз.Filtering says yes/no; ranking says how good, and in what order — which matches how users read top-down.

The pedagogical payoff of graded relevance: if there are degrees of "good", a yes/no filter is lossy. Users read top-down and rarely past the first few results, so the order is the product. This is the bridge to ranking metrics — \(\text{nDCG}@10\) rewards putting grade-4 above grade-2 — and it sets up the quiz at the end of Part 01. Making the arrow concrete: \(\text{DCG}@k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{2^{g_i}-1}{\log_2(i+1)}\), where \(g_i\) is the grade at rank \(i\); the gain \(2^{g_i}-1\) is divided by a position discount \(\log_2(i+1)\). Normalise by the ideal ordering: \(\text{nDCG}@k = \text{DCG}@k / \text{IDCG}@k \in [0,1]\). Worked example: a grade-4 doc gives gain \(2^4-1=15\); at rank 1 it contributes \(15/\log_2 2 = 15\), but at rank 2 only \(15/\log_2 3 \approx 9.46\) — that drop is exactly the "discount by position" the deck claims.

Структура данных, которая делает поиск возможнымThe data structure that makes search possible

ОпределениеDefinition
ОпределениеDefinition
Инвертированный индексInverted index term → list of documents

Прямой индекс отображает документ → термины; ответить на запрос — значит просканировать каждый документ, \(O(N)\) по размеру корпуса.A forward index maps doc → terms; answering a query means scanning every document — \(O(N)\) in corpus size.

Инвертированный индекс переворачивает это: словарь (термин + документная частота \(\mathrm{df}\)), указывающий на список постингов (отсортированные ID документов, опционально частота термина \(\mathrm{tf}\) и позиции).An inverted index flips it: a dictionary (term + document frequency \(\mathrm{df}\)) pointing at a postings list (sorted doc IDs, optionally term frequency \(\mathrm{tf}\) and positions).

Теперь стоимость зависит от числа совпавших документов, а не от размера корпуса — вот почему поиск веб-масштаба вообще возможен.Cost now depends on the number of matching documents, not the corpus size — this is why web-scale search is even possible.

This is the one classical data structure every search person must know cold. The key insight is the cost flip: instead of \(O(N)\) over the whole corpus, you pay roughly for the length of the postings lists you intersect. Positions in the postings enable phrase queries. We will build BM25 on top of this in Lecture 03 — \(\mathrm{tf}\) and \(\mathrm{df}\) are already living in the index.

If students remember one "why search is hard" slide, make it this one. The Furnas 80%-disagreement number is the empirical anchor. The escape hatch — embeddings that place "couch" and "sofa" near each other — is the entire motivation for the dense-retrieval weeks. Логи кликов лгутClick logs lie

Позиционное смещение и смещение подачиPosition & presentation bias
Что предполагают командыWhat teams assume

«Результат №1 собирает больше всего кликов, значит он самый релевантный — давайте обучим ранжировщик на кликах»."Result #1 gets the most clicks, so it must be the most relevant — let's train the ranker on clicks."

Айтрекинг («золотой треугольник», популяризированный Enquiro 2005; рецензируемая опора — Granka, Joachims & Gay 2004) показывает, что внимание концентрируется вверху слева. Органический результат №1 получает ~32% кликов во многом потому, что он №1, а не потому, что он лучший. Значит, сырые клики — это смещённые обучающие данные: наивная модель обучения ранжированию учится «предсказывать то, что уже было наверху» и закрепляет действующих лидеров на месте. Решение: моделировать смещение явно — несмещённое LTR со взвешиванием по обратной склонности (вернёмся к этому в Части 03).Eye-tracking (the "Golden Triangle", popularized by Enquiro 2005; peer-reviewed backing Granka, Joachims & Gay 2004) shows attention concentrates top-left. The #1 organic result earns ~32% of clicks largely because it is #1, not because it is best. So raw clicks are biased training data: a naive learning-to-rank model learns "predict what was already on top" and freezes the incumbents in place. Fix: model the bias explicitly — unbiased LTR with inverse-propensity weighting (we return to this in Part 03).

Worked simulation · grounded in _research/data/position_bias.py.

  • ·Model — examination probability \( p_r \propto 1/r^{\gamma} \), a rank-only power-law from the family compared by Craswell et al. 2008 (their own cascade model is sequential, not this closed form). \( \gamma = 0.94 \) is fit so rank-1 ≈ 32%, a CTR level repeatedly reported in click-log studies.
  • ·Framing — that clicks reveal relative not absolute relevance is Joachims, Granka et al. 2005; Enquiro 2005's “golden triangle” is the eye-tracking behind attention concentrates top-left, not the click percentage.
  • ·Under equal relevance (top-10): rank 1 = 32.3%, rank 2 = 16.8%, rank 3 = 11.5%, … top-3 together 60.6%.
  • ·So a rank-1 click carries nearly 3× (2.8×) the weight of a rank-3 click even when the docs are equally good — the bias to undo.
  • ·Train LTR on raw clicks and you bake the ranking into the model; IPW reweights each click by \( 1/p_r \). Unbiased only if the propensities are right, and \( 1/p_r \) explodes for deep ranks — so systems clip propensities or jointly learn examination + relevance.
This is the bridge between the IR half and the systems half. Position bias means the feedback you collect is contaminated by the very ranking that produced it — a feedback loop. Training naively on clicks bakes in a rich-get-richer dynamic. The fix (IPW / unbiased LTR) shows up again on the feedback-loops slide in Part 03; flag it now so it lands later. Закон GoodhartGoodhart's law

Когда мера становится целью…When a measure becomes a target…

Оптимизируйте CTR достаточно сильно — и вы оптимизируете кликбейт, возмущение и случайные касания: прокси растёт, а то, что вы на самом деле хотели, падает. As Strathern (1997) crisply restated Goodhart's 1975 observation: "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Picture Goodhart the Trickster yanking the CTR line up with a clickbait hook while real satisfaction quietly sinks. A pure CTR objective rewards clickbait, outrage, ambiguity, and accidental taps — none of which is satisfaction. The mitigation is exactly the guardrail discipline from two slides back: pair CTR with dwell time and pogo-sticking/quick-back rate so the degenerate direction is caught. Prefer causal goal metrics over convenient proxies where you can.
