Mechanism slide for the vocabulary act, which named the PRF ceiling but never showed it. The relevance model is built ONLY from the feedback documents, so its support is contained in the union of their term sets; any term outside that union has probability zero in both mixture components, hence zero in RM3 — for every λ. This is the formal statement of "RM3 can only re-copy what it already read": channeling is in D_k so it can be up-weighted, under-extraction is not so it can never appear. λ trades mass between the query model and the relevance model but never widens the support — turning it up only accelerates drift toward the trap's terms. The act asserts "rank-fusion is robust, score-fusion needs calibration" in a comparison table but never shows why. Two lines do it. (1) Bounded influence: every list's contribution to the RRF sum lies in (0, 1/(k+1)], so score-scale inflation cannot amplify one list beyond that bound. This does not guarantee that a bad list cannot change the winner: at k=60, ranks A=[1,1000,1000] beat B=[1000,100,100]. Raw cosine scores are also bounded, although their distributions vary. The structural guarantees are bounded contributions and decreasing sensitivity; retrieval robustness remains an empirical question.
