\(O(V)\to O(k)\) — на пять порядков дешевле. Тяни истинную пару вверх (\(\sigma\to 1\)), толкай \(k\!=\!5\text{–}20\) случайных негативов вниз (\(\sigma\to 0\)); шум \(P_n\propto \mathrm{freq}^{3/4}\). Альтернатива — иерархический софтмакс (дерево Хаффмана, \(O(\log V)\)).\(O(V)\to O(k)\) — about five orders of magnitude cheaper. Push the true pair up (\(\sigma\to 1\)), push \(k\!=\!5\text{–}20\) random negatives down (\(\sigma\to 0\)); noise \(P_n\propto \mathrm{freq}^{3/4}\). The alternative is hierarchical softmax (a Huffman tree, \(O(\log V)\)).

The word2vec punchline, derived as one moving story instead of three static cards. Step zero: the skip-gram objective is a softmax — the probability of context c given centre w is the exponentiated dot-product score over the exponentiated scores of every word in the vocabulary. Step one: the numerator is one cheap dot product, but watch the denominator light up red — it sums over all V words. Step two: that normaliser costs on the order of V, around a million, dot products on every single update, so we literally strike it out. Step three: we replace the whole multi-class softmax with a binary logistic test — make the true pair's sigmoid go toward one, and draw k random negatives, typically five to twenty, sampled in proportion to frequency to the three-quarters power, and push theirs toward zero. Cost drops from order V to order k, five orders of magnitude. Use the arrows to step through the algebra.
Step 0 — the exact objective: a softmax over the whole vocabulary V.
Step 1 — the denominator (highlighted) is the costly part: a sum over every word.
Step 2 — cost ∝ V ≈ a million per update; strike the normaliser out.
Step 3 — negative sampling: the true pair vs k sampled negatives, O(k) per step.
