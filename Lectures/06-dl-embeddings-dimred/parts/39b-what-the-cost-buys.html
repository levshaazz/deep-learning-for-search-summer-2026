Три ловушки — не огрехи реализации: цель покупает соседство и только его, а за зазоры и размеры кластеров не платит вовсе. Поэтому у UMAP последняя строка та же — меняется цена, а не то, что куплено.The three traps are not implementation flaws: the objective buys neighbourhoods and nothing else, and never pays for gaps or cluster sizes. Which is why UMAP's last row reads the same — the price changes, not what you bought.

The mechanism under the act's headline claim — distances, gaps and cluster sizes are not readable — which the deck has so far only asserted and illustrated. Two ingredients, and each buys exactly one trap. First, the cost is a Kullback-Leibler divergence from the high-dimensional neighbour distribution to the low-dimensional one, and it is asymmetric on purpose. Every term is weighted by p: when two points really are neighbours, p is large, so drawing them apart makes the log ratio blow up and the layout pays heavily. But when p is near zero, the whole term is near zero no matter what q is — a far-apart pair can be placed at any distance in the plane for free. So the optimiser is bribed to get neighbourhoods right and is simply not charged for the between-cluster geometry. That is traps one and two. Second, the bandwidth: sigma is not global, it is solved per point so that every point's conditional distribution has the same perplexity. A dense region gets a small sigma and a sparse one a large sigma, which is precisely a per-point rescaling of the picture — dense clumps are spread out, sparse ones squeezed. That is trap three, cluster size. Land the last line: this is not a bug anyone could patch, it is the bargain the objective makes, which is why UMAP — a different objective with a different price — still forbids the same reading. No numbers: everything on this slide is structural.
