Совпали контексты — совпали координаты; вот чем оплачена карта. Та же строка задаёт предел: у многозначного слова распределение одно на два смысла, и вектор садится между ними. Развести их сможет только контекст — это L7.Matching contexts force matching coordinates — that is what the map cost. The same line sets the limit: a polysemous word has one distribution for two senses, so its vector lands between them. Only context separates them — L7.

The payoff act says the words now have coordinates and that nearness means related meaning. This slide is why that is true rather than lucky, and it uses only the objective from the word2vec act. Read the top line as the skip-gram softmax: the probability that context c appears beside word w is governed by a single dot product between the word vector and the context vector. Now suppose two words really do have the same context distribution — the distributional hypothesis's premise, stated as an equation. Equal softmax outputs for every context force the dot products to differ by at most a constant that the normaliser absorbs, so the difference of the two word vectors is orthogonal to every difference of context vectors. Once those context differences span the space, the only vector orthogonal to all of them is zero: the two words must occupy the same point. Real corpora only make the distributions approximately equal, so the residual is small rather than zero, and small residual is exactly high cosine. That is the whole of Sir Cosine's authority on this map — nearness is not decoration, it is what the training objective enforces. Then the corollary that opens the bridge: a polysemous word has only one distribution to fit, and that distribution is the mixture of its senses, so the single vector settles between the two senses and represents neither. No amount of training fixes that, because the model was never given the sentence — which is exactly what Lecture 7 changes. Symbolic only; every number in this deck is grounded in data/.
