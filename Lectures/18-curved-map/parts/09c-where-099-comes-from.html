Проверка на нашей игрушке (\(c=3\), \(\sigma=1\), \(d=10\)): формула даёт \(9/(9+1) = 0{,}90\), измерено \(0{,}8985\). Обратный ход превращает числа Ethayarajh в механизм: \(\cos = 0{,}6 \Rightarrow c/\sigma = \sqrt{0{,}6/0{,}4} = 1{,}2247\) — ось лишь чуть громче шума; \(\cos = 0{,}99 \Rightarrow c/\sigma = \sqrt{99} = 9{,}9499\) — ось в десять раз громче смысла. И \(d\) сократилась: анизотропия — не проклятие размерности. Оговорка: левое равенство верно для любого общего \(\mu\); \(\mathbf{1}_d\) — модельная подстановка ради простой правой части, а не описание настоящей оси (важно на слайде про LayerNorm).Check it on our toy (\(c=3\), \(\sigma=1\), \(d=10\)): the formula says \(9/(9+1) = 0.90\), the measurement says \(0.8985\). Run it backwards and Ethayarajh's numbers become a mechanism: \(\cos = 0.6 \Rightarrow c/\sigma = \sqrt{0.6/0.4} = 1.2247\) — the axis is barely louder than the noise; \(\cos = 0.99 \Rightarrow c/\sigma = \sqrt{99} = 9.9499\) — the axis is ten times louder than the meaning. And \(d\) cancelled: anisotropy is not a curse of dimensionality. One caveat: the left-hand equality holds for any shared \(\mu\); \(\mathbf{1}_d\) is a modelling substitution that buys the tidy right-hand side, not a description of the real axis (this matters on the LayerNorm slide).

Worked derivation (plan §4 W2). Model a contextual vector as a shared axis plus individual noise: x = c·1_d + ε. For two independent draws E[x·y] = ‖μ‖² = c²d and E[‖x‖²] = c²d + dσ², so E[cos] ≈ c²/(c²+σ²) — the dimension cancels. Verified numerically against our own toy: c=3, σ=1, d=10 predicts 0.90 against a measured rawCos of 0.8985. Inverted, the formula converts the two reported cosines into a signal-to-noise reading: 0.6 ⇒ c/σ = 1.2247, 0.99 ⇒ c/σ = 9.9499. This is also the formula printed by the cone-dial widget on the previous slide, and it is the reason act 01 (SNR) and act 03 (dimension) are two different animals — the point the next slide turns into a misconception. Caveat added after the late review §2: E[cos] ≈ ‖μ‖²/(‖μ‖²+dσ²) holds for ANY shared mean μ; collapsing it to c²/(c²+σ²) is what requires μ = c·1_d. Say the substitution out loud, because the LayerNorm slide depends on it: LayerNorm annihilates exactly the 1_d component (⟨LN(x), 1_d⟩ = 0 identically), so an axis that literally WERE c·1_d would not survive it.
