The boundary the lecture was missing (plan F0-d, R3). The six skew values are in Radovanović 2010's footnote 5 (p. 2492) referring to Fig. 1, they are S_{N₅} at n = 10 000, and they are EUCLIDEAN — the paper explicitly marks cosine as the exception. Our own toy (gen_l18.py) is Euclidean as well, computing norm(sub(...)), which is stated on the toy slides. Munyampirwa et al. 2024 confirm it by measurement on real ANN datasets: "cosine distance does not have a dramatic skew, even for d ∈ {1024, 1536}". Without this slide the lecture generalises a dot-product result to the normalised-vector world it actually teaches. The counterweight keeps it honest: QB-Norm (CVPR 2022 Table 3) measures a k-occurrence skew of 0.939 on real cosine-space embeddings and reduces it to 0.509, so hubness on normalised vectors is weaker but real — hence "measure first" rather than "ignore it".
