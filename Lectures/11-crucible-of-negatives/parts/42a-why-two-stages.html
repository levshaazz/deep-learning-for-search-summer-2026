Оба этапа кормят одну сумму разной выборкой: массовый батч держит её честной, чистая горстка трудных — острой. И вот почему горстка: смести выборку сильно — и модель разложит красиво лишь окрестность кромки. Самозванец из акта 4 — то же смещение, с неверным знаком.Both stages feed one sum with different samples: the massive batch keeps it honest, the clean handful keeps it sharp. That is why the handful stays small — bias it too far and the model tidies only the edge's neighbourhood. The impostor of act 4 is the same bias, wrong sign.

The mechanism under the act's headline claim — "uniformity from in-batch, the boundary from a few clean hard negatives" — which slides 41-43 assert but never derive. Write InfoNCE as two terms: an alignment term A that pulls the positive in, and a log-sum-exp term U over every candidate in the batch. Only U sees the negatives, and U is a sampled estimate of how crowded the corpus is around the query. That single observation explains the whole two-stage recipe. Tens of thousands of cheap in-batch negatives make that estimate broad and nearly unbiased, so the repulsion is spread across the sphere — that is what "uniformity" actually is, not a slogan. A handful of mined hard negatives does the opposite: as slide eighteen showed by hand, the softmax weights collapse onto the nearest few, so those few dominate the direction of the push and set the decision boundary. So the two stages are not two ideas glued together — they are two samplers feeding the same sum. It also answers the question the recipe leaves open, namely why the hard set stays small: push the sample too far toward the edge and the estimate stops being about the corpus at all, and the model only tidies the neighbourhood of the boundary. The impostor from act four is the same bias with the sign of the error flipped. Symbolic on purpose — batch sizes and hard-negative counts are recipe-specific.
