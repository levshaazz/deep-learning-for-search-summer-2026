The gradient weights, by hand

two negatives carry ~⅞ of the push
$$ w_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}, \qquad z_i = \cos(q,\,d_i^-)/\tau, \quad \tau = 0.1 $$ $$ \cos: \;\; n_1\,0.05 \;\;\; n_2\,0.41 \;\;\; n_3\,0.63 \;\;\; n_4\,0.75 \;\;\; n_5\,0.79 $$ $$ w: \;\; n_1\,0.00 \;\;\; n_2\,0.01 \;\;\; n_3\,0.11 \;\;\; \mathbf{n_4\,0.35} \;\;\; \mathbf{n_5\,0.53} $$

With \(z=\cos\times 10\), almost all the weight lands on the two hardest: \(w_5\approx0.53,\ w_4\approx0.35\) — together \(\approx0.88\) of the push, while \(n_1\) gets essentially nothing. As \(\tau\to0\) the weight collapses onto the single hardest (nearest-neighbour hard-max). A negative is worth exactly its gradient.
