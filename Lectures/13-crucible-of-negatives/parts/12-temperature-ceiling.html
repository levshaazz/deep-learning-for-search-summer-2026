Temperature sharpens; N raises the ceiling

more negatives raise the ceiling
Temperature \(\tau\) · the hardness amplifier
Lowering \(\tau\) piles probability onto the hardest candidate, and the loss falls.
  • ·\(\tau\) decides how much a near-miss dominates the gradient.
The \(\log N\) ceiling · honest version
$$ I \ge \log N - L_N $$
  • ·Here \(N\) is the number of candidates the softmax chooses among — 1 positive + \((N-1)\) negatives — so \(\log N\) is the ceiling.
  • ·More negatives do not ‘maximise’ mutual information — they raise the ceiling, and the bound only saturates at \(\log N\).
  • ·Because it is a lower bound, InfoNCE can certify at most \(\log N\) nats of MI no matter how good the encoder is (Poole 2019) — so for high-MI tasks the bound is loose and large batches are a hard requirement, not a nicety.
  • ·That ceiling is why retrievers scale N.
Two knobs: τ amplifies hardness (lower τ → probability piles on the hardest candidate → loss falls); N raises the log N ceiling in I ≥ log N − L_N, which the bound only saturates at. The honest reading: scaling N raises a ceiling, it does not magically maximise MI. Next: the boundaries slide.
