Forged by worthy opponents

A dense retriever is forged by the opponents it trains against. Easy negatives teach nothing; the hardest are often impostors that corrupt the edge; effectiveness lives in a narrow hard-but-honest band — and the field’s whole history is the search for it. The cures all accept one truth: at equal hardness, only something that knows relevance — a filter, a teacher, a label — tells a worthy negative from an impostor.

Séréga raises a keen, true-edged blade. But the geometry it lives in has its own troubles — embeddings crowd into a narrow cone and some points become unwanted hubs. That is the next deep-dive, The Curved Map.
