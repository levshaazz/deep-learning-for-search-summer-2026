Two axes, not one

hardness and danger are orthogonal
The 2×2 of hardness
$$ \text{hardness}=\cos(q,d^-)\ \perp\ \text{collateral danger}=\cos(d^-,d^+) $$

where q = the query vector, d− = a negative passage, d+ = the positive passage, cos = cosine similarity, and ⊥ = the two axes are independent (orthogonal).

  • ×The impostor n₅: \(\cos(q)=0.79\) and \(\cos(d^+)=0.80\) — right beside the positive.
  • ✓A hard true negative n₄: \(\cos(q)=0.75\) but \(\cos(d^+)=0.62\) — clearly below the positive.
  • ·Same hardness on the query axis — danger lives ONLY on the second axis (separation ≈0.18), invisible to a query-only view.
Two-axis. hardness=cos(q,d⁻) ⊥ collateral danger=cos(d⁻,d⁺). Impostor n₅: cos(q)=0.79, cos(d⁺)=0.80; hard true negative n₄: cos(q)=0.75, cos(d⁺)=0.62. Same query-axis hardness; the danger is purely on the second axis (separation 0.80−0.62 ≈0.18), invisible to a query-only view.
