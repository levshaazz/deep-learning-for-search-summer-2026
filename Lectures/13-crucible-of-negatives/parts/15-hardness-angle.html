Hardness is an angle

a small angle to the query = a hard negative
Definition · geometric hardness
$$ \text{hard}(d^-) \;\propto\; \cos(q,\,d^-) $$
  • ·A hard negative sits close to the query in angle — high \(\cos\) — and is easy to confuse with the answer.
  • ·An easy negative is far, already separated — it gives almost no gradient.
  • ·Lay \(q\), \(d^+\) and the negatives on a unit arc by angle — hardness becomes something you can see. Sir Cosine returns.
Hardness is an angle: hard(d⁻) ∝ cos(q,d⁻). High cos = crowds the query = easy to confuse = hard. Easy = far = no gradient. Lay q, d⁺, negatives on a unit arc by angle and you can SEE hardness. Sir Cosine returns (L13-04).
