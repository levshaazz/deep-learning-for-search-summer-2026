What you will be able to do

four objectives — and one glossary, because “hard” is overloaded
  1. Explain why contrastive retrieval needs negatives (the space collapses without them) and read an InfoNCE loss off a batch by hand.
  2. Place a negative on the 2×2 grid — geometric hardness \(\cos(q,d^-)\) × label correctness (true / false = unlabelled positive). The axes are orthogonal.
  3. Trace the arc — random → in-batch → BM25-static → ANCE-dynamic → denoised → distilled → modern two-stage — and name the specific failure each step fixes.
  4. Diagnose a weak dense retriever (“is it a negatives problem?”) and choose a strategy under real compute and label constraints.

Glossary — four senses of “hard”: geometric-hard (high \(\cos(q,\cdot)\)) · BM25-hard (lexical) · dynamic-hard (the model’s own current near-miss) · false (an unlabelled positive — a label property, not a distance).

Objectives + the hardness glossary. The 2×2 (objective 2) is the spine of the whole lecture: hardness ⟂ label-correctness. Reuses L5 (word2vec NEG), L6 (InfoNCE), L7 (bi-encoders, in-batch negatives), L9 (the ANN index ANCE mines).
