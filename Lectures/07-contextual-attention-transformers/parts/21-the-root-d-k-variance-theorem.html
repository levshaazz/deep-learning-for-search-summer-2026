Предложение · масштаб \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\) Proposition · the \(1/\sqrt{d_k}\) scale
Пусть координаты \(q,k\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k}\) независимы, со средним \(0\) и дисперсией \(1\). Тогда для \(s=q^{\top}k\): \(\mathbb{E}[s]=0\) и \(\operatorname{Var}(s)=d_k\). Деление на \(\sqrt{d_k}\) даёт \(\operatorname{Var}(s/\sqrt{d_k})=1\) — независимо от \(d_k\). Let the coordinates of \(q,k\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k}\) be independent with mean \(0\) and variance \(1\). Then for \(s=q^{\top}k\): \(\mathbb{E}[s]=0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(s)=d_k\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) gives \(\operatorname{Var}(s/\sqrt{d_k})=1\) — independent of \(d_k\).
Доказательство Proof
Каждое слагаемое \(q_j k_j\) центрировано с \(\operatorname{Var}(q_j k_j)=\mathbb{E}[q_j^2]\mathbb{E}[k_j^2]=1\); слагаемые независимы, значит дисперсии складываются: \(\operatorname{Var}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_k}1=d_k\). Стандартное отклонение скоров — \(\sqrt{d_k}\): при \(d_k=64\) они качаются в \(\pm 8\), softmax насыщается, градиент \(\to 0\). Делим на \(\sqrt{d_k}\) — и \(\operatorname{Var}=1\), softmax «мягкий». У нас \(d_k=4\Rightarrow\sqrt{d_k}=2{,}0\). \(\blacksquare\) Each term \(q_j k_j\) is mean-zero with \(\operatorname{Var}(q_j k_j)=\mathbb{E}[q_j^2]\mathbb{E}[k_j^2]=1\); the terms are independent, so the variances add: \(\operatorname{Var}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_k}1=d_k\). The score standard deviation is \(\sqrt{d_k}\): at \(d_k=64\) they swing across \(\pm 8\), softmax saturates and the gradient \(\to 0\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) makes \(\operatorname{Var}=1\), keeping softmax soft. Here \(d_k=4\Rightarrow\sqrt{d_k}=2.0\). \(\blacksquare\)
The scaling stated as a variance proposition with a proof, not a myth. Take a query and key whose coordinates are independent, mean zero, variance one. Their dot product is a sum of d k terms, each term a product of two independent unit-variance numbers, so each term has mean zero and variance one. Because the terms are independent the variances add, giving total variance exactly d k — so the scores have standard deviation root d k. For a realistic key size of sixty-four that is a swing of plus or minus eight; feed scores that large into softmax and it saturates, one weight to one and the rest to zero, and the gradient of softmax collapses to nearly zero so no signal flows back. Dividing every score by root d k pulls the variance back to one and keeps softmax in its soft, differentiable regime. In our toy example d k is four so the divisor is exactly two.
