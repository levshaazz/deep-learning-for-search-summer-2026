Ни одну из побед не прописывали правилом. Полисемия: тот же \(x\) получает разные добавки, потому что соседи разные. Синонимия: контрастное обучение ни разу не сравнивает couch с sofa напрямую — оно тянет каждый к общему якорю, а неравенство треугольника доделывает остальное.Neither win was written down as a rule. Polysemy: the same \(x\) receives different additions because the neighbours differ. Synonymy: contrastive training never compares couch with sofa directly — it pulls each toward a shared anchor, and the triangle inequality does the rest.

The payoff act named two wins but never showed why either one had to happen; both fall out of one line each. Polysemy first: the contextual vector is the token's own starting embedding plus the attention-weighted mixture of its neighbours. The starting vector for "bank" is the same in both sentences — what differs is the second term, because the neighbour sets are disjoint. Two identical inputs, two different additions, two separated outputs. No sense inventory, no disambiguation rule. Synonymy second, and this is the part that usually feels like magic. Contrastive training never puts couch and sofa side by side; they are simply positives of the same anchor — the same query or the same document. On the unit sphere the angle is a metric, so it obeys the triangle inequality: the angle between couch and sofa is at most the sum of their angles to that shared anchor. Pull both toward the anchor and you have provably pulled them toward each other, transitively, through material neither of them appears in. That is the cage: geometry, not a synonym list — and it is why the win generalises to pairs the training data never mentioned together.
