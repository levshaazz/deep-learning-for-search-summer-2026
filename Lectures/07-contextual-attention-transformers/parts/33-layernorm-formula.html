Среднее \(\mu\) и дисперсия \(\sigma^2\) считаются по \(d\) признакам одного токена (не по батчу — в этом отличие от BatchNorm). Затем масштаб \(\gamma\) и сдвиг \(\beta\) возвращают сети свободу. Не зависит от размера батча — идеально для переменной длины.The mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\) are taken over a single token’s \(d\) features (not the batch — the difference from BatchNorm). A learned scale \(\gamma\) and shift \(\beta\) restore freedom. Independent of batch size — ideal for variable-length sequences.

LayerNorm precisely, because the over-what matters. For each token independently, compute the mean and variance across that token's d feature values, subtract the mean, divide by the standard deviation plus a tiny epsilon for numerical safety, then apply a learned per-feature scale gamma and shift beta. The crucial contrast with batch normalisation is the axis: BatchNorm normalises each feature across the examples in a batch, whereas LayerNorm normalises each example across its features. That makes LayerNorm independent of batch size and of the other sequences in the batch — exactly what you want for variable-length text where batch statistics would be unstable. This is the Norm in every Add and Norm.
