The second transfer, and it reuses today's loss verbatim. CLIP, from Radford and colleagues in 2021, trains two encoders at once — an image encoder, often a Vision Transformer, and a text encoder, a Transformer — and forces them to project into one shared space. How does it align them? With exactly the InfoNCE loss we derived two sections ago. Take a batch of B image-caption pairs, compute the B by B matrix of cosine similarities between every image and every caption, and apply InfoNCE so that each image's true caption is the positive and the other B minus one captions are in-batch negatives — and symmetrically for each caption. That is the entire training signal. The payoff is striking: zero-shot image classification by comparing an image to text prompts, and text-to-image retrieval, both falling straight out of cosine nearness in the shared space. The temperature tau, the cosine, the in-batch-negatives trick — every piece is what we built today, just with one tower fed pixels instead of words.
