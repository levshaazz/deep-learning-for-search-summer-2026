Проверь на трёх следующих слайдах: ViT режет картинку на патчи, CLIP ставит две такие нарезки в одно пространство, а на графе роль \(p_i\) берут рёбра — и внимание читается как передача сообщений.Porting the architecture means swapping the cut and \(W_e\) and leaving the block alone. Check that against the next three slides: ViT cuts an image into patches, CLIP puts two such cuts into one space, and on a graph the edges take over the job of \(p_i\) — which is why attention reads as message-passing.

The mechanism the transfer act was asserting rather than showing. Everything the encoder block does happens after a very narrow interface: a matrix X of n rows by d columns. Getting there takes exactly two modality-specific moves — cut the raw input into pieces, and project each piece linearly into d dimensions, then add a positional encoding. Words, image patches, audio windows, amino acids, graph nodes: the cut differs, the projection matrix differs, and nothing after that point differs at all. Say the operational consequence: to port a Transformer to a new modality you write a tokenizer and one linear layer, and you do not touch attention, the residual, LayerNorm or the feed-forward network. This is why the next three slides are variations and not new architectures — ViT swaps the cut for a patch grid, CLIP runs two different cuts into one shared space so that cosine can compare across them, and the graph view is the case where explicit edges replace the positional encoding, which is exactly what makes attention look like message-passing. The universality claim on the divider is this narrow interface, nothing more mystical.
