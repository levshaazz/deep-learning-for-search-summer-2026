«BERT — мощный, значит его векторы сразу готовы для косинусного поиска».“BERT is powerful, so its vectors are ready for cosine search out of the box.”

Нет. Сырые векторы анизотропны: они теснятся в узком конусе, поэтому средняя косинусная близость случайных предложений высока (\(\sim 0{,}6\) и выше). Косинус почти не различает — и это не та концентрация расстояний из L2: она про размерность, а конус — про общее среднее (разбор в L14). Лекарство: контрастное обучение раздвигает векторы; геометрию выпрямляют центрирование и отбеливание. Нормализация задаёт линейку, но конуса не трогает: косинус от длины вектора не зависит.No. Raw vectors are anisotropic: they occupy a narrow cone, so the mean cosine of random sentences is high (\(\sim 0.6\) and up). Cosine barely discriminates — and this is not the distance concentration of L2: that one is about dimensionality, the cone is about a shared mean (worked out in L14). The cure: contrastive learning spreads the vectors; the geometry is straightened by centring and whitening. Normalisation fixes the ruler, not the cone — cosine does not depend on vector length.

The trap, made precise, with the L2 callback. Anisotropy means the embedding cloud is not spread evenly — it occupies a thin cone, so any two vectors, even for unrelated sentences, share a small angle and therefore a deceptively high cosine, around zero point six or more on random pairs. Ethayarajh in 2019 measured exactly this in BERT, ELMo and GPT-2. Cosine cannot rank well when everything looks similar, and this is the same curse-of-dimensionality pathology the Wraith embodied back in Lecture 2 — distances concentrate, contrast vanishes. The cure is twofold and is precisely what we just built: contrastive training actively pushes negatives apart, spreading the cone, and normalisation onto the sphere restores a usable geometry.
