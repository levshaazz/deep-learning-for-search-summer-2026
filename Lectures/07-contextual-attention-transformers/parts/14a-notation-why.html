Формула не показывает ценуA formula does not show you the cost

формула говорит, ЧТО считать — но не СКОЛЬКО это стоитa formula says WHAT to compute — never what it COSTS
Что думают студентыWhat students think

«В формуле написано всё, что нужно знать об операции».“The formula says everything there is to know about the operation.”

Нет. Формула говорит, что вычислить, но не говорит, сколько это стоит — и не может показать ошибку. Ось \(n\times n\) в записи \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\) не написана нигде: она рождается внутри \(QK^\top\), живёт через softmax и умирает при умножении на \(V\). Между рождением и смертью — весь счёт за память. По какой оси нормирует softmax, из формулы тоже не видно.No. A formula tells you what to compute; it does not tell you what it costs — and it cannot show you a bug. The \(n\times n\) axis is written nowhere in \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\): it is born inside \(QK^\top\), survives the softmax, and dies against \(V\). Between its birth and its death sits the entire memory bill. Nor does the formula say which axis the softmax normalises over.

Урок.Lesson. Нужна запись, в которой ось видна. Это neural circuit diagram: провод — ось, глиф — операция над ней. Инструмент, а не тема: выучим за три слайда и дальше будем просто им пользоваться.We need a notation in which the axis is visible. That is the neural circuit diagram: a wire is an axis, a glyph is an operation on it. A tool, not a topic: three slides to learn it, and then we simply use it.

Before the formula arrives, earn the notation. A formula is a recipe: it tells you what to compute, in what order. What it does not tell you is what the computation costs, and it cannot show you a bug. Look at softmax of Q K transpose over root d k, times V. Every symbol in it refers to a matrix you have — Q, K, V — and yet the object that dominates the memory of every Transformer you will ever train, the n by n score matrix, appears nowhere as a named thing. It is born the moment you write Q K transpose, it persists through the softmax, and it is destroyed the moment you multiply by V. That birth and death is invisible in the algebra. Worse: the formula does not even record which axis the softmax normalises over — and, as we will see in three slides, getting that wrong does not crash anything. So for the next three slides we learn a notation in which an axis is a thing you can point at: a wire. This is a tool, not a topic. We teach it once, briefly, and then simply use it for the rest of the course.
