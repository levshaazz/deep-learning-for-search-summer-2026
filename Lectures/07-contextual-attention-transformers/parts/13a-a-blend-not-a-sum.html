Two consequences: an attention layer alone adds no non-linearity — the FFN later supplies it; and one question stays open — where the \(\alpha\) come from. That is the next act.

The mechanism behind the word "blend", which the act used but never justified. Write the output for token i as a sum over all tokens of a weight times that token's value. Two constraints do all the work: the weights are non-negative, and they sum to one across the row. Non-negativity means a neighbour can only contribute, never cancel another neighbour; summing to one means the output's scale is independent of how many tokens are in the sentence — a forty-token sequence does not produce a vector forty times longer than a four-token one. Together they say something stronger and worth stating: the output is a convex combination, so it lies inside the convex hull of the value vectors. Attention mixes what is already present and cannot point in a direction none of the values point in. That is exactly why the block cannot be attention alone — the feed-forward network later in this lecture is what breaks out of the hull. And it isolates the one thing still unspecified: how the alpha weights are computed. That is the whole of the next act.
