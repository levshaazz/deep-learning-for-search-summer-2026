Разница не количественная, а типовая. У таблицы производная ноль по построению: сколько её ни обучай, «bank» не почувствует «river». Контекстным вектор делает ровно ненулевая перекрёстная производная — а как получить её дёшево и сразу для всех \(i\), отвечает следующий акт.The gap is one of type, not of size. For a lookup that derivative is zero by construction: train it forever and “bank” still cannot feel “river”. A vector turns contextual exactly when the cross-derivative is non-zero — and how to get one cheaply, for every \(i\) at once, is what the next act answers.

The mechanism this act was missing. The comparison table asserted that a contextual vector "depends on the word and its context", but never said what that buys formally. Here it is: a static embedding is a function of one index, so the derivative of output i with respect to input j is exactly zero for every other token — not small, zero, by the type of the object. A contextual encoder is a map from a whole sequence to a whole sequence, and what makes it contextual is precisely that those cross-derivatives are non-zero: perturb the neighbour "river" and the vector sitting on "bank" moves. Say the consequence out loud: no amount of training enlarges a lookup table into a contextual model, because the missing thing is not capacity, it is the wiring between positions. That reframes the rest of the lecture as one engineering question — how do you build a sequence-to-sequence map whose cross-derivatives are non-zero, cheaply, for every position in parallel? Attention is the answer, and it arrives on the next divider.
