The catchphrase opened the lecture — the crew has too many voices, who do you listen to — and we answered it: attention decides who to heed, the Transformer turns that into contextual vectors, and contrastive learning shapes those vectors for search, caging the Gremlin. The open question for next time is efficiency and architecture. Do we encode the query and the document separately and compare vectors — a bi-encoder, fast enough to search millions — or jointly for maximum accuracy — a cross-encoder, too slow to run over the whole corpus? Lecture 10 is the neural retrieval cascade that uses both. The Council has learned to listen; now we make it search at scale.
