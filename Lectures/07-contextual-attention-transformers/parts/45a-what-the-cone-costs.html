Пока \(\lVert\mu\rVert\gg\lVert r\rVert\), различающее слагаемое тонет в общем, и порядок выдачи держится на последних знаках. Отсюда обе половины лекарства: вычесть \(\mu\) — центрирование, первый шаг отбеливания (whitening) — убирает добавку точно; контрастное обучение растит разброс \(r\), расталкивая негативы.While \(\lVert\mu\rVert\gg\lVert r\rVert\) the discriminating term drowns and the ranking hangs on the last digits. Hence the two halves of the cure: subtract \(\mu\) — centring, whitening’s first step — and the offset is gone exactly; contrastive training grows \(r\)’s spread by pushing negatives apart.

The mechanism under the trap. The act asserted that a narrow cone inflates cosine and kills discrimination; here is the algebra that makes it obvious. Split every embedding into the cloud's mean vector mu — the axis of the cone — plus that text's own deviation r. Expand the dot product of two texts and you get four terms. The first is the squared norm of mu, which is the same number for every pair in the corpus: a constant offset that raises all similarities together and eats the usable range. The two middle terms involve one text each, so they can shift a text's similarity to everything but cannot express a relation between the two. Only the last term, the dot product of the two deviations, actually knows which pair you asked about — and when mu is long relative to r, it is the smallest term in the sum, so the ranking survives only in the trailing digits. Now both cures read off the same line rather than sounding like folklore. Subtract mu and the constant vanishes exactly — that is centring, the first step of whitening. Contrastive training attacks the other side: pushing negatives apart grows the spread of r relative to mu. Normalising onto the sphere then keeps the geometry stable.
