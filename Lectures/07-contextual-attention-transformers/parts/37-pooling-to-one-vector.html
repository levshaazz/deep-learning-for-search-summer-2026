[CLS]: один токен, обученный «суммировать» — удобно для классификации. Mean-pooling: среднее по токенам — на практике лучше для сходства предложений (SBERT). Выбор пулинга — часть дизайна ретривера. И не путай со словом «пулинг» из L5: там это отбор документов на судейство (TREC), здесь — стягивание векторов токенов в один. Слово одно, операции не связаны.[CLS]: a single token trained to “summarise” — handy for classification. Mean-pooling: average over tokens — empirically better for sentence similarity (SBERT). The pooling choice is part of retriever design. And do not confuse this with L5’s “pooling”: there it selects documents for judging (TREC), here it collapses token vectors into one. Same word, unrelated operations.

Reimers & Gurevych (2019) показали: сырой BERT-[CLS] для косинусной близости работает плохо — хуже усреднённого GloVe. Нужна не только архитектура, но и правильная цель обучения — следующая часть.Reimers & Gurevych (2019) found raw BERT-[CLS] for cosine similarity is poor — worse than averaged GloVe. The architecture alone is not enough; you need the right training objective — the next part.

Pin down pooling and set up the next turn. The [CLS] token is trained as a sequence summary and is the natural pooler for classification. For sentence similarity, though, mean-pooling — averaging the token vectors — tends to work better, and it is the default in Sentence-BERT. The sharp lesson from Reimers and Gurevych in 2019 is that the architecture by itself does not give you a good search space: raw BERT [CLS] vectors compared with cosine actually underperform averaged GloVe, the static vectors from last lecture. That is the motivation for everything that follows — we have a powerful contextual encoder, but its vectors are not yet shaped for retrieval. Contrastive learning is how we shape them.
