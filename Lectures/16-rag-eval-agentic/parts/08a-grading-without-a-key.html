The mechanism behind the act's promise. The act says a fluent answer reveals nothing, that there is no gold answer, and that RAGAS grades "against the retrieved context" — but never shows what makes reference-free grading possible at all. Here is the shape. A classic metric needs two arguments, the answer and a key, and the key is missing. Two moves rescue it. First, decompose the answer into atomic claims: a whole paragraph has no truth value you can count, a single claim does, and phrasing varies while the claim does not — which is precisely the "many valid phrasings" objection from the previous slide, answered. Second, replace string comparison with a relation check: ask whether the retrieved context entails each claim. Nothing external is needed, because the question and the context are themselves a partial key — they do not say what the answer is, but they do say what it may assert and what it must address. The score is then a counting fraction, hand-checkable and diagnostic: it names which claim failed rather than returning a distance. Every metric of the next act is this same shape with different texts plugged into the two sides of the entailment. Say the caveat out loud now, because act 3 is built on it: the entailment check is performed by a model, so the score is a proxy from its very first line.
