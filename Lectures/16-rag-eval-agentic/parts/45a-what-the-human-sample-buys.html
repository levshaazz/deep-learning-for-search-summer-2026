Выборка с людскими метками не делает прокси истиной — она оценивает \(b\), и только поэтому прокси можно пользоваться. Отсюда правила: держи её неизменной, сравнивай одним судьёй, сообщай разницу, а не абсолют.A human-labelled sample does not turn the proxy into truth — it estimates \(b\), which is what makes the proxy usable. Hence the rules: keep it frozen, compare under one judge, report the difference, not the absolute.

The mechanism behind this act's ladder. The comparison table asserts that humans "calibrate the cheap proxies" and act as "the anchor that keeps them honest" — but never shows what calibration mechanically does. Model any proxy score as true quality plus a systematic offset plus run-to-run noise. Three consequences follow, and each is a rule the act already states without justification. (1) The absolute proxy number is uninterpretable on its own, because it carries an unknown offset; averaging more runs shrinks the noise and does nothing at all to the offset, so a bigger evaluation set never fixes a biased judge. (2) A difference between two systems under the same judge cancels the shared part of the offset — which is exactly why pairwise comparison is more trustworthy than absolute scoring, and exactly why verbosity bias is the dangerous one: it makes the offset depend on the answer, so it no longer cancels. (3) Goodhart, in the same notation: optimising the observed score pushes the system toward whatever the offset rewards, so the number climbs while true quality stands still — the flip we computed by hand in act 3. What the human sample buys is an estimate of the offset, not the truth. Keep it small, held out, and frozen; re-use it to check the proxies rather than to train against them.
