On in-domain MS MARCO, dense models clearly beat BM25. But zero-shot across BEIR, plain BM25 (0.43) beats DPR (0.38) — a 30-year-old lexical baseline still embarrasses some neural models out of domain. Sources: MS MARCO / Anserini; Karpukhin 2020; Khattab 2020; Thakur et al. 2021 (BEIR).

Numbers verbatim from the benchmarks JSON, all cited, none synthesised. On MS MARCO, a large in-domain training set, dense retrievers clearly win: BM25 at 0.187 MRR, DPR 0.330, ColBERT 0.360. But the headline is the BEIR row. Averaged zero-shot over eighteen diverse datasets, plain BM25 scores 0.43 nDCG and beats DPR at 0.38 — a thirty-year-old lexical method outperforms a dense model that was not trained on the target domain. ColBERT, a late-interaction model, reaches 0.50. The lesson for the course: BM25 is the baseline every neural method must beat, and out of domain it is genuinely hard to beat. BM25 scores content; it has no notion of authority or popularity — that gap motivates PageRank next.
