Numbers verbatim from the benchmarks JSON, all cited, none synthesised. On MS MARCO, a large in-domain training set, dense retrievers clearly win: BM25 at 0.187 MRR, dense ~0.330 (later DPR-style reproductions; the original DPR paper does not evaluate MS MARCO), ColBERT 0.360. But the headline is the BEIR row. Averaged zero-shot over eighteen diverse datasets, plain BM25 scores 0.43 nDCG and beats DPR at 0.23 — DPR generalizes worst of all models in the BEIR paper (−47.7% vs BM25), so a thirty-year-old lexical method outperforms a dense model that was not trained on the target domain. ColBERTv2 (Santhanam 2022), a late-interaction model, reaches 0.50 over its 13 reported BEIR sets. The lesson for the course: BM25 is the baseline every neural method must beat, and out of domain it is genuinely hard to beat. BM25 scores content; it has no notion of authority or popularity — that gap motivates PageRank next.
