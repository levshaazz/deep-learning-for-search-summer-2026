Bag-of-WordsBoW

Represent a document by the multiset of its words — keep how often each term appears, discard the order.

A document becomes a vector indexed by the vocabulary: entry \(i\) is the count of term \(i\). Stack the vectors for all documents and you get the document-term matrix. It is the input to TF-IDF, BM25, and classic text classifiers.

Name it. The Bag-of-Words model is the single most important representation in classical IR: a document is just a vector of word counts over a fixed vocabulary. The word "bag" is literal — order is thrown away, only multiplicity survives. Every weighting we study (raw counts, TF-IDF, BM25) is a transformation of this count vector. Its great virtue is simplicity; its great flaw, that it forgets word order, comes two slides on.
