Retrieval by cosine

Weight every document with TF-IDF, weight the query the same way, and rank by cosine. The shared rare terms drive the score; shared stopwords (idf 0) add nothing.

Near-duplicate detection

Two documents with cosine \(\approx 1\) on TF-IDF vectors are almost the same text — the basis of dedup and plagiarism checks.

Why it generalizes

  • ·Same machinery as keyword extraction — just compare two weighted vectors instead of reading one.
  • ·Length-normalized, so a long and a short article about NASA still match.
  • ·Still blind to synonyms: shuttle vs spacecraft share no axis (the Gremlin again).
The second live application closes the loop with the cosine ranker. TF-IDF plus cosine is a complete retrieval system: weight the query, weight every document, rank by angle. The same setup detects near-duplicates — two texts whose TF-IDF vectors point almost the same way are essentially the same document, which powers dedup and plagiarism detection. Its blind spot is the recurring one: synonyms occupy different axes, so cosine cannot see that "shuttle" and "spacecraft" mean nearly the same thing. That limitation is the whole reason embeddings exist.
