What Bag-of-Words throws away

order · sparsity · dimensionality

Order is gone

!
dog bites man = man bites dog

Identical bags, opposite meaning. BoW cannot tell them apart — a real limit for meaning-sensitive tasks.

Partial fix: \(n\)-grams ("new york" as one feature) re-inject local order, at the cost of a much larger vocabulary.

Sparse & high-dimensional

  • ·Vocabulary is huge (\(10^5\)–\(10^6\) terms); each document touches a few hundred → vectors are almost all zeros.
  • ·Stored as the inverted index, not a dense matrix — that is why the positional index of Part 2 matters.
  • ·No notion of synonymy: couch and sofa are orthogonal axes (foreshadows the Gremlin).
Two structural limits. First, discarding order means "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" have identical representations — fatal for tasks where word order carries meaning, partially patched by n-gram features. Second, the vectors live in a space with one dimension per vocabulary word, so they are enormous and almost entirely zero; we never materialise them densely, we store the inverted index. And crucially, distinct strings are orthogonal axes, so synonyms get no credit — the lexical gap, which we name properly in Part 8.
