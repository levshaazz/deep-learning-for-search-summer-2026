Simplify → the two extremes

BM15 (no norm) · BM11 (full norm)
$$ \text{BM15: } \frac{(K_1+1)f}{K_1 + f} \qquad\qquad \text{BM11: } \frac{(K_1+1)f}{K_1\,\frac{\mathrm{len}}{\mathrm{avgdl}} + f} $$

After dropping \(G\) and fixing \(S_1=K_1+1\), only the TF factor remains — in two flavors. BM15 ignores length; BM11 normalizes by it fully. Real collections usually want something in between.

With G gone and the scale fixed, the per-term weight is just the idf times a tf factor, and that factor comes in two pure forms. BM15 uses K1 plus one times f over K1 plus f — no length term at all. BM11 divides instead by K1 times relative length plus f — full normalisation. Experiments showed neither extreme is universally best: some collections have meaningfully varied document lengths and want normalisation, others do not. That empirical "in between" is the gap the very next idea fills.
