Here \(k_1\) is the saga's \(K_1\) (renamed lowercase; \(S_1{=}k_1{+}1\) folded into the numerator). One knob \(b\) slides continuously between the two earlier algorithms — they are the ends of one continuum.

This is the punchline of the saga. Put a single parameter b inside the length bracket as a convex combination: at b equals zero the bracket is one and you recover BM15, at b equals one it is full relative length and you recover BM11, and in between you get any blend. So BM25 is not a new algorithm — it is the realisation that BM11 and BM15 are two ends of one continuum, and that the right amount of length normalisation is a tunable dial. That insight, plus the saturating tf factor, is the entire idea. Everything else is choosing k1 and b.
