From odds to a sum over query terms

why every scorer here is a \(\sum_{t\in q}\)

Where the \(\sum_{t\in q}\) shape comes from — sketched on a 2-term query \(q=\{t_1, t_2\}\). This derivation is motivation, not examinable; you only need the boxed result.

01
Start from the odds
PRP says rank by \(O(R\mid d)=\frac{P(R{=}1\mid d)}{P(R{=}0\mid d)}\). A ratio is awkward to compute directly — so transform it.
odds
02
Bayes flips it to likelihoods
\(\frac{P(R{=}1\mid d)}{P(R{=}0\mid d)} = \frac{P(d\mid R{=}1)}{P(d\mid R{=}0)}\cdot\frac{P(R{=}1)}{P(R{=}0)}\). The prior is the same for every \(d\) → it doesn't change the order.
likelihood ratio
03
Term independence factorizes
Assume terms are independent given relevance: \(P(d\mid R)=\prod_t P(t\mid R)\). The ratio becomes a product over terms: \(\frac{P(t_1\mid R{=}1)}{P(t_1\mid R{=}0)}\cdot\frac{P(t_2\mid R{=}1)}{P(t_2\mid R{=}0)}\).
\(\prod_{t}\)
→
Take logs → a sum
\(\log\) turns the product into \(\sum_{t\in q} w(t)\), with \(w(t)=\log\frac{P(t\mid R{=}1)}{P(t\mid R{=}0)}\). That is the per-term weight — the next slide names it BM1.
\(\sum_{t\in q} w(t)\)
4/4
The scaffold that makes the prerequisite cliff honest. Students should not have to take "the odds factorise into a sum over terms" on faith. Four short moves on a two-term query: (1) PRP hands us the odds of relevance; (2) Bayes rewrites the posterior odds as a likelihood ratio times a prior, and the prior is constant across documents so it drops out of the ranking; (3) the term-independence assumption — strong and admittedly false, but the working assumption of the Binary Independence Model — factorises the document likelihood into a product over terms; (4) taking logs turns that product into a sum of per-term log-likelihood-ratio weights. That sum is exactly the shape of BM1, BM25, and every scorer in this part. State plainly that this is motivation, not an examinable derivation — the boxed per-term weight on the next slide is all they need to carry forward.
