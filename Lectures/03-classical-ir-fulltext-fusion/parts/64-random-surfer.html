A surfer follows a random link with prob \(d\), else teleports to a random page. Teleport makes the walk ergodic — it breaks out of cycles and guarantees one unique stationary rank. A true sink (a dangling page with no out-links) needs one more fix: redistribute its mass each step, or treat it as linking to all pages.

PageRank is the stationary distribution of a random surfer. With probability d, here 0.85, the surfer follows one of the current page's out-links uniformly; with probability one minus d it teleports to a uniformly random page. The teleport term is not cosmetic — without it a small cycle acts as a rank sink that absorbs all probability mass and never gives it back, so the iteration never converges to anything useful. Teleport guarantees the chain is ergodic, so a unique stationary distribution exists. A true dangling page with no out-links is a separate issue: damping alone does not conserve mass when one exists — its mass has to be reinjected each step (treat it as linking to every page, or renormalise). Our toy graph has no dangling node, so it sidesteps that. That stationary distribution is the rank.
