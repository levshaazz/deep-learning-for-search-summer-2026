Where the ~100 ms budget comes from

attention · QPS · fan-out
  • ·Human attention. Past ~100 ms a UI feels sluggish; past ~1 s users abandon. Search must feel instant.
  • ·Throughput (QPS). A big engine serves tens of thousands of queries per second; per-query cost is multiplied by that rate.
  • ·Fan-out. One query hits hundreds of index shards in parallel; the slowest shard sets the latency, so each must finish in a few ms.

The budget is a hard wall

A retrieval stage gets only a slice of ~100 ms; the rest goes to re-ranking, snippet generation, and the network. So first-stage retrieval must be sublinear in the corpus size.

This is why we precompute a structure once and consult it per query, instead of scanning documents.

The budget is not arbitrary. It is set by human perception (a sluggish UI loses users), by throughput (cost is paid tens of thousands of times per second), and by fan-out (a query is split across many shards and the slowest one wins). Within that hundred-millisecond wall, first-stage retrieval gets only a slice — so it has to be sublinear in N. That requirement is the whole motivation for the inverted index.
