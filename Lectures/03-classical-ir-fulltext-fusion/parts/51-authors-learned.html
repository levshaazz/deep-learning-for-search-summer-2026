The lesson

BM25 is what survived TREC experiments, not a closed-form derivation. The team added the G factor, evaluated, and switched it off — the discipline that made a 30-year baseline.

The engineering beat that gives BM25 its character. Across the Okapi TREC runs the authors learned three things. The G length-correction's best weight was zero, so they removed it — a model with fewer parameters that performs just as well is a strictly better model. The scale constants S1 and S3 are best set to K plus one, which makes the saturating factor equal one for a single occurrence at average document length, so one mention of a term yields the bare idf. The meta-lesson is the point: BM25's robustness comes from ruthless empirical pruning, not elegance. They tried more, measured, and kept only what earned its place.
