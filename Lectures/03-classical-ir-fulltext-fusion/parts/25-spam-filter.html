Live BoW: a spam filter

word counts → spam / ham

A classic BoW application: features are word counts, and Naïve Bayes multiplies per-word likelihoods \(P(\text{word}\mid\text{class})\). "free", "money", "winner" push toward spam; order never matters. Same representation, different task than retrieval.

Concrete BoW in the wild. A spam filter ignores grammar entirely: it counts words and asks which class makes those counts more likely. Naïve Bayes assumes words are independent given the class and multiplies their likelihoods — crude, but famously effective on email. The point for us is that the very same Bag-of-Words vector that feeds a classifier here feeds a retrieval scorer in the rest of the lecture. The representation is shared; only the downstream model changes.
