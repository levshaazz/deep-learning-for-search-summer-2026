The recall ceiling

You can't re-rank what you didn't retrieve

If retrieval gets 80%, a perfect re-ranker still tops out at 80%.

!
The hard ceiling is first-stage recall

Whatever the retrieval stage (Stage 0) drops, no re-ranker — even a perfect one — can recover.

Precisely: \(\text{recall@}10\) of the served list \(\le \text{recall@}N\) of the retrieved set, where \(N\) is the retrieval depth (e.g. 1000). A re-ranker reorders the \(N\) candidates; it cannot inject a document that was never in the set.

Loss that happens at retrieval is permanent. Invest in first-stage recall (and hybrid retrieval) before you tune the re-ranker.

The single most common production mistake: teams pour effort into a fancy re-ranker while 20% of relevant documents were silently dropped at the retrieval stage (Stage 0). The leaky-bucket image makes it physical — the re-ranker robot cannot reach a star that already fell through the floor. The fix is better recall: hybrid retrieval (lexical + dense), which we will see closes recall@1000 to ≈0.98.
