Timeline

Each step kept the previous step's wins and patched one of its scars. Production today runs all of them, cascaded.

  1. 1958
    Luhn

    TF / auto-index

    Term frequency marks topic. The bedrock weight.

  2. 1972
    Spärck Jones

    IDF

    Down-weight words that appear everywhere. Specificity matters.

  3. 1994
    Robertson · Walker

    BM25

    TF · IDF with length normalisation. Still the lexical baseline 30 years later.

  4. 2013
    Mikolov et al.

    word2vec

    Words become vectors. Synonyms get close. The lexical gap starts to close.

  5. 2019
    SBERT · DPR

    Dense retrieval

    Bi-encoders make semantic search fast — but stumble on rare exact tokens.

  6. ~2020
    Bruch · Lin · … (RRF: Cormack 2009)

    Hybrid fusion

    Fuse lexical and dense (RRF dates to Cormack et al. 2009). recall@1000 ≈ 0.98 — the production norm.

  7. 2020+
    Lewis et al.

    RAG

    Retrieval grounds an LLM. The course's final destination.

The narrative spine of the whole term, drawn as the arc that took us here. BM25 over the inverted index is fast, interpretable, and strong on rare/exact terms (names, IDs, codes) — but it hits the lexical-gap wall. Dense retrieval (SBERT, DPR) bridges synonymy/polysemy but can lose substantial recall on rare exact tokens (names, IDs, codes). Fuse them and recall@1000 climbs to ≈0.98 — hybrid is the production norm. The orange-bead pivots — BM25 (1994) and dense (2019) — are the two transitions everything else hangs off.
