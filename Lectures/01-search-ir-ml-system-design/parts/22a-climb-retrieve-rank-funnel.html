Каскад с прошлого слайда — теперь пройди его сверху вниз сам. Каждая следующая ступень тратит больше вычислений на одного кандидата — и потому обязана получать этих кандидатов всё меньше. Дешёвый отбор отвечает за полноту, дорогой финал — за точность там, куда смотрит пользователь. Запомни это разделение труда: весь курс — о том, чем заполнять эти ступени.The cascade from the previous slide — now walk it top to bottom yourself. Each successive stage spends more compute per candidate, so it must be handed fewer of them. The cheap sweep owns recall; the expensive finish owns precision where the user actually looks. Remember this division of labour: the whole course is about what to put on each stage.

Act 2’s climb, mounted right after the cascade is introduced and right before the recall ceiling. All stage sizes are read from data/l1-funnel.json — nothing is computed in the browser. Drive it with the arrow keys: (0) the whole corpus and why you cannot run the expensive model on it; (1) the cheap high-recall sweep — DWELL here: whatever this stage drops is gone forever, which is exactly the recall-ceiling point the NEXT slide formalises; (2) the mid-cost ranker; (3) the expensive re-ranker at the top. Students usually stumble on “why not just run the best model on everything” — answer it at step 0 with the per-stage cost framing, not with hand-waving.
