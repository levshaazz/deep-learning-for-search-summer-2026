Building an inverted index, by hand

Worked example · fox AND quick

Two documents — Doc 1 "the quick brown fox", Doc 2 "the lazy fox". Build the index, then answer fox AND quick.

01
Tokenize each document
Doc 1 → the · quick · brown · fox; Doc 2 → the · lazy · fox.
2 docs
02
Emit (term, docID) pairs
(the,1)(quick,1)(brown,1)(fox,1)(the,2)(lazy,2)(fox,2).
7 pairs
03
Sort & group into postings
brown→[1], fox→[1,2], lazy→[2], quick→[1], the→[1,2].
5 terms
04
Look up the query terms
fox → [1,2] and quick → [1]. We only touch these two postings lists, not the corpus.
\(\mathrm{df}_{\text{fox}}=2\)
→
Intersect the postings
[1,2] ∩ [1] = [1]. Answer: Doc 1. A merge of two sorted lists — \(O(\text{list lengths})\), not \(O(N)\).
→ Doc 1
5/5
Step through it slowly — this is the concrete payoff of the previous definition. Emphasise step 5: the intersection is a linear merge of two sorted lists, which is why postings are kept sorted. With more terms you intersect shortest-first to minimise work. This worked example is the seed of BM25 scoring next lecture: \(\mathrm{tf}\) and \(\mathrm{df}\) are already in the postings.
