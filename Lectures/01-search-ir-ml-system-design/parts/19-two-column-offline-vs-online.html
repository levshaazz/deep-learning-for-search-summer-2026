Two clocks, two worlds

Build the index vs walk the index

Offline · build

Crawl, analyze, index. Latency-insensitive, throughput-bound. Crawl billions of pages over days; rebuild on a schedule.

i
The clock here is hours.

You optimise for coverage and cost-per-document.

Online · serve

Parse → retrieve → rank → present. Latency-critical. Walk the pre-built index and answer in \(<200\) ms.

!
The clock here is milliseconds.

You optimise for tail latency (p99) and relevance.

"Offline builds the index; online walks it." The asymmetry between the two clocks shapes every design decision that follows.

The mantra: offline builds the index, online walks it. This split is why we can afford an expensive analysis pipeline (it runs once, offline) but must keep retrieval cheap (it runs per request). It also seeds the train/serving-skew discussion in Part 03 — the same feature must be computed identically in both worlds.
