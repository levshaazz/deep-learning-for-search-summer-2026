The cascade: narrow before you spend

Retrieval → ranking → re-rank
Corpus \(\sim 10^{9}\)
the whole index — far too many to score with an expensive model
Retrieval (L0) \(\sim 10^{3}\)
cheap, high-recall: BM25 / ANN — optimises recall
Ranking (L1) \(\sim 10^{2}\)
moderate cost, many features: GBDT / LTR
Re-rank (L2) \(\sim 10\)
expensive cross-encoder + business rules — optimises precision@k

Why not run the best model on every document? A BERT cross-encoder at ~tens of ms/pair × \(10^{9}\) documents is roughly \(10^{8}\times\) over budget. So: cheap-and-broad, then expensive-and-precise.

The cascade is the central systems idea of search. The arithmetic on the caption sells it: you cannot afford the good model on \(10^{9}\) documents, so each stage spends ~10× more compute on ~10× fewer candidates. Recall lives early (you can never recover a document you didn't retrieve — next slide); precision lives late. The same funnel reappears as a latency budget in Part 03.
