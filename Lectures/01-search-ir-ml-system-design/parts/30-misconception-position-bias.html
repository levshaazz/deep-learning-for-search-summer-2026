Click logs lie

Position & presentation bias
What teams assume

"Result #1 gets the most clicks, so it must be the most relevant — let's train the ranker on clicks."

Eye-tracking (the "Golden Triangle", Enquiro 2005) shows attention concentrates top-left. The #1 organic result earns ~32–33% of clicks largely because it is #1, not because it is best. So raw clicks are biased training data: a naive learning-to-rank model learns "predict what was already on top" and freezes the incumbents in place. Fix: model the bias explicitly — unbiased LTR with inverse-propensity weighting (we return to this in Part 03).

Worked simulation · grounded in _research/data/position_bias.py. Cascade examination model from Craswell et al. 2008 ("An Experimental Comparison of Click Position-Bias Models"): examination probability \( p_r \propto 1/r^{\gamma} \), the rank-dependent decay that turns equal relevance into unequal clicks. The clicks ≠ relevance framing — that clicks reveal relative not absolute relevance — is the contribution of Joachims, Granka et al. 2005 ("Accurately Interpreting Clickthrough Data as Implicit Feedback"); the ~32% rank-1 share is the Enquiro 2005 "golden triangle" eye-tracking number that anchors \( \gamma = 0.94 \) here.
Under that model, with equal true relevance for the top-10: rank 1 = 32.3%, rank 2 = 16.8%, rank 3 = 11.5%, … top-3 together 60.6%.
So a click on rank-1 carries nearly 3× (2.8×) the weight of a click on rank-3, even when the docs are equally good — that is the bias to undo.
Train LTR on raw clicks and you bake the ranking into the model; IPW reweights each click by \( 1/p_r \) to recover an unbiased estimate of true relevance.

This is the bridge between the IR half and the systems half. Position bias means the feedback you collect is contaminated by the very ranking that produced it — a feedback loop. Training naively on clicks bakes in a rich-get-richer dynamic. The fix (IPW / unbiased LTR) shows up again on the feedback-loops slide in Part 03; flag it now so it lands later.
