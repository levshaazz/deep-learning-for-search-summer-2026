Bust the myth
What everyone wants to believe

"Our new model wins on \(\text{nDCG}@10\) offline, so it will win with real users."

The offline–online gap is real and routine. Offline metrics are computed in isolation on historical, biased logs; they miss freshness, diversity, trust, presentation, session dynamics, and novelty. A model can win offline and lose the A/B test. Strategy: validate which offline metrics actually correlate with online lift, then use offline for iteration and online (interleaving + A/B) for the real verdict.

Numeric anchor. Kohavi, Tang & Xu (2020), Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: at Microsoft Bing, only about 1 in 3 ideas that look promising offline produce a positive online A/B effect; the other ~⅔ are flat or worse. Comparable rates have been reported at Netflix and Booking. The discipline: treat your offline metric as a hypothesis to validate online, not a verdict.

This is the most important reality check in the systems half. Offline metrics are necessary (you can't A/B every idea) but not sufficient. The discipline is to treat your offline metric as a hypothesis about online behaviour and to measure the correlation empirically.
