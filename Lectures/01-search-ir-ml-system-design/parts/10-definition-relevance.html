The slippery word: relevance

Definition
Definition
Relevance does this document satisfy the need?

Binary vs graded — relevant / not, or a 5-point scale (Perfect · Excellent · Good · Fair · Bad) that feeds \(\text{nDCG}\).

Topical vs situational — a document can be perfectly on-topic yet useless: wrong level, outdated, paywalled, wrong language.

Relevance is dynamic (freshness), personal and situational. This is precisely why an offline label rarely equals real user happiness.

Relevance is not a property of the document alone — it is a relation between document, need, and context. The topical/situational split is the seed of the whole offline–online gap we hit in Part 03: a human judge marks topical relevance against a label, but the user lives in a situation the judge never saw. Graded relevance is what makes \(\text{nDCG}\) possible — keep that thread.
