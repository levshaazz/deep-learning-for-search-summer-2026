Audience question

Quick check
Why does a search engine return a ranked list instead of just every matching document?
A Ranking is cheaper to compute than returning everything
B Relevance is graded and user attention is top-heavy, so order is the product
C To hide irrelevant documents from the user entirely
Reveal explanation
B. Relevance comes in degrees, and users overwhelmingly read top-down — most clicks land in the first few positions. So the order, not the membership, carries the value. Returning "everything" is useless when "everything" is \(10^9\) documents. This is why ranking metrics like \(\text{nDCG}@10\) discount by position.
Let them commit before revealing. Distractor A is tempting but wrong — ranking is more work, not less. The real reasons are graded relevance (Slide 10) and top-heavy attention (Slide 29's position bias previews this). Tie it back: order is the product.
