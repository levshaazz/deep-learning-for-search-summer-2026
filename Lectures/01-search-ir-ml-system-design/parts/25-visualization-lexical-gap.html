Furnas et al. (1987): двое людей выбирают одно и то же слово для одной и той же вещи менее чем в 20% случаев. Один этот факт мотивирует LSI, расширение запроса и нейронный поиск.Furnas et al. (1987): two people pick the same word for the same thing under 20% of the time. This single fact motivates LSI, query expansion, and neural retrieval.

If students remember one "why search is hard" slide, make it this one. Synonymy and polysemy are dual failures of lexical matching: synonymy means a relevant doc shares no query words (recall loss); polysemy means irrelevant docs share the query word (precision loss). The Furnas 80%-disagreement number is the empirical anchor. The escape hatch — embeddings that place "couch" and "sofa" near each other — is the entire motivation for the dense-retrieval weeks. Meet the Lexical Gremlin: the little troublemaker holding that BM25 wall in place between synonyms — embeddings are how we finally get him to let go.
