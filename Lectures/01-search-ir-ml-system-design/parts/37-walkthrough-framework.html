The framework, in six steps

Each chip = the artifact you produce

One disciplined loop, told through our running search example. The chip on the right is the concrete deliverable of each step.

01
Frame the problem
Business goal → ML objective → proxy. "Users find what they want" → learn \(P(\text{relevant}\mid q,d)\). Google Rule #1: don't be afraid to launch without ML first.
spec
02
Get the data
Collection, labels, splits, leakage. Query/click logs + graded 0–4 judgments. Beware temporal leakage and position bias.
\((q,d,\text{label})\)
03
Model & baselines
Simplest first. BM25 / two-tower → GBDT / neural re-ranker. Google Rule #4: keep the first model simple, get the infrastructure right.
BM25 baseline
04
Evaluate
Offline that predicts online, then online. \(\text{nDCG}@10\) offline; interleaving + A/B online.
\(\text{nDCG}@10\)
05
Serve
Latency, throughput, cost. ANN retrieval → ranking under a \(p_{99}\) budget. Watch train/serve skew (Rules #29/#32).
\(p_{99} < 200\)ms
→
Monitor & close the loop
Drift, skew, degeneracy. Track feature/score distributions and online metrics; logs feed the next retraining cycle.
logs → retrain
6/6
This is the backbone of the systems half — memorise the six steps. Two Google Rules of ML anchor the early steps: Rule #1 (a heuristic often beats a premature model and de-risks the infra) and Rule #4 (a simple first model proves the pipeline before you add complexity). The chips are deliverables, so students can ask "what artifact have I actually produced?" at each stage. Step 6 loops back to step 2 — the lifecycle is a cycle, not a line.
