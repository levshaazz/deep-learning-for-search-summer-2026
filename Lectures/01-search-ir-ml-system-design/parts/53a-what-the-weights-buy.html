Постановка задачи живёт не в преамбуле, а внутри функции потерь; поэтому её ошибку и не видно, читая код модели.That is the reframe as mechanism: change the example weights and the same sigmoid estimates a different quantity. Framing lives inside the loss, not in the preamble — which is why reading the model code never shows it is wrong.

The mechanism behind the previous slide's claim that the odds "approximate expected watch time" — stated there as a fact, derived here. Setup: N logged impressions, k of them clicked, T_i the watch time observed on a click and zero otherwise. In a weighted logistic the positives carry weight T_i and the negatives weight one, so the learned odds are the total positive weight over the total negative weight: sum of T_i divided by N minus k. Now read the right-hand side. Since k is a small fraction of N, the denominator is close to N, so the ratio is close to k over N times the mean watch time given a click — which is exactly the expectation of T over all impressions, because non-clicks contribute zero. Two things to say slowly. First: nothing about the architecture, the features or the logs changed; only the weights attached to the training rows did, and that alone moved the sigmoid from "probability of a click" to "expected watch time". Second, and this is the point of the act: framing is not a slide before the modelling work, it is a term inside the loss — invisible in the model's code, invisible in the training curves, and expensive precisely because it fails while every dashboard looks healthy. Deliberately symbolic: N, k and T are quantities of the reader's own system, and this deck states no number that data/ does not ground.
