The cascade is a systems knob

Latency & cost vs relevance gain
Stage choice Added latency Relevance gain Note
ANN retrieval (\(\sim 10^{9} \to \sim 10^{3}\)) ~10ms sets the recall ceiling ~99.9999% reduction
Light re-ranker (MiniLM, \(\sim 10^{3} \to \sim 10^{2}\)) ~60ms +5–15 \(\text{nDCG}@10\) distilled: 70–90% of gain at 30–50% cost
Heavy re-ranker (\(\sim 10^{2} \to \sim 10\)) ~+200ms finalises the order only on a tiny shortlist

Candidate-set size and per-stage model complexity are the two dials. Halving a candidate set (e.g. \(10^{2} \to 50\)) roughly halves cost for a tiny relevance loss — that is the trade you tune.

This reframes the Part 02 cascade as an explicit engineering decision rather than an IR abstraction. The numbers matter: a distilled 60–120M-parameter re-ranker recovers most of the relevance gain at a fraction of the cost — usually the right default. The two tunable dials are candidate-set size and model complexity per stage; you spend your latency budget where it buys the most \(\text{nDCG}\) per millisecond.
