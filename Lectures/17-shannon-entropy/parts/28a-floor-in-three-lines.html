Красиво то, что KL появляется здесь, за пять слайдов до акта про языковые модели — и появляется не как «мера расстояния между распределениями», а как переплата за неверную длину слова. Через пять слайдов та же величина вернётся под именем «налог за неверную модель». Это одно и то же число, а не аналогия. Cover & Thomas §5.3. The beautiful part is that KL shows up here, five slides before the language-model act — and not as “a distance between distributions” but as the overpay for a wrong codeword length. Five slides on, the same quantity returns as “the wrong-model tax”. One number, not an analogy. Cover & Thomas §5.3.

The proof the deck asserted but never gave. Rewrite L̄ − H as Σ p_i log2(p_i / 2^{−l_i}), split off the normaliser, and you get D_KL(p‖r) − log2 Σ 2^{−l_i} where r is the normalised code distribution. Both terms are ≥ 0: the first by Gibbs' inequality, the second by Kraft. Equality iff the code is complete AND p_i = 2^{−l_i} — i.e. iff the probabilities are dyadic, which is exactly why the dyadic source hits the floor and the non-dyadic one does not. Teaching payload: a CODE IS A MODEL. Choosing codeword lengths is choosing a distribution, and paying too many bits is paying KL. That identity is the whole bridge into act 5.
