целочисленный ступенчатый логарифм — и мотивирован он Ципфом, а не Шенноном an integer bucketed logarithm — motivated by Zipf, not by Shannon

$$ f(n) = m \ \text{ при }\ 2^{m-1} < n \le 2^m $$ $$ w(t) \;=\; f(N) - f(n) + 1 $$

Никакого \(\log(N/\mathrm{df})\). Ты считаешь удвоения: сколько раз надо удвоить единицу, чтобы дойти до \(n\). В 1972-м это было дешевле логарифма — и точнее отражало Ципфа. No \(\log(N/\mathrm{df})\) anywhere. You count doublings: how many times you must double 1 to reach \(n\). In 1972 that was cheaper than a logarithm — and truer to Zipf.

Её же реплика 2004 года: «предложение об idf не было помещено ни в какие теоретические рамки»; формулу подсказал Роджер Нидем, и идее понадобилось около 25 лет, чтобы дойти до реального мира. Her own numbers reproduce to the digit and sit within half a bit of the smooth logarithm. Her own remark, 2004: “the idf proposal was not set within any specific theoretical framework”; Roger Needham suggested the formula, and the idea took some twenty-five years to reach the real world.

The single best slide of the search act, because it dissolves a myth by reading the source. Spärck Jones 1972 defines f(n)=m such that 2^{m−1} < n ≤ 2^m and weights a term by f(N)−f(n)+1 — an INTEGER bucketed logarithm, not −log p. Her Cranfield examples (N=200): a term in 90 documents → weight 2, in 3 documents → 7; a document matching terms of frequency 15 and 43 scores 5+3=8, one matching 3 and 7 scores 7+6=13. All six recompute exactly, and they track log2(N/n)+1 within half a bit — so the modern formula is a smoothing of hers, not her formula. The bucketing was motivated by Zipf (frequencies fall geometrically), NOT by information theory, and she said so herself in 2004. Sets up the next slide's honest caveat.
