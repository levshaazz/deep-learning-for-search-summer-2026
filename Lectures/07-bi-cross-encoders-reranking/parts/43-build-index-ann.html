The index build, and the bridge to the ANN lecture. Once the corpus is prepared, the bi-encoder embeds every document and the vectors go into an approximate-nearest-neighbour index. This is what fixes the Scout's one remaining weakness — the linear sweep. A brute-force scan is order N dot products per query; ANN brings it toward log N. The two families: HNSW builds a navigable small-world graph, with M controlling links and memory and efSearch tuning recall against latency at query time; IVF-PQ partitions space into cells you probe a few of, and product-quantizes vectors down to about eight bytes each for billion-scale corpora. Recall versus latency is a dial — on the standard Sift benchmark, eighty percent recall costs about a millisecond and full recall about fifty. Lecture 9 is the whole story.
