The unglamorous step that decides everything downstream. First clean and deduplicate — near-duplicate documents waste the shortlist and crowd the top of the ranking. Then chunk: documents longer than the encoder's token limit must be split, typically into five-hundred-to-thousand-token pieces with ten-to-twenty-percent overlap so a key sentence is never severed at a boundary. Finally, document expansion: docTTTTTquery uses a T5 to generate the queries a passage would answer and appends them before indexing, which lifts MS MARCO MRR from eighteen-four to twenty-seven-seven by pushing the neural work to index time — note the eighteen-four baseline is the doc2query paper's own BM25 configuration, a slightly different setup from the tuned Anserini BM25 figure quoted on other slides, so the two baselines differ without contradiction. Retrieval quality is capped here — garbage in, garbage in the index.
