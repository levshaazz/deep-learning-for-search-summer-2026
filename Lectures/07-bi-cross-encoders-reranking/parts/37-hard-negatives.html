Трудные негативы — и ловушка «твинов»Hard negatives — and the “twin” trap

случайные → BM25 → ANCE → денойзинг (RocketQA), MRR@10random → BM25 → ANCE → denoised (RocketQA), MRR@10

Лучше негативы — лучше Разведчик: случайные → BM25-негативы (DPR) → глобальные из ANN-индекса (ANCE) → кросс-батч + денойзинг (RocketQA). Но «сырые» трудные негативы прячут ложные (на деле релевантные) и роняют MRR с \(32.4\) до \(26.0\); кросс-энкодер их отсеивает → \(36.4\).Better negatives, better Scout: random → BM25 negatives (DPR) → global ANN-index negatives (ANCE) → cross-batch + denoising (RocketQA). But raw hard negatives hide false ones (actually relevant) and drop MRR from \(32.4\) to \(26.0\); a cross-encoder filters them → \(36.4\).

The whole history of dense retrieval is a history of better negatives. Random and in-batch negatives are easy; DPR added one BM25 hard negative; ANCE mined global hard negatives from an asynchronously refreshed index of the entire corpus; RocketQA pushed cross-batch negatives and, crucially, denoised them. The bar chart is RocketQA's own ablation. Cross-batch lifts MRR slightly over in-batch. But naive hard negatives — the top retrieved documents — secretly contain false negatives that are actually relevant, and training on them regresses MRR by six points, from thirty-two four down to twenty-six. Using a cross-encoder to filter those out turns the same hard negatives into a gain, up to thirty-six four. The lesson: a hard negative that is really a twin of the answer poisons training.
