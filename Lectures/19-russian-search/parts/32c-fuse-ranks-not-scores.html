Для русского разница не педантская: лемматизация из акта 2 склеивает словоформы в один терм — едут \(df\), idf и вся шкала BM25, а плотное плечо остаётся прежним. Значит \(\alpha\), подобранная до смены нормализатора, после неё неверна. Ранги ту же замену переживают без правок — с них и начинай. For Russian this is not pedantry: act 2’s lemmatization collapses surface forms into one term, so \(df\), idf and the whole BM25 scale move — while the dense arm stays as it was. An \(\alpha\) tuned before that swap is wrong after it. Ranks survive the same swap untouched — start there.

The act recommends the hybrid on three slides and never once shows how the two arms are actually combined — this is that slide, and it also pays off 31c's correction (MIRACL fuses SCORES; RRF from L12 fuses RANKS; they are not the same recipe). Left: a weighted sum of rescaled scores, which needs the two scales made comparable and a weight α fitted. Right: Reciprocal Rank Fusion, a sum of 1/(k + rank) over the arms, which reads only positions. The Russian-specific point is the one to land: this deck spends a whole act changing the lexical arm's text normalization, and every such change moves document frequencies, hence idf, hence the magnitude of BM25 scores — while leaving the dense arm's cosine untouched. So a fitted α silently expires whenever the normalizer changes, whereas a rank fusion is invariant under any monotone rescoring of either arm. Practical order: start with RRF, and reach for score fusion only when you have a Russian evaluation set to re-fit and re-check α on. Note also that no Russian RRF measurement exists yet (31c) — that empty cell is still the assignment.
