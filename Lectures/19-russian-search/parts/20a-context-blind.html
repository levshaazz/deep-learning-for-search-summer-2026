On one shared test set PyMorphy2 reaches 91.23 % lemma accuracy while an oracle that picks the best parse out of its own list reaches 94.08 %. That three-point gap is exactly what disambiguation is worth — and exactly what a context-free lemmatizer leaves on the table.

For BM25, usually nothing: three points of parse accuracy barely show up in ranking. But if your index is full of homonymous entities (том, банка, коса, ключ), either move to a context-aware parser (Stanza, spaCy) or keep a keyword field for them.
The evidence behind slide 16's corrected cell. pymorphy2/pymorphy3 are context-free by construction: parse() returns a ranked list of analyses for an isolated token, and taking [0] is a prior, not a disambiguation. Live examples: том → тот at 0.896 with the book-volume reading third at 0.006; стали → стать at 0.975 with сталь at 0.011. The cost is measurable rather than rhetorical — Rubic2's 2025 comparison on one shared test set puts PyMorphy2 at 91.23 % top-1 and PyMorphy2* (an oracle allowed to pick the best of its OWN parse list) at 94.08 %, so ~3 points of lemma accuracy live entirely in disambiguation. Keep the engineering conclusion proportionate: for ranking this rarely matters, but for homonymous named entities it does, and then you either pay for a context-aware parser or keep an exact-match field.
