Тот же дробящий токенизатор, который в Акте 1 был налогом, в Акте 3 работает на совпадение: BM25 требует одинаковой строки, энкодер довольствуется общим подсловом. Отсюда и вывод предыдущего слайда — лемматизатор плотному поиску не нужен.The very tokenizer that was a tax in Act 1 works for the match in Act 3: BM25 demands an identical string, the encoder settles for a shared sub-word. Hence the previous slide’s conclusion — dense retrieval needs no lemmatizer.

The formal statement of the previous slide's first mechanism, and the act-to-act hinge of the whole lecture. BM25 sums over t ∈ q ∩ d, so when the query says "котята" and the document says "котёнок" the intersection is empty and the score is exactly zero — that was Act 2's worked example. A sub-word tokenizer maps both forms onto overlapping piece sets, so |sub(q) ∩ sub(d)| > 0 even while |q ∩ d| = 0, and the encoder has something to build a shared representation from. Note the reversal: the finer the fragmentation, the higher the token tax (Act 1) but also the likelier the sub-word overlap (Act 3). The same property, two signs. This is why "dense retrieval needs no lemmatizer" is a mechanism, not a slogan.
