Честный выигрыш — \(59{,}04 \rightarrow 68{,}19\). Разница \(68{,}19\) против \(71{,}34\) — не шум и не версия: это 3,15 пункта трансдукции, то есть подгонки на тех самых предложениях, которые потом оцениваются. В проде такого корпуса у тебя нет. А строка SICK-R говорит, что даже честный выигрыш не универсален.The honest gain is \(59.04 \rightarrow 68.19\). The gap between \(68.19\) and \(71.34\) is neither noise nor a variant: it is 3.15 points of transduction — fitting on the very sentences you will then be scored on. In production you do not have that corpus. And the SICK-R row says even the honest gain is not universal.

Su 2021 BERT-whitening, reported STS-B Spearman ρ×100 — REWRITTEN after the source check (plan §1.0 F0-c/F9). Three corrections to what this slide used to say. (1) 59.04 is NOT "raw BERT": it is BERTbase-first-last-avg, i.e. a pooling choice already made; the truly raw BERT-base STS-B is 47.29 (Li et al. 2020). (2) 71.34 is the "(target)" row — μ and W are fitted on the train+dev+TEST sentences, which is TRANSDUCTIVE. The inductive number, fitted on NLI, is 68.19 — 3.15 points lower, and it is the one a practitioner will actually get. This is the first place in the course where the transductive/inductive distinction is introduced. (3) Whitening HURTS SICK-R in the same table: 63.75 → 60.6, and on an already fine-tuned SBERT it loses in 4 of 7 columns. The old "whitening-256 ≈ same, shorter" row is gone — it was the only cell in the deck with prose where a number belongs. All values reported (Su 2021 Table 1 / Li 2020), from data/l18-bench.json.
