Откуда конус: редкие слова уезжают в общий угол — и LayerNorm их там оставляет Where the cone comes from: rare words drift to one corner, and LayerNorm leaves them there

Gao J. 2019 · «representation degeneration problem» · механизм + почему почти неизбежно Gao J. 2019 · the "representation degeneration problem" · the mechanism, and why it is near-unavoidable
механизм · обучение по правдоподобию mechanism · likelihood training
Softmax-обучение почти не встречает редкие слова. Их выходные векторы толкаются в одну общую сторону, лишь бы дать им низкую вероятность везде. Softmax training rarely sees rare words. Their output vectors get pushed in one shared direction, just to keep their probability low everywhere.

Редкие токены слипаются в общий угол — так рождается направление конуса. А LayerNorm это не чинит. Он вычитает среднее по координатам вектора и стирает ровно ту долю сдвига, которая смотрит вдоль \(\mathbf{1}_d\): \(\langle \mathrm{LN}(x),\ \mathbf{1}_d\rangle = 0\) тождественно. Но настоящее \(\mu\) вдоль \(\mathbf{1}_d\) не лежит — остаток проходит нормировку насквозь, а сдвиг \(\beta\) возвращает общее смещение. reported (Gao J. 2019). Rare tokens clump into a common corner — the cone's preferred direction is born. LayerNorm does not fix it. It subtracts the mean across each vector's own coordinates, erasing exactly the share of the shift along \(\mathbf{1}_d\): \(\langle \mathrm{LN}(x),\ \mathbf{1}_d\rangle = 0\) identically. But a real \(\mu\) does not lie along \(\mathbf{1}_d\) — the rest passes through untouched, and the learned shift \(\beta\) puts a shared offset back. reported (Gao J. 2019).

Вот зачем была оговорка к \(x = c\,\mathbf{1}_d + \varepsilon\): будь ось буквально \(c\,\mathbf{1}_d\), LayerNorm убил бы её в одну строку. Лечит всё равно вычитание среднего по облаку, а не по координатам (Часть 02). Но это не единственное объяснение: следующий слайд. That is what the caveat on \(x = c\,\mathbf{1}_d + \varepsilon\) was for: if the axis literally were \(c\,\mathbf{1}_d\), LayerNorm would kill it in one line. The cure is still subtracting the mean over the cloud, not over the coordinates (Part 02). But this is not the only explanation: next slide.

Gao 2019 ("Representation Degeneration Problem in Training Natural Language Generation Models"), both halves merged (plan N2, the 11+12 salami pair). Mechanism: likelihood/softmax training rarely updates rare words, so the gradient pushes their output embeddings in one shared direction to keep their probability low on frequent contexts; rare tokens clump into a common corner, which is the cone's preferred direction. Why near-inevitable, stated precisely after the late review §2: LayerNorm subtracts the mean across each vector's OWN coordinates, so it annihilates exactly the component along 1_d — ⟨LN(x), 1_d⟩ = 0 identically. The cone survives only because a real transformer's μ is NOT proportional to 1_d (the component of μ orthogonal to 1_d passes straight through) and because the learned affine shift β re-injects a shared offset afterwards. Never say "the shared shift survives LayerNorm" without that qualifier while the toy model on 09c writes the axis as c·1_d — the two statements contradict each other, and the toy is the one that is a simplification. Because the cause is a shared shift, mean-subtraction is the first cure (Part 02). The NEXT slide is the important qualifier: the frequency story is contested, and the competing explanation is both simpler and better measured.
