Это тот же CSLS, поделённый на два, — порядок выдачи он не меняет. Прочти строку справа налево: \(W\) правит, что сравнивают, \(r_K\) — как сравнивают. Отсюда и порядок работ: сначала перепроецируй, потом мерь плотность — плотности сырого пространства уже не те. Обнули любое из двух слагаемых — и вернётся ровно один из двух отказов. This is CSLS divided by two — same ranking. Read the line right to left: \(W\) fixes what is compared, \(r_K\) fixes how it is compared. Hence the order of work: re-project first, measure density after — the raw space’s densities are the wrong ones. Zero out either term and exactly one of the two failures returns.

The complementarity was asserted on the previous slide; this is the algebra behind it. Write the corrected score as one line: CSLS (halved, which leaves the ranking untouched) evaluated on whitened vectors. The two repairs enter at different arguments. Whitening is a single linear map applied to every vector independently of its neighbours, so it can be baked in at index time and it changes WHAT gets compared. The crowding term is a function of a point's neighbourhood, computed at lookup, and it changes HOW the comparison is normalised — no fixed linear map of the space can reproduce a per-point neighbourhood offset, which is precisely why one move cannot substitute for the other. Two operational consequences worth saying aloud: the order matters (re-project, then measure local density — densities computed in the raw space belong to a space you are no longer searching), and dropping either term reproduces exactly one of the act's two failure modes: the collapsed score scale, or the hub-dominated neighbour graph.
