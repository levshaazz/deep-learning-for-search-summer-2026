C. \(\mathrm{CSLS}_d = 2d - \bar{r}(q) - \bar{r}(y)\), lower is better: \(0.8979\) for the hub against \(-0.4631\) for #58. The key distractor is D: the two distortions are independent, and a map can be flattened and still crowded. \(N_k\) measures centrality, never importance — which is why B misses too.

Correct answer C: CSLS_d = 2d − r̄(q) − r̄(y) taxes each candidate by its own neighbourhood density — r̄(#18) = 3.6884 against r̄(#58) = 5.5713 — so the hub's 0.2609 of distance advantage — doubled by the formula's 2·d term to 0.5218 — is outweighed by 1.8829 of density penalty, leaving 1.3611.
