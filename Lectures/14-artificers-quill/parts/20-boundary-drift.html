Boundary #1 · hallucination drift

the Lexical Gremlin laughing · a confident answer to the wrong question
The tempting belief

“A hypothetical answer can only help — and generating more of them can only make retrieval safer.”

A wrong-domain hypothetical retrieves a confidently-wrong neighbourhood. If the quill misreads \(q\) and drafts about the wrong topic, \(e\) lands near fluent-but-irrelevant docs — and they look like a great match.

Why more drafts don’t save you. Averaging cancels independent noise, but a systematic bias is shared across all \(k\) drafts — so averaging amplifies it, pulling \(e\) further into the wrong region, not back toward gold. The optional \(+f(q)\) anchor only softens this; it does not cure it.

Boundary #1 — hallucination drift (the Gremlin laughing): a wrong-domain hypothetical retrieves a confidently-wrong neighbourhood of fluent-but-irrelevant docs. Crucially, generating more drafts does NOT help when the error is systematic — a shared bias survives averaging and is amplified, dragging e further from gold. The +f(q) anchor softens but does not cure it.
