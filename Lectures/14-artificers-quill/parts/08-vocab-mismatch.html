Definition · vocabulary mismatch
A relevant document may share few (or no) query terms — and even the words it does share can match an off-topic passage more strongly.

Formally, relevance lives in meaning, but the score reads surface form — a thin, generic overlap can lose to a thicker off-topic one:

$$ q = \{\text{espresso, sour, watery, taste}\} $$ $$ q \cap d_{\text{gold}} = \{\text{sour}\} \;\subsetneq\; q \cap d_{\text{trap}} = \{\text{sour, taste}\} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{trap outranks gold} $$

where q = the query terms, dgold = the truly-relevant passage, dtrap = an off-topic passage that shares more surface words, ∩ = shared terms. The gold shares only the generic word sour; the trap shares sour and taste, so raw surface overlap ranks the trap above the gold.

Vocabulary mismatch, formally: a relevant doc may share few or no query terms, and colloquial phrasing under-matches even dense encoders. On the toy q∩d_gold={sour} — one generic word — while the trap d4 shares {sour, taste}, so the trap (cos 0.45) outranks the gold d1 (cos 0.22): a thin overlap loses to a thicker off-topic one.
