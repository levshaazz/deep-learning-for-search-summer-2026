The shift · where new vocabulary comes from
Classical PRF draws terms from the corpus’s top-k. An LLM draws them from world knowledge.
  • ✓Ask an LLM about a sour, watery shot and it knows the term is under-extraction — a word no top-k passage handed us.
  • ·It can invent the bridging vocabulary the query lacked, not just re-copy.
The bridge · GAR (Mao 2021)
Generation-Augmented Retrieval: generate context from a model, then append it to the query.
$$ q' = q \;\Vert\; \text{LLM-generated context} $$

where q′ = the expanded query sent to the retriever, q = the original query (kept, not replaced), ‖ = string concatenation.

GAR is the antecedent of the expansion branch (→ Query2doc), not of HyDE — it keeps the original query and adds to it.

The LLM turn: unlike PRF (terms from the corpus top-k), an LLM draws vocabulary from world knowledge — it can INVENT the missing word "under-extraction". GAR (Mao 2021) is the bridge: generate context and APPEND it to the query (q' = q ‖ generated). GAR is the antecedent of the expansion branch (→ Query2doc), NOT of HyDE, which replaces rather than appends.
