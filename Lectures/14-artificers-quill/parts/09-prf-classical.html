The classical cure: assume the top-k are right, fold their words back

two sibling families of pseudo-relevance feedback — not a lineage
Family A · vector-space (Rocchio)
Move the query vector toward the centroid of the top docs, away from the non-relevant.
$$ \vec{q}\,{}' = \alpha\,\vec{q} + \beta\,\overline{\vec{d}}_{\text{rel}} - \gamma\,\overline{\vec{d}}_{\text{non}} $$

where q′ = the reweighted query, q = the original query, d̄rel = centroid of the top (assumed-relevant) docs, d̄non = centroid of the non-relevant docs, α,β,γ = weights trading off the three pulls.

A geometric nudge of the query centroid.

Family B · language-model (RM → RM3)
Build a relevance LM from the top docs, then interpolate it with the original query LM.
$$ P_{\text{RM3}}(w) = (1-\lambda)\,P(w\mid q) + \lambda\,P_{\text{RM}}(w) $$

where w = a candidate word, P(w∣q) = its probability under the original query model, PRM(w) = its probability under the relevance model built from the top docs, λ = the interpolation weight (feedback trust).

\(\lambda\) sets how far to trust the feedback. Same idea, probabilistic dialect.

Shared mechanism: retrieve → assume the top-k relevant → harvest their terms → re-query. Parallel siblings, not ancestor-and-heir.

Pseudo-relevance feedback = the pre-LLM cure for vocabulary mismatch, in TWO sibling families (parallel, not a lineage): vector-space Rocchio (move the query centroid toward the top-doc centroid) and language-model RM→RM3 (interpolate the query LM with a relevance LM at weight λ).
