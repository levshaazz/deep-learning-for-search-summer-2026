Схема считает компромиссThe circuit counts the trade-off

шаг (←/→): разведчик → судья → позднее взаимодействие — считай прямоугольники кодировщикаstep (←/→): Scout → Judge → late interaction — count the encoder boxes

Слайд 30 сказал, что судье кэшировать нечего — схема это показывает: на шаге 1 серые предвычисленные векторы исчезают с карточек, остаётся пустой пунктирный слот. И компромисс становится счётным: посчитай прямоугольники кодировщика — у разведчика один (на запросе), у судьи N, по одному на каждый документ. Шаг 2 — анонс L12: стягивание можно отложить.Slide 30 said the Judge has nothing to cache — the circuit shows it: at step 1 the grey pre-computed vectors vanish from the cards, leaving an empty dashed slot. And the trade-off becomes countable: count the encoder boxes — one for the Scout (on the query), N for the Judge, one per document. Step 2 is L12’s trailer: the contraction can be made late.

The notation from Lecture 7, put to work on the argument this whole act has been making. Three steps, one corpus of four documents, and the retrievers differ in exactly one thing: what runs per document. Step zero, the Scout. The encoder box fires once — on the query — and every document card carries a row of grey cells, the vectors we computed offline. Inside the corpus region, per document, there is only a cup: a cheap contraction, q dot d i. Count the encoder boxes: one. Step one, the Judge, and the lesson is what DISAPPEARS. The grey vectors are gone from every card; where the cached vector used to sit there is now an empty dashed slot. That absence is the whole of slide thirty: a cross-encoder reads the query and the document together, so the document half cannot be encoded before the query arrives, and there is nothing to precompute. In its place, a full encoder box on every row. Count them: N of them, one per document. The ranking is sharper — look at the gap between the gold documents and the borscht recipe — and that sharpness costs N BERT passes per query, which on a real corpus you simply cannot pay. That is the accuracy-versus-cost trade-off, and it is now a counting exercise, not a claim: the price of the accuracy is the number of orange boxes, and the price of those boxes is the empty slots. Step two is a trailer for Lecture 12, so do not dwell: the vectors come back, the encoder count drops to one again, and the contraction moves to the very end. That middle ground has a name — ColBERT — and it is where the next lecture starts.
