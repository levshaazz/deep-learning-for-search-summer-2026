Почему офлайн окупается — и когда перестаётWhy offline pays off — and when it stops

подели счёт на запросы: у сборки есть делитель, у запроса — нетdivide the bill by queries: the build has a divisor, the query has none
$$ \bar{c} \;=\; \frac{C_{\mathrm{build}}}{Q} \;+\; c_{\mathrm{query}} $$

где Cbuild — весь офлайн-счёт (чистка, чанкование, эмбеддинг корпуса, индекс), cquery — работа на один запрос, Q — сколько запросов проходит между двумя пересборками, а не за всю жизнь системы.where Cbuild is the whole offline bill (clean, chunk, embed the corpus, build the index), cquery the work per query, and Q the queries served between two rebuilds — not over the system's life.

За черту уносится ровно то, что не зависит от запроса: вектор документа один на все запросы — считаем заранее; оценка пары своя на каждый запрос — предпосчитать нечего. Оговорка: делится счёт, но не память — индекс занимает место всё время, пока лежит.Only query-independent work crosses the line: a document vector is the same for every query — compute it in advance; a pair's score is fresh per query — nothing to precompute. Caveat: the bill divides, memory does not; the index holds space the whole time it sits.

The mechanism under this act's central promise — "done once, it pays off on every query" — which the act asserts three times and never derives. Average cost per query is the build bill divided by Q plus the per-query cost. Two consequences the act uses silently. First, only the build term has a divisor, so it vanishes as traffic grows, while the per-query term never does — that is the real reason the cross-encoder is confined to a shortlist and the corpus embedding is not. Second, and this is the part slide forty-four hides behind the phrase "re-index on drift": rebuilding does not increase the build bill, it shrinks Q. An index rebuilt nightly only amortises over a single day of traffic, so the same offline cost can be cheap or ruinous depending purely on how fast the corpus drifts. And the criterion for what may cross the line at all is query-independence — the same criterion slide thirty gave for why cross-encoder scores cannot be cached. Deliberately symbolic: the crossover is corpus- and traffic-specific, so no numbers here. Honest limit to state aloud: this divides cost, not memory — a resident index costs RAM every second regardless of Q.
