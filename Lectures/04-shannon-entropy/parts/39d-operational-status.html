Тонкая мысль, которой в лекции про энтропию обычно нет. Среднее — \(H\) — куплено теоремой о кодировании источника: есть код, есть предел, есть проверяемое обещание. Слагаемое — \(-\log p\) отдельного исхода — куплено только тем, что оно красиво усредняется. IDF — это слагаемое. Поэтому мост из прошлого слайда — аналогия: он берёт величину без операционного статуса и вешает на неё ранжирование. Работает — но не потому, что Шеннон это доказал. A subtle point usually missing from an entropy lecture. The average — \(H\) — is bought by the source coding theorem: there is a code, a limit, a checkable promise. The summand — one outcome's \(-\log p\) — is bought only by the fact that it averages nicely. IDF is a summand. That is why the previous slide's bridge is an analogy: it takes a quantity with no operational status and hangs ranking on it. It works — but not because Shannon proved it.

Robertson 2004's appendix, reproduced to the digit: a binary source with p = (0.89, 0.11) has H = 0.4999 bit — an operational fact, because the source coding theorem says the stream compresses to half its length and you can go and check. Its pointwise self-informations 0.1681 and 3.1844 bits are, in Robertson's reading, numbers behind which no theorem stands; he quotes Gray (1990): "Information measures are important because coding theorems exist imbuing them with operational significance and not because of intuitively pleasing aspects of their definitions." This is the deepest slide of the act and the honest close to the IDF bridge: averages are theorems, summands are intuitions. Students who internalise this stop over-claiming from information theory for the rest of their careers.
