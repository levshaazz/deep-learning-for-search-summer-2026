Вот почему числа — границы, а не измерение: доля попаданий с первой попытки \(q_1 = 0{,}80\) даёт лишь одну точку распределения, а весь хвост \(q_2, q_3, \dots\) решает остальное. При 100 буквах контекста Шеннон получает 0,6 ≤ \(F_{100}\) ≤ 1,3 бита. Оговорка — его собственная: нижняя граница доказана для идеального предсказателя, а частоты сняты с человека, поэтому её строгость условна. This is why the numbers are bounds, not a measurement: the first-try hit rate \(q_1 = 0.80\) is one point of a distribution, and the tail \(q_2, q_3, \dots\) decides the rest. At 100 letters of context Shannon gets 0.6 ≤ \(F_{100}\) ≤ 1.3 bits. The caveat is his own: the lower bound is proved for an ideal predictor, while the frequencies came from a human, so its rigour is conditional.

The missing payoff of the guessing act — the deck reported 0.6–1.3 with no mechanism. Shannon's formula (17): Σ_r r(q_r − q_{r+1}) log r ≤ F_N ≤ −Σ_r q_r log q_r, where q_r is the fraction of letters first guessed correctly on attempt r. The upper bound is simply the entropy of the rank stream (you can always code the ranks); the lower bound uses the monotonicity a rational guesser must satisfy. Say aloud that Shannon himself flags the asymmetry: the lower bound is proved for an IDEAL predictor while the frequencies came from a human.
