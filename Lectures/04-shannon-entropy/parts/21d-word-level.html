A known slip in Shannon 1951; the correction is Grignetti 1964: ≈ 9.8 bits per word. We print 11.82 only because 2.62 and 2.14 are derived from it. And a second caveat: calling this \(F_{4.5}\) is tempting, but a word model behaves more like \(F_5\)–\(F_6\); the divisor has a trap too — Shannon's word is 4.5 letters (without the space), Jurafsky & Martin's is 5.5 (with it).
