Дай всем словам длину \(-\log_2 p_i\) — и \(\sum 2^{\log_2 p_i} = \sum p_i = 1\): полка заполнена ровно. Сделай хоть одно короче — сумма перевалит за 1, и беспрефиксного кода не существует. Отсюда \(\bar L \ge H\): не «трудно», а нельзя. Cover & Thomas §5.2. Give every codeword length \(-\log_2 p_i\) and \(\sum 2^{\log_2 p_i} = \sum p_i = 1\): the shelf is exactly full. Make one shorter and the sum passes 1, so no prefix-free code exists. Hence \(\bar L \ge H\): not “hard” but impossible. Cover & Thomas §5.2.

Closes gap G3. The deck asserted H ≤ L̄ < H+1 with no hint where it comes from. Kraft: a prefix-free code with lengths l_i exists IFF Σ 2^{−l_i} ≤ 1. The shelf metaphor makes it physical — a 1-bit word occupies half the shelf, a 2-bit word a quarter — so short codewords are literally expensive in shelf space. Then the floor is one line: the ideal lengths −log2 p_i fill the shelf EXACTLY (Σ 2^{log2 p_i} = Σ p_i = 1), so nothing can be systematically shorter.
