шаг (←/→): собственная информация → вклады → кривая \(H(p)\) → наша монета → неверная модельstep (←/→): self-information → the contributions → the \(H(p)\) curve → our coin → a wrong model

Редкое событие бьёт по стрелке сильнее — но вносит в среднее мало, потому что редко. Максимум кривой \(H(p)\) стоит ровно на \(p=0{,}5\): честная монета — самый неудобный источник на свете. Наша смещённая монета \(p=0{,}25\) даёт \(H=0{,}8113\), а неверная модель \(q=0{,}5\) поднимает плату до 1,0 бита — этот зазор мы в акте 5 назовём KL.A rare event throws the needle further — yet contributes little to the average, precisely because it is rare. The peak of \(H(p)\) sits exactly at \(p=0.5\): a fair coin is the most inconvenient source there is. Our biased coin at \(p=0.25\) gives \(H=0.8113\), while the wrong model \(q=0.5\) raises the bill to 1.0 bit — a gap act 5 will name KL.

Act 2's climb. The widget reads data/l17-entropy.json (coin + coinCurve) — nothing is computed in the browser. Steps: (0) self-information bars −log2 p and −log2(1−p); (1) weight them by p and 1−p, so the rare outcome's contribution shrinks; (2) the sum is H(p), the curve appears, maximum 1 bit at p=0.5; (3) lock p=0.25 → H=0.8113, below one bit, so the skew is exploitable; (4) overlay the wrong model q=0.5 → H(p,q)=1.0 and hatch the KL gap 0.1887. Step 4 is a deliberate forward-reference: the students meet the picture of the wrong-model tax before its name.
