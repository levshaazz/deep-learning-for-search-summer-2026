Почти никто из них не изобретает новую математику соседства заново. Внутри (у закрытых проприетарных движков — по их же словам, проверить нельзя) — HNSW, IVF и PQ, разобранные в трёх предыдущих актах; строка index-factory с предыдущего слайда описывает ровно то же самое. Различаются они не структурами, а эксплуатацией: кто хранит, кто фильтрует, кто дежурит.Almost none of them reinvents nearest-neighbour mathematics. Inside (for the closed proprietary ones — on their word; you cannot check) sit HNSW, IVF and PQ — the three lanes you just walked; the index-factory string on the previous slide describes the very same objects. They differ in operations, not in structures: who stores, who filters, who is on call.

Vector DB landscape, three families. (1) Dedicated engines — Qdrant, Milvus, Weaviate: filtering, sharding, replication and an API out of the box, at the cost of one more system to operate. (2) Extensions of a store you already run — pgvector inside PostgreSQL, kNN inside Elasticsearch/OpenSearch: vectors next to the data, one backup (and inside Postgres, one transaction), but the index competes with the primary workload and exposes fewer dials. (3) Managed service — Pinecone: you operate nothing, you pay a bill and give up control. The point of the slide: all of them are wrappers around the HNSW / IVF / PQ structures of the three previous acts. The choice is an operations question, not a mathematics question — which is exactly what the Catch slide at the end of this insert argues. No gated numbers here on purpose: any QPS or scale figure would be vendor- and hardware-specific.
