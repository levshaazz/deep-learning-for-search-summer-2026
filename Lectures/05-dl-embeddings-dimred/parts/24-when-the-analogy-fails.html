Впечатляюще, но не «всегда».The best 300-d word2vec/GloVe models reach \(\sim\!75\%\) on Google's semantic set (Mikolov et al. 2013); the 50-d vectors we demo here score well below that. Impressive, not infallible.

The honest counterpoint to the headline result. Students see king minus man plus woman land on queen and conclude the parallelogram is universal. It is not — it is a linear regularity, so it breaks wherever the relation is not linear or the vectors are poorly estimated. Four failure modes. One: rare words have few contexts and therefore noisy vectors, so a capital analogy on an obscure country often misses. Two: polysemy — apple is one vector blending the fruit and the company, so a clean brand direction does not exist. Three: genuinely non-linear relations like comparatives, superlatives, and morphology do not lie along a single offset vector. Four: the practical leak — if you forget to exclude the three input words, the nearest vector is frequently one of them, usually king itself, and the arithmetic appears to do nothing. Reported accuracy on the standard Google analogy set is around seventy-five percent for semantics — impressive evidence of structure, not proof of understanding.
