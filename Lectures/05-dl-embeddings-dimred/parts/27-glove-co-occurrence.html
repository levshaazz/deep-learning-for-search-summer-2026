Глобальный счёт вместо локального предсказания — а карта получается та же.GloVe builds the matrix \(X\) (how often \(i\) co-occurs with \(j\) across the corpus) and fits vectors so dot products approximate \(\log X_{ij}\). Global counts instead of local prediction — yet the same map.

GloVe, from Pennington, Socher and Manning in 2014, takes the count road. First build the global co-occurrence matrix X, whose entry i j is how often word i appears near word j across the whole corpus. Then learn vectors whose dot product, plus two bias terms, equals the log of that count. It is a weighted least-squares factorisation of the log co-occurrence matrix, fitted in one global pass rather than streaming windows. The striking result is that this count-based method lands in essentially the same geometry as predictive word2vec — analogies and cosine nearness work the same way. The map is robust to how you draw it.
