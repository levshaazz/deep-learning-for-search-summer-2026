Вред: модель в найме/поиске тиражирует и усиливает гендерные/расовые шаблоны. Дебиасинг: оценить «гендерное направление» \(g\) (напр. \(v_{\text{she}}-v_{\text{he}}\)) и спроецировать его прочь из нейтральных слов: \(\;v \leftarrow v - (v^{\top}\hat{g})\,\hat{g}\). Помогает, но не стирает скрытую предвзятость (Gonen & Goldberg, 2019).Harm: a hiring or search model trained on this reproduces and amplifies gender/race patterns. Debiasing: estimate a “gender direction” \(g\) (e.g. \(v_{\text{she}}-v_{\text{he}}\)) and project it out of neutral words: \(\;v \leftarrow v - (v^{\top}\hat{g})\,\hat{g}\). It helps but does not erase residual bias (Gonen & Goldberg, 2019).

An ethics beat that follows naturally from the analogy. The very linear structure we celebrated is value-neutral about what relations it encodes — so a corpus full of human stereotypes produces an embedding full of them. Bolukbasi and colleagues in 2016 showed the same arithmetic that maps king to queen also completes man is to computer programmer as woman is to homemaker. The harm is concrete: any downstream hiring, ranking, or search model built on these vectors will reproduce and even amplify those patterns. One mitigation is geometric: estimate a gender direction g — for instance the difference between she and he — normalise it, and for every word that should be gender-neutral subtract its projection onto g, v minus v-dot-g-hat times g-hat. This visibly removes the obvious analogies, but Gonen and Goldberg in 2019 showed the bias largely survives in the surrounding geometry, so debiasing is a partial fix, not a solution. The lesson: a map of meaning is also a map of whatever was in the text.
