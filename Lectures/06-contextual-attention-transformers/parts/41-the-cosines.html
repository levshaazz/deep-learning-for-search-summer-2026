InfoNCE as an optimization, shown end to end, with the final state pinned to the data file's exact number. Step zero, the setup: the anchor cat sits at the centre of an embedding plane; the positive kitten and the three negatives — airplane, computer, france — orbit it, with distance equal to one minus cosine, so closer means more similar; a dashed green ring marks the close zone, cosine above zero. Step one, untuned: the catch — the negative airplane has crept closer to the anchor than the positive kitten, so the softmax over candidates bets ninety-six percent on the negative, the positive gets only three-point-seven percent, and the InfoNCE loss, minus log of that, is a large three-point-three-one. Step two, mid-training: the gradient pulls the positive inward and pushes the negatives outward; the positive's probability climbs to forty-three percent and the loss falls to zero-point-eight-six. Step three, tuned: the positive is now the closest point, cosine zero-point-six-four — exactly the deck's cosines, kitten point-six-four, airplane point-three-seven, computer point-three-five, france point-zero-nine — the softmax concentrates p-plus at zero-point-eight-eight-seven-seven, and the loss is minus log of that, zero-point-one-one-nine-one. The loss curve on the right tells the whole story: three-point-three-one, down to zero-point-eight-six, down to zero-point-one-two.
