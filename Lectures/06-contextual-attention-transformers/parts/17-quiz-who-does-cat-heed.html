B. softmax монотонна: наибольший скор → наибольший вес. Деление на \(\sqrt{d_k}\) меняет резкость, но не порядок. \([0,4,2]/2=[0,2,1]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{softmax}}[0.09,\,0.665,\,0.245]\) — cat кладёт \(0.665\) на себя. (D неверно: softmax сохраняет порядок, а не уравнивает.)softmax is monotone: the largest score gives the largest weight, and scaling by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) changes sharpness, not the order. \([0,4,2]/2=[0,2,1]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{softmax}}[0.09,\,0.665,\,0.245]\) — cat puts \(0.665\) on itself. (D is wrong: softmax preserves order, it does not equalise.)
A forced prediction before the reveal, to make the next slide land. Students already saw cat's score row, zero four two, on the previous slide. Ask them to commit: after scaling and softmax, which token wins the weight? The answer is cat itself, option B, because softmax is monotone — the biggest score becomes the biggest weight, and dividing by root d k only changes how peaked the distribution is, never the ordering. The distractor D is the common misconception that softmax flattens or equalises; it does not, it sharpens.
