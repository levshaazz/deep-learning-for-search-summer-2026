Вместо одного большого блока внимания — \(h\) независимых голов, каждая со своими \(W_Q^{(i)},W_K^{(i)},W_V^{(i)}\) в подпространстве размерности \(d/h\). Каждая голова учит своё отношение: одна — синтаксис, другая — кореференцию.Instead of one big attention — \(h\) independent heads, each with its own \(W_Q^{(i)},W_K^{(i)},W_V^{(i)}\) in a \(d/h\)-dimensional subspace. Each head learns a different relation: one syntax, another coreference.

Это и есть «совет»: каждая голова — отдельный советник со своим взглядом на то, кого слушать. У нас в данных \(h=2\) головы.This is the “council”: each head is a separate councillor with its own view of who to heed. Our data uses \(h=2\) heads.

Multi-head attention, the Council made literal. A single head computes one attention pattern, which can only capture one type of relationship. Multi-head attention splits the model dimension into h smaller subspaces and runs an independent attention head in each, with its own query, key and value projections. Because the subspaces and projections differ, the heads specialise — empirically one head may track subject-verb agreement, another may resolve pronouns, another may attend to the previous token. This is exactly our council metaphor: h councillors, each with its own opinion about who matters. Our data file uses two heads; real models use eight, twelve, sixteen.
