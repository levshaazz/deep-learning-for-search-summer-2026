Схема — это и есть einsumThe circuit IS the einsum

шаг (←/→): индекс, который есть слева от стрелки и нет справа, — стянутstep (←/→): an index that is on the left of the arrow and not on the right has been contracted

Схема не иллюстрирует einsum — она и есть einsum. Индекс, который встречается во входах и отсутствует в выходе, стянут: это чаша на схеме, \(\Sigma\) в формуле и просто буква, которую вы не написали справа от стрелки. Поэтому нотацию можно проверять, а не только рассматривать.The diagram does not illustrate the einsum — it is the einsum. An index that appears in the inputs and not in the output has been contracted: a cup in the diagram, a \(\Sigma\) in the formula, and a letter you simply did not write on the right of the arrow. That is what makes the notation checkable rather than decorative.

This is the slide that makes the notation worth its ten minutes. Three panes, one statement. Step zero: watch the letter d. In the einsum string it is on the left of the arrow twice — once in Q's n-d, once in K's m-d — and on the right it does not appear at all. That is the complete definition of a contraction, and it is exactly why the diagram has a cup there and the formula has a sigma there. The picture is not a picture of the einsum; the two are the same object written in different alphabets. Step one: the letters that survive on the right of the arrow are precisely the wires leaving the circuit, and read in order they are the output shape, n by m — so you never have to run the code to find out what came out. Step two is the exception that defines the triangle: softmax works across m but does not consume it, n by m goes in and n by m comes out, no letter vanishes, so there is no cup. Step three closes it: the second contraction, over m, obeys the same rule and gives the context. So attention is two contractions with a softmax wedged between them — and you can prove that from the subscript strings alone, without looking at a picture at all. That is checkability, and it is the whole reason we bothered.
