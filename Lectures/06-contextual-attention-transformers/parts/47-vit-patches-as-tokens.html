The first cross-domain transfer: vision. The Vision Transformer, from Dosovitskiy and colleagues in 2021, does something almost embarrassingly simple. Take an image, cut it into a grid of small patches — sixteen by sixteen pixels each — and flatten and linearly project each patch into a vector. Now you have a sequence of patch tokens, two hundred or so for a standard image, and you feed them through exactly the same encoder block we built today: positional encoding, multi-head attention, Add and Norm, feed-forward. A CLS token pools the whole image into a single vector, just like BERT pools a sentence. No convolutions anywhere. Given enough training data this beats the convolutional networks that dominated vision for a decade. The lesson that matters for us is the universality: the architecture did not change at all, only the tokenizer that turns the raw input into a sequence. This is the direct ancestor of the multimodal retrieval models we will meet in Lecture 12.
