$$ \begin{aligned} H &= \mathrm{LayerNorm}\big(X + \mathrm{MultiHead}(X)\big) \\[4pt] Z &= \mathrm{LayerNorm}\big(H + \mathrm{FFN}(H)\big) \end{aligned} $$

Два подслоя, оба в обёртке «остаток + LayerNorm». Первый перемешивает токены (multi-head attention), второй преобразует каждый (FFN). Выход \(Z\) той же формы \(T\times d\), что и вход — поэтому блоки складываются стопкой без изменения размерности.Two sublayers, each in the “residual + LayerNorm” wrapper. The first mixes tokens (multi-head attention), the second transforms each (FFN). The output \(Z\) has the same shape \(T\times d\) as the input — which is why blocks stack without changing dimensions.

Вход и выход блока — обе матрицы \(T\times d\). Значит выход можно подать на вход следующему такому же блоку — повторяем \(N\) раз.A block’s input and output are both \(T\times d\) matrices. So the output can feed an identical next block — repeated \(N\) times.

The block compressed to two lines, which is worth memorising. H is the layer-norm of X plus multi-head attention of X — the mixing sublayer with its residual. Z is the layer-norm of H plus the feed-forward network of H — the per-token sublayer with its residual. That is the entire encoder block. The structural point in the callout is what makes a Transformer a Transformer: input and output are both T-by-d matrices, the shape is preserved, so you can feed Z straight into an identical block and repeat N times. Mixing then transforming, wrapped twice, stacked deep — that is the whole architecture.
