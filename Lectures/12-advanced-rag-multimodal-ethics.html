Part 0 · The longest quest

The Deep Field

Some questions no single record can answer. Point a telescope at an empty patch of sky and stare long enough, and a thousand galaxies bloom out of the dark. The Ship has learned to find a passage and to answer from it — but the deepest answers are scattered across many records, in many forms. This is the deep field.

Cold open. Callback to L10/L11 (the Oracle answers, and we learned to judge it) → but the deepest questions need MANY records, in MANY modalities. The deep-field metaphor: stare long enough at the dark and structure resolves. Sets up multi-hop, multimodal, ethics — the last territory before the Defense.

A question no single record can answer

“What field did the founder of Acme Corp study?”

The corpus has both halves — apart

  • d1 — “Acme Corp was founded by Dana Reyes…” names the founder, but no field of study.
  • d2 — “Dana Reyes studied computer science at MIT…” names the field, but never mentions Acme.
  • No single document holds both the founder and the field.

The answer lives in the chain

The fact we want — computer science — exists only in the link d1 → d2, joined by the shared entity Dana Reyes. Grab one best chunk and you miss it. To answer, the Ship must reason across records, not merely find one. That is the deep field in miniature.

Hook. Anchor question for the GraphRAG act, verbatim from data/l12-graphrag.json (question, docs d1/d2, shared entity Dana Reyes, answerNode "computer science"). The answer is split across two docs — flat retrieval cannot assemble it. Sets up the single-hop-fails problem slide next.

Flat retrieval ignores chains and structure

a bag of chunks has no edges to walk

What naive RAG does

  • Embed the query, take the top-k chunks by cosine, stuff, generate — the whole loop from L10.
  • It treats the corpus as a flat bag of independent passages: no notion of which entity appears in which document.
  • For “What field did the founder study?” the single best chunk (d1) names the founder — and recall is 0: the field is one hop away in d2.

What the question needs

  • A multi-hop question is a path: company → founder → field. The corpus must be navigable, not just searchable.
  • Two answers to two failures: build a graph of entities and relations to walk (GraphRAG), and search beyond text when the record is an image (multimodal).
  • And once it can answer the deepest questions, it must answer them responsibly.
Problem. Naive top-k RAG is a flat bag of chunks — no structure, no edges. Single-hop recall 0 on the anchor question (data/l12-graphrag.json recallSingleHop). Frames the three acts: GraphRAG (reason across), multimodal (search beyond text), ethics (answer responsibly).
Part 1 · Reason across records

The constellation map

Stop treating the corpus as a flat bag of passages. Extract the entities and the relations between them and link them into a graph — suddenly the scattered records resolve into a constellation. Now an answer that no single document holds can be walked to, one hop at a time. Multi-hop reasoning · GraphRAG.

Divider · GraphRAG. The constellation metaphor: flat documents → a navigable graph of entities + relations. Edge (Edge et al., Microsoft, arXiv:2404.16130) for GraphRAG; HotpotQA (arXiv:1809.09600) for multi-hop QA. Walk the graph to reach answers no one document holds.

Multi-hop retrieval, formally

the answer is a path, not a passage
Definition · multi-hop over an entity graph
$$ \mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{E},\,\mathcal{R}), \quad \text{answer} = \text{walk}\big(e_0 \xrightarrow{r_1} \cdots \xrightarrow{r_h} e_h\big) $$
  • ·Nodes \(\mathcal{E}\) are entities, edges \(\mathcal{R}\) relations — each a triple (head, relation, tail) from a document.
  • ·\(h\) hops from start \(e_0\) to answer \(e_h\). Our anchor: \(h=2\), Acme Corp → Dana Reyes → computer science.
  • ·On top of L10's \(R_k\) — retrieval finds documents; the graph's edges chain facts across them.
Multi-hop-formally. answer = walk over graph G=(E,R); h hops. h=2 on the anchor: Acme Corp → Dana Reyes → computer science (data/l12-graphrag.json hops, path). Graph adds edges on top of L10's flat R_k. GraphRAG = Edge et al. arXiv:2404.16130. Two-col def|image for fit.

Single-hop vs multi-hop recall, by hand

binary answer-containment · answer node = computer science
$$ \text{recall} = \mathbb{1}\big[\text{answer node} \in \text{retrieved}\big] $$ $$ \text{single-hop } (\text{best doc } d_1):\ \{\text{Acme Corp},\ \text{Dana Reyes},\ \text{Portland}\} \;\not\ni\; \text{computer science} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{recall} = \mathbf{0} $$ $$ \text{2-hop walk } (d_1 \to d_2):\ \text{Acme Corp} \xrightarrow{\text{founded\_by}} \text{Dana Reyes} \xrightarrow{\text{studied}} \textbf{computer science} \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{recall} = \mathbf{1} $$

Single-hop stops at \(d_1\) and never sees the field — recall 0. The 2-hop walk crosses two documents (\(d_1 \to d_2\)) along the shared entity Dana Reyes and lands on the answer node — recall 1. The lift comes entirely from the edges: the same documents, now navigable. This is a binary answer-hit indicator on the single answer node — not L04's Recall@k (the fraction of relevant items retrieved); we reuse the word for the one-node case here.

Building the graph — the offline indexing pipeline

where the edges come from, before any query arrives
Pipeline · index-time, once per corpus
extract triples → resolve & merge entities → build the graph → detect communities & pre-summarise — all offline, so query-time is just a walk.
  • 1Extract — an LLM reads each chunk and emits (head, relation, tail) triples (our llama3.1:8b ran exactly this, returning 7 triples from 3 docs).
  • 2Resolve — merge co-referent mentions into one node, so edges from different documents actually connect (in a real corpus you’d fold “Dana”, “Dana Reyes”, “the founder” together — our clean toy already uses one surface form).
  • 3Build & cluster — assemble the graph, detect communities, and pre-summarise each (the global-search step). This is the cost the boundaries slide warned about — paid once, amortised over every query.
GraphRAG build pipeline (offline, like L10's offline/online split). Steps: extract triples (LLM, our llama3.1:8b → 7 triples), entity resolution / co-reference merge, build + community detection + pre-summarise. Index-time cost (the boundaries slide's warning), amortised. Query-time = a walk. Mirrors the offline/online framing from L10's rag-pipeline.

Multi-hop in the wild — HotpotQA

a benchmark built so that one passage is never enough
Benchmark · HotpotQA (Yang et al., EMNLP 2018)
Questions deliberately need two or more documents, with supporting facts annotated — find the bridge, not one passage.
  • ·Bridge questions — our anchor shape: an entity in doc A answers from doc B. Single-hop cannot solve them.
  • ·Explainable — labelled supporting sentences grade the evidence chain, not just the answer.
  • ·Why it matters — real questions whose answers live in the links — what GraphRAG makes walkable.
HotpotQA. data/l12-bench.json: Yang et al., "HotpotQA", EMNLP 2018, arXiv:1809.09600. Diverse, explainable multi-hop QA — questions need 2+ docs, supporting facts annotated. Bridge questions = our anchor shape. The empirical motivation for multi-hop / GraphRAG.

Two ways to query a graph: local walk vs global summary

GraphRAG clusters the graph and pre-summarises each community
Definition · local & global search (Edge et al., 2024)
Cluster the graph into communities, pre-summarise each, then route a query to a local walk or a global map-reduce.
  • ·Local search — walk the query entities' neighbourhood (our 2-hop traversal). Best for specific questions.
  • ·Global search — map: each community summary yields a partial answer plus a self-rated relevance score; reduce: drop the low-scored partials, combine the rest. That per-community LLM pass is the cost global search pays for corpus-wide “main themes?” coverage.
  • ·From Local to Global — the paper's title is the idea: the close walk and the bird's-eye view.
Community summaries / global-vs-local. data/l12-graphrag.json communitySummaryNote: GraphRAG clusters into communities, pre-summarises (global search); local search walks entities (the 2-hop walk we showed). Edge et al. "From Local to Global" (arXiv:2404.16130). Two-col def|image for fit.

When GraphRAG earns its cost — and when it doesn't

a graph is powerful, and expensive to build & keep current
Applicability · don't reach for a graph by default
GraphRAG pays off on relational, multi-hop answers — at the price of an extraction-and-indexing pipeline.
  • ✓Reach for it when — facts chain across documents (multi-hop), or corpus-wide theme questions.
  • ×Skip it when — the answer is in one passage. Naive RAG (L10) is cheaper.
  • ×Extraction is noisy — relations get named inconsistently, edges missed or hallucinated. Graph quality, not just freshness, gates GraphRAG.
  • ·The costs — corpus-wide extraction, graph storage, staleness. Start flat; earn the graph.
GraphRAG boundaries. Reach for it on relational/multi-hop and global theme questions; skip when the answer is single-passage (naive RAG cheaper). Costs: corpus-wide extraction at index time, graph storage, staleness on updates. Don't over-engineer — diagnose first (echoes L10's applicability lesson).
Part 2 · Search beyond text

Seeing, not only reading

Half the deep field is not written down. A diagram, a screenshot, a scanned invoice, a photograph — records the Ship has never been able to read. The trick is to embed pictures and words into one shared space, so a caption can find its image and an image its caption. Sir Cosine measures the angle — now across modalities. CLIP · ColPali.

Divider · multimodal. Sir Cosine (Séréga-in-knight-costume, green cap) rules the shared CLIP space — callback to L2/L6 cosine + contrastive. Embed images and text into ONE space; cross-modal retrieval is argmax cosine. CLIP (Radford et al., arXiv:2103.00020); ColPali (Faysse et al., arXiv:2407.01449).

CLIP, formally — one shared space, two encoders

cross-modal retrieval is argmax cosine
Definition · contrastive image–text pretraining (Radford et al., 2021)
$$ \text{retrieve}(\text{image}) = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{j}\ \cos\!\big(\mathbf{e}_{\text{img}},\, \mathbf{e}_{\text{txt}}^{(j)}\big) $$

where eimg = the query image's embedding, etxt(j) = candidate caption j's embedding, cos = cosine similarity in the shared space, arg maxj = the caption index that scores highest.

  • ·Two encoders, one space — an image encoder \(f_{\text{img}}\) and a text encoder \(f_{\text{txt}}\) map into the same coordinate system, so an image and a caption are comparable by cosine.
  • ·Retrieval = argmax cosine — embed both, take the nearest neighbour: the same NN search as L9 (argmax cosine), which at scale runs over L9's ANN index — now cross-modal.
  • ·One figure says it all — a cosine matrix of images vs captions; the diagonal should win every row. Let's compute it.
CLIP-formally. Two encoders into ONE shared space; cross-modal retrieval = argmax cosine over captions. Same NN search as L9, now cross-modal. Sets up the cosine-matrix climb (diagonal should win each row). CLIP = Radford et al., ICML 2021, arXiv:2103.00020.

How the shared space is learned

the contrastive objective — pull matched together, push the rest apart
$$ \mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \frac{\exp\!\big(\cos(\mathbf{e}_{\text{img}}^{(i)},\,\mathbf{e}_{\text{txt}}^{(i)})/\tau\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} \exp\!\big(\cos(\mathbf{e}_{\text{img}}^{(i)},\,\mathbf{e}_{\text{txt}}^{(j)})/\tau\big)} $$ $$ \text{maximise the diagonal } \cos(\text{img}_i,\text{txt}_i), \quad \text{minimise the off-diagonal } \cos(\text{img}_i,\text{txt}_{j\neq i}) $$

For a batch of \(N\) image–caption pairs, the loss is a softmax over the row of the cosine matrix: the correct caption (the diagonal cell) should win. This is the same family as L6's in-batch contrastive loss — the off-diagonal pairs are the negatives. CLIP additionally symmetrises, averaging this row-wise (image→text) loss with the matching column-wise (text→image) loss — the symmetric InfoNCE. Minimising it is what carved the 0.6153 gap we measured.

\(\tau\) is the temperature: a small \(\tau\) sharpens the softmax so the matched pair must dominate — which also makes in-batch false negatives (e.g. two near-duplicate captions of the same image) costly — while a large \(\tau\) flattens the distribution. CLIP learns \(\tau\) rather than fixing it (a learnable logit-scale, clamped to a ceiling). Lower \(\tau\) ⇒ a wider matched/mismatched gap.

Contrastive objective. CLIP InfoNCE over the batch cosine matrix: softmax per row, diagonal = positive, off-diagonal = in-batch negatives. Identical to L6 in-batch contrastive loss. Temperature τ. This loss carves the matched/mismatched gap (0.9944/0.3791/0.6153) the matrix slide measured. No NEW gated number here — the gap is the same one from data/l12-clip.json.

The cosine matrix, by hand — the diagonal wins

argmax each image row · then average matched vs mismatched
$$ \cos(\text{img}_i,\text{txt}_j) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0.9974} & 0.6547 & 0.171 \\ 0.6609 & \mathbf{0.991} & 0.2877 \\ 0.1712 & 0.329 & \mathbf{0.9949} \end{bmatrix} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{j}\ \text{row}_i = \text{diagonal},\ \ \mathbf{3/3} $$ $$ \overline{\cos}_{\text{matched}} = \tfrac{0.9974+0.991+0.9949}{3} = \mathbf{0.9944}, \quad \overline{\cos}_{\text{mismatched}} = \tfrac{2.2745}{6} = \mathbf{0.3791} $$ $$ \text{contrastive gap} = 0.9944 - 0.3791 = \mathbf{0.6153} $$

Each image's top match is the diagonal cell — its own caption — so cross-modal retrieval is right 3 of 3. Matched pairs sit near 1.0 while everything else sits near 0.38; the 0.6153 gap between them is precisely what makes the diagonal win so cleanly. This is contrastive cosine, now across modalities.

Why the diagonal wins — Sir Cosine, across modalities

the same contrastive idea from L6, now between pictures and words
Callback · L6 contrastive learning (Sir Cosine)
An image and its caption are trained to be neighbours in the shared space; every other image–caption pairing is pushed apart.
  • ·Same trick as L6 — the in-batch contrastive loss pulls matched pairs together and repels the rest. CLIP just makes one side an image and the other its caption.
  • ·That is the gap — training drives matched cosine high (0.9944) and mismatched low (0.3791); the 0.6153 gap is the contrastive objective, made visible.
  • ·Sir Cosine returns — the angle he measured on the unit sphere in L2/L6 is the very same score; he simply now compares a photograph to a sentence.

The matched/mismatched gap (0.9944 / 0.3791 / 0.6153) IS the contrastive objective. Sir Cosine (green-cap knight) returns from L2/L6.

Frontier · ColPali — retrieve over the page, as an image

skip the OCR pipeline; embed the document picture directly
Frontier idea · arXiv:2407.01449
Render each document page as an image, embed it with a vision-language model into patch-level vectors, and retrieve with late interaction — ColBERT's MaxSim (L8), now over image patches.
  • ·Fixes: the classic pipeline is OCR → layout parse → chunk → embed — brittle on tables and scans. ColPali skips it: the page is the input.
  • ·Trick: a query token attends to the most similar image patch (MaxSim). The figure, the table cell, the stamp — all retrievable without transcription.
  • ·Callback: it is L8's late-interaction (Sir Cosine measuring per-patch) fused with L12's shared image–text space — the two frontiers meet (Faysse et al., 2024).
  • ·Cost: late interaction keeps one vector per image patch (hundreds–to–over-a-thousand per page), so the index is one–two orders of magnitude larger than a single CLIP page vector — ColBERT's multi-vector tax (L8), now per page.
ColPali frontier. arXiv:2407.01449 (Faysse et al.). Render the page as an image; embed patch-level with a VLM; retrieve with late interaction (ColBERT MaxSim, L8) over patches. Skips the brittle OCR→parse→chunk pipeline. Fuses L8 late-interaction with L12 shared image-text space.

What multimodal retrieval buys — and what it costs

one shared space is powerful, and only as good as its training pairs
Applicability · the shared space has edges too
CLIP unlocks records text never could — but it only knows the concepts it was trained on, and inherits its image–caption biases.
  • ✓Reach for it when — the corpus is visual or you need text↔image search; ColPali when layout matters.
  • ×Mind the limits — fine detail (counting, exact text, rare concepts) is weak; real matrices noisier than our 3/3.
  • ×Modality gap — image and text embeddings sit in offset regions, so cross-modal cosines run lower; tune the cutoff per modality (it's why the toy's 0.9944 cosines look unrealistically high).
  • ·The catch ahead — a model that sees and answers can also confabulate. Power → responsibility.
Multimodal boundaries. Reach for CLIP on visual corpora / text↔image search; ColPali for layout. Limits: fine-grained detail weak; real matrices noisier than the toy 3/3. Bridges to ethics: power → responsibility; training-pair bias foreshadows the bias risk. Two-col def|image for fit.

Hallucination is the harm; grounding is the control

a fluent, confident answer about something that does not exist

The harm

  • Ask a model about the “Quasar-9 vector database” — a fictional entity that does not exist.
  • Closed-book, llama3.1:8b does not abstain — it confabulates concrete features: “a collection of 9000 vectors… large-scale dataset…”
  • Fluent and confident and entirely invented. A user cannot tell this apart from a true answer.

The control

  • Ground the same question in a retrieved context that says no such database exists.
  • Now the model abstains: “I cannot answer this question. The context does not provide any information…”
  • Same model, same question — grounding plus the freedom to say “I don't know” turns a confident fabrication into an honest refusal.

Risk 1 — bias

retrieval and generation both inherit it
Risk · bias enters at two stages
Retrieval + generation inherit the biases of the corpus and the model — what gets indexed, what ranks highly, and what the LLM was trained to say.
  • ·Where it enters — a skewed corpus surfaces skewed passages (the recall floor from L10, now ethical); a biased model amplifies them in the answer. Both stages compound.
  • ·Multimodal makes it sharper — CLIP's shared space is only as fair as its image–caption training pairs (the limit we just flagged).
  • ✓Mitigation — audit the corpora; measure subgroup performance; diversify sources. Bias you don't measure, you ship.
Risk · Bias. data/l12-ethics.json framework[0]: what = "Retrieval + generation inherit corpus and model biases"; mitigation = "Audit corpora; measure subgroup performance; diversify sources." Two-stage (retrieval + generation). Multimodal sharpens it (CLIP training-pair bias).

Risk 2 — privacy / PII

the index is an attack surface
Risk · the index can leak
Indexed documents may leak personal or secret data — once a passage is retrievable, the generator can surface it to anyone who asks the right question.
  • ·Where it enters — PII or confidential text swept into the corpus becomes retrievable; a well-phrased query is now an exfiltration path. Multi-tenant systems can leak across tenants.
  • ·Grounding cuts both ways — the very thing that makes RAG trustworthy (it answers from the index) is what makes a leaky index dangerous.
  • ✓Mitigation — PII scrubbing; access control on the index; per-tenant isolation. Treat the index with the same care as the source database.
Risk · Privacy/PII. data/l12-ethics.json framework[2]: what = "Indexed documents may leak personal or secret data"; mitigation = "PII scrubbing; access control on the index; per-tenant isolation." The index is an attack surface; grounding makes a leaky index dangerous. Multi-tenant cross-leak.

Risk 3 — attribution

an unsourced answer cannot be checked
Risk · no source, no recourse
Answers without sources cannot be verified or contested — the user has no way to tell a grounded claim from a confident guess, and no way to push back.
  • ·Where it enters — the generator can paraphrase the context without pointing to it. Fluency hides the seam between “the passage said” and “the model decided.”
  • ·This is the L10 citation discipline made ethical — we wrote grounded prompts with citations; attribution is why that was non-negotiable.
  • ✓Mitigation — cite the retrieved passages; show provenance; let users inspect. A claim a user can trace is a claim a user can challenge.
Risk · Attribution. data/l12-ethics.json framework[3]: what = "Answers without sources cannot be verified or contested"; mitigation = "Cite the retrieved passages; show provenance; let users inspect." Callback to L10's grounded-prompt-with-citations discipline — attribution is why it matters.

Recap · the advancing edge of retrieval-augmented generation
Every frontier answers a different failure of naive RAG — better structure, better indexing, better self-correction, and better modalities.
  • ·Structure — GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) walks an entity graph for multi-hop; RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., ICLR 2024) builds a tree of recursive summaries to retrieve at the right level of abstraction.
  • ·Self-correction — Self-RAG (Asai et al., ICLR 2024) bakes reflection tokens in to gate and critique retrieval — the faithfulness instinct, learned.
  • ·Modalities — CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and ColPali (Faysse et al., 2024) extend retrieval beyond text into one shared space. The frontier is wide, and still moving.
