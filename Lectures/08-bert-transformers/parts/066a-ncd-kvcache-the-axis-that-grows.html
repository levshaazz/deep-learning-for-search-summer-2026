Ось, которая растётThe axis that grows

шаг (←/→): prefill → один шаг декодирования → чем ты платишь на самом делеstep (←/→): prefill → one decode step → what you actually pay for

Экономия кэша видна как отсутствие прямоугольника: перед сохранёнными строками \(K\) и \(V\) больше нет проекции — их не пересчитывают, а читают. Prefill считает и сохраняет каждую строку; шаг декодирования добавляет ровно одну. Отсюда инверсия, ради которой существуют MQA, GQA и paged-attention: на генерации дорогая ось — кэш (\(2\cdot n\cdot d\) на слой на голову, растёт с каждым токеном), а внимание шага дёшево: оценки — \(1\times(n{+}1)\), а не \(n\times n\). Квадрат — это счёт prefill.The saving shows up as the absence of a box: in front of the stored \(K\) and \(V\) rows there is no projection any more — they are not recomputed, they are read. Prefill computes and keeps every row; a decode step adds exactly one. Hence the inversion MQA, GQA and paged-attention exist for: while you generate the expensive axis is the cache (\(2\cdot n\cdot d\) per layer per head, growing with every token), and a step’s attention is cheap — the scores are \(1\times(n{+}1)\), not \(n\times n\). The square is prefill’s bill.

ncd-kvcache, three steps, and it is the slide that fixes the mental model most people carry out of a decoding lecture. Step zero is prefill: the whole prompt, all n tokens at once, goes through L_Q, L_K, L_V exactly once. Here the query really IS a block of n rows, so attention really does build the full n-by-n score matrix — in fp16 across twelve heads that is twenty-five point eight gigabytes at thirty-two thousand tokens, and the ledger bills it. This is the ONE step where that bill is real. And the K and V rows are not thrown away at the end of it: they are KEPT, and the dashed figure around them is the cache. Step one is a single decode step, and the picture changes completely. One token arrives, so exactly one q, one new k row and one new v row are computed — that is everything the step costs. Now look at the OLD rows: in front of them there is no box at all. The path is empty. They are not recomputed, they are read — the projections that made them ran once, at prefill, and never run again. That absence, that missing rectangle, IS the saving; there is nothing else to see, and there does not need to be. And the scores: the query is one row, so the scores are one row too — one by n plus one. No n-by-n matrix is built here. Step two states the trade in shapes, and then the inversion. With the cache, one more token costs one K row and one V row; without it you would push L_K and L_V over all n+1 positions — redoing the whole prefill for every single token. So you buy compute with memory. And here is the thing nearly everyone has backwards: while you are DECODING, the expensive axis is the cache, not the attention. The cache holds K and V both — two times n times d per layer per head — and it grows linearly with every token you emit, forever, which is exactly why MQA, GQA and paged attention exist and why context length is a memory problem before it is a compute problem. The quadratic bill belongs to prefill. The decode step is cheap; it is the axis that grows underneath it that eventually eats the GPU.
