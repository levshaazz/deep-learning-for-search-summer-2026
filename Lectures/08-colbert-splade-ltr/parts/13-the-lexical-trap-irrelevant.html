$$ d_{\text{irr}} = \text{“the bank approved a loan”} $$ $$ \text{row maxes} = (0.20, 0.98, 0.12), \quad \text{MaxSim} = 0.20 + 0.98 + 0.12 = \mathbf{1.30} $$ $$ \mathbf{2.35} \;>\; \mathbf{1.30} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \textbf{BAM} $$

Финансовый документ совпадает с «bank» почти идеально (\(0{,}98\)) — но «river» и «flood» не находят пары (\(0{,}20, 0{,}12\)). MaxSim \(= \mathbf{1{,}30}\) проигрывает \(\mathbf{2{,}35}\): MaxSim награждает совпадение многих токенов, а не одного громкого. (Игрушечные векторы здесь нарочно контекстно-независимые — worst case до контекстуализации: настоящий BERT-энкодер развёл бы два «bank» ещё до MaxSim.)The finance doc matches "bank" almost perfectly (\(0.98\)) — but "river" and "flood" find no partner (\(0.20, 0.12\)). MaxSim \(= \mathbf{1.30}\) loses to \(\mathbf{2.35}\): MaxSim rewards matching many tokens, not one loud one. (The toy vectors here are deliberately context-independent — the worst case before contextualization: a real BERT encoder would pull the two "bank"s apart even before MaxSim.)

The BAM: a near-perfect single match (0.98 on bank) still loses, 1.30 < 2.35. Late interaction defeats the lexical trap. Note the caveat: the toy token vectors are deliberately context-independent, the worst case before contextualization — a real BERT-style encoder would already separate the river-"bank" from the finance-"bank", so the 0.98 is the adversarial setup, not what ColBERT would see.
