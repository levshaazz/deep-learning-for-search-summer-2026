You can't eyeball quality at scale

why we need a number

Reading one ranking by eye is fine. Judging \(10^{4}\) queries by eye is not — and "looks good" cannot be averaged, compared, or trended over time. We need three things: a relevance label per result (ground truth), a metric that turns a ranking into one comparable score, and statistics to tell signal from noise.

The core problem. With one query you can skim the list and form an opinion. But a real evaluation set has thousands of queries, and opinions don't aggregate: you cannot average "looks good", you cannot tell whether yesterday's change helped, and you cannot compare two systems fairly. The fix is a number — but a number needs ground truth to compare against, and a difference between two numbers needs a significance test before you believe it. Labels, metric, statistics: that is the spine of the lecture.
