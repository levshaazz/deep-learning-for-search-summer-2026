\(\mathrm{Precision}@k\) and \(\mathrm{Recall}@k\), step by step

pattern \([0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1]\) · 4 relevant total

Watch \(\mathrm{Recall}@k\) climb to \(1\) while \(\mathrm{Precision}@k\) drifts as \(k\) grows.

01
\(k=1\)
Top doc (rank 1) is not relevant. Relevant in top 1: \(0\). \(\mathrm{P}@1=0/1=0\); \(\mathrm{R}@1=0/4=0\).
\(0\ /\ 0\)
02
\(k=3\)
Ranks 1–3 contain one relevant (rank 2). \(\mathrm{P}@3=1/3\approx 0.333\); \(\mathrm{R}@3=1/4=0.25\).
\(0.333\ /\ 0.25\)
03
\(k=5\)
Ranks 1–5 contain two relevant (ranks 2, 4). \(\mathrm{P}@5=2/5=0.4\); \(\mathrm{R}@5=2/4=0.5\).
\(0.4\ /\ 0.5\)
→
\(k=8\)
All 4 relevant are now in view. \(\mathrm{P}@8=4/8=0.5\) (half the list relevant); \(\mathrm{R}@8=4/4=1.0\) (everything found).
\(0.5\ /\ 1.0\)
4/4
Walk it one row at a time, reading numerator/denominator aloud. Precision drifts 0 → 0.333 → 0.4 → 0.5 — it never gets high because the relevant docs are diluted by hockey docs at every odd rank. Recall marches 0 → 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0: by rank 8 we have found all four relevant docs. The two metrics disagree on purpose; that disagreement is exactly why we report both at chosen cutoffs — and why the tradeoff curve on the next slide matters.
