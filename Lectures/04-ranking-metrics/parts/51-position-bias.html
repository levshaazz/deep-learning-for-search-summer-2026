Клики смещены позициейClicks are biased by position

вероятность просмотра · модель просмотра (позиционная)examination probability · the examination (position-based) model

Геометрическая модель просмотра: \(P(\text{exam}\mid \text{rank}) = \gamma^{\,\text{rank}-1}\) при \(\gamma=0{,}94\). Документ на ранге 1 просматривается с вероятностью \(1{,}0\); на ранге 10 — лишь \(0{,}57\). Клик означает «релевантно и просмотрено» — поэтому низкие ранги получают меньше кликов независимо от качества.Geometric examination model: \(P(\text{exam}\mid \text{rank}) = \gamma^{\,\text{rank}-1}\) with \(\gamma=0.94\). A doc at rank 1 is examined with prob \(1.0\); at rank 10 only \(0.57\). A click means "relevant and examined" — so low ranks get fewer clicks regardless of quality.

The single biggest reason raw clicks lie. Users examine the top of the list far more than the bottom, so a click conflates two things: was the result relevant, and was it even looked at. The examination (position-based) model formalises this — examination depends only on rank; here we use a geometric form, probability of examining rank r is gamma to the r minus 1, gamma 0.94. Rank 1 is examined with certainty, rank 10 only 57% of the time. So a great result buried at rank 8 earns few clicks not because it's bad but because nobody saw it. Any click-based metric must correct for this. One caveat: this examination model is the simplest of a family. The cascade model (Craswell et al. 2008) makes examination of rank r conditional on not being satisfied by anything above it, so the ranks couple — a strong rank-1 result starves the clicks below it (the same intuition ERR uses on slide 36). And a click also needs the snippet to be attractive: caption/trust bias means users sometimes click on the wording, not the true relevance. So debiasing position alone is incomplete.
