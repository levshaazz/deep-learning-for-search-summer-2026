Part 04

Graded relevance:
nDCG

My counting metrics call every hit equal — but on my course a gem at rank 1 is worth more than a trinket at rank 8. Time to grade.

✓ You can now compute \(\mathrm{P}@k\), \(\mathrm{R}@k\), MRR and MAP by hand. Next: weight by how relevant and where.

My counting metrics treat every relevant doc as equally good and only loosely reward position. nDCG fixes both: it uses graded gains (some docs are more relevant than others) and a smooth logarithmic position discount, then normalises by the best possible ordering so the score lands in [0, 1] and is comparable across queries. This is the headline metric of modern web ranking, and the one I'll lean on hardest.
