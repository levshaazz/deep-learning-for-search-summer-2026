Mean nDCG: \(A = 0.6046\), \(B = 0.6443\), so \(\overline{\Delta} = +0.0397\). B looks better — but note \(4\) of \(15\) per-query diffs are negative. Is \(+0.04\) real?

q6's \(+.00\) is a tiny positive (\(+0.0001\)), not a tie — Wilcoxon drops exact zeros, so all \(15\) diffs are kept and ranked.

This pays off the "two rankers" hook from slide one with a real second system: B is a tuned BM25 with a small genuine edge over baseline A, measured per-query over 15 queries. The means are 0.6046 and 0.6443, a difference of 0.0397 in B's favour. But look at the delta row — B loses on four queries. The mean improvement is small relative to the per-query spread. A naive reading says "B wins, ship it"; a careful one asks whether 0.04 could arise by chance from 15 noisy queries. That question is the rest of Part 5.
