MRR over two queries

so MRR \(\neq\) any single RR

Reciprocal rank per query, then average. Q1 is our running ranking; Q2 hits at rank 1.

Q1
first hit at rank \(2\)
Pattern \([0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1]\). First relevant doc (D3) is at rank \(2\). \(\mathrm{RR}_1 = 1/2 = 0.5\).
\(0.5\)
Q2
first hit at rank \(1\)
A second query whose pattern is \([1,0,1,1,0,0,1,0]\). First relevant doc is at rank \(1\). \(\mathrm{RR}_2 = 1/1 = 1.0\).
\(1.0\)
→
average
\(\mathrm{MRR} = \dfrac{0.5 + 1.0}{2} = 0.75\). Note: this equals neither query's RR — the mean is the metric.
\(0.75\)
3/3
This is why we add a second query: with one query, MRR collapses to that query's RR and the "mean" is invisible. Here Q1's first hit is at rank 2 (RR 0.5) and Q2's is at rank 1 (RR 1.0). The mean is 0.75 — and crucially it is not equal to either query's reciprocal rank. That's the whole point of the leading "M": MRR summarises a query set, and a single-query demo hides the averaging that makes it a real evaluation metric.
