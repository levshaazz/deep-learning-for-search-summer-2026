What happens
$$Q_1=[0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1],\ Q_2=[1,0,1,1,0,0,1,0]$$

Two queries, four relevant docs each. AP averages \(\mathrm{P}@k\) at the hit ranks.

$$\mathrm{P}@2,\mathrm{P}@4,\mathrm{P}@6,\mathrm{P}@8 = \tfrac12,\tfrac24,\tfrac36,\tfrac48$$

Q1 hits at ranks \(2,4,6,8\): each precision is exactly \(0.5\).

$$\mathrm{AP}_1 = \tfrac{0.5+0.5+0.5+0.5}{4} = 0.5$$

AP\(_1\). Average over \(R=4\) relevant docs.

$$\mathrm{P}@1{=}\tfrac11,\ \mathrm{P}@3{=}\tfrac23,\ \mathrm{P}@4{=}\tfrac34,\ \mathrm{P}@7{=}\tfrac47$$

Q2 hits at ranks \(1,3,4,7\): \(1.0,\,0.6667,\,0.75,\,0.5714\) — front-loaded, so higher precision.

$$\mathrm{AP}_2 = \frac{1.0+0.6667+0.75+0.5714}{4} = 0.747$$

AP\(_2\). Average the four hit precisions over \(R=4\) relevant docs.

$$\mathrm{MAP} = \frac{0.5 + 0.747}{2} = 0.6235$$

Result. MAP equals neither AP — the mean is the metric.

overview
The full MAP computation, with two queries so MAP differs from AP. Q1's relevant docs sit at ranks 2,4,6,8 — each just below a non-relevant doc — so Precision@k at every hit is exactly one-half, giving AP1 = 0.5. Q2's relevant docs are front-loaded at ranks 1,3,4,7, so its precisions are higher and AP2 = 0.747. MAP is the arithmetic mean, 0.6235 — equal to neither AP. The uniform 0.5 in Q1 is a tidy artifact of the alternating pattern; on real query sets these vary and MAP separates good systems from bad.
