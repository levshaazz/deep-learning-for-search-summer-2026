Why graded relevance & position matter

two things counting metrics miss

Not all hits are equal

A perfect, on-topic, authoritative result is worth more than a barely-relevant one. Binary relevance flattens this; graded gain keeps it.

i
Our grades \(0/1/3\)

Strong space hits (D3, D2) \(=3\); marginal (D0, D1) \(=1\); hockey \(=0\).

Rank 1 \(\gg\) rank 8

Users read top-down and rarely scroll. A relevant doc at rank 1 helps far more than the same doc at rank 8 — so the metric must discount by position.

i
The discount

Each rank \(i\) is divided by \(\log_2(i+1)\): rank 2 keeps \(0.63\), rank 8 only \(0.32\).

nDCG exists because the counting family ignores two facts. First, relevance is graded: an authoritative, exactly-on-topic document beats a tangential one, and we lose that with 0/1 labels. Second, position is everything: people scan from the top and almost never reach rank 8, so a hit there is nearly worthless compared to a hit at rank 1. nDCG bakes both in — graded gain in the numerator, a position discount that shrinks with depth.
