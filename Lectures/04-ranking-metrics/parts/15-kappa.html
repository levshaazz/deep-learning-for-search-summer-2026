Do the judges even agree?

Cohen's \(\kappa\) · label noise
$$ \kappa = \frac{p_o - p_e}{1 - p_e} $$
\(p_o\)
Observed
fraction of pairs the two judges label the same
\(p_e\)
Chance
agreement expected from label rates alone
\(\kappa\)
Agreement
\(1\) perfect, \(0\) chance, \(<0\) worse

Rule of thumb: \(\kappa>0.6\) substantial, \(>0.8\) near-gold. Many judges → Fleiss' \(\kappa\). Low \(\kappa\) means your "ground truth" is noisy and caps the metric's resolution.

Ground truth is not handed down from heaven — humans disagree, especially on borderline relevance. Cohen's kappa corrects raw agreement for the agreement you'd get by chance: subtract chance, normalise by the room left above chance. Above 0.6 is usually called substantial, above 0.8 near-gold; Fleiss generalises to more than two judges. The practical lesson: if your annotators only weakly agree, your labels are noisy, and no metric can resolve system differences finer than that noise. Measure agreement before you trust the qrels.
