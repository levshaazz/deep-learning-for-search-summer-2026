DCG, IDCG, and nDCG

gain, discounted by position, then normalized
$$ \mathrm{DCG}@k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{g_i}{\log_2(i+1)}, \qquad \mathrm{nDCG}@k = \frac{\mathrm{DCG}@k}{\mathrm{IDCG}@k} $$
\(g_i\)
Gain
graded relevance of the doc at rank \(i\)
\(\log_2(i+1)\)
Discount
deeper ranks are worth less
\(\mathrm{IDCG}@k\)
Ideal DCG
DCG of the best possible ordering

Why \(\log_2(i+1)\)? A smooth, slowly-decaying discount — gentler than \(1/i\), so rank 10 still counts. Dividing by \(\mathrm{IDCG}\) puts \(\mathrm{nDCG}\) in \([0,1]\): \(1\) is a perfect ranking.

Three pieces. DCG sums each doc's gain divided by a logarithmic position discount, so a relevant doc high up contributes more than the same doc buried deep. The log is deliberately gentle — unlike 1/i it doesn't crush deep ranks to nothing, which matches how users still occasionally scroll. IDCG is the DCG of the ideal ranking — all highest-gain docs first — the maximum achievable. Dividing DCG by IDCG normalizes to [0,1], making nDCG comparable across queries with different numbers and grades of relevant docs. Next slide walks it on our graded ranking, both gain functions.
