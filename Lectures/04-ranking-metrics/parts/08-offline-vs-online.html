Two regimes: offline and online

the lecture roadmap

Offline · test collection

  • ·A fixed set of queries + human relevance judgments (qrels).
  • ·Cheap, repeatable, no users at risk. Run it on every commit.
  • ·Metrics: \(\mathrm{P}@k\), \(\mathrm{R}@k\), MRR, MAP, nDCG.
  • ·Risk: labels are a proxy for what users actually want.

Online · real users

  • ·Ship the change to a slice of live traffic, watch behaviour.
  • ·Signals: clicks, dwell, conversion — the truth, but noisy and biased.
  • ·Methods: A/B testing, interleaving, counterfactual/IPS.
  • ·Risk: slow, expensive, can hurt real users.

Offline = fast iteration on a proxy. Online = slow confirmation on the truth. Mature teams run both, and check that offline gains predict online wins.

This is the map for the whole day. Offline evaluation uses a frozen test collection: a set of queries with human labels, so you can score any system instantly and repeatably — perfect for the inner loop of development. But labels are a proxy. Online evaluation puts the change in front of real users and measures behaviour, which is the real thing you care about — but it is slow, noisy, biased by position, and risky. Parts 2–5 are offline; Part 6 is online; Part 7 is the bridge — and the warning that the two can disagree.
