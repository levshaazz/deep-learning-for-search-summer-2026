Quick check

Audience question
Offline test on 15 queries: B beats A at \(p=0.039\). Most defensible next step before shipping B?
AShip B — \(p<0.05\) proves it's better.
BConfirm on more queries or a live A/B test — the CI nearly touches 0 and \(n\) is tiny.
CRe-run the same 15 queries until \(p\) drops.
Reveal explanation
B. \(p<0.05\) is necessary, not sufficient: the 95% CI \([0.0023,0.0772]\) nearly reaches 0, so the gain could be tiny, and 15 queries is under-powered. Re-running the same queries (C) adds nothing and invites p-hacking — confirm on independent data, ideally a live A/B test.
Consolidates Part 5. The discipline is: a single significant offline result on a small query set is a hint, not a verdict. The CI tells you the effect might be negligible; the small n tells you you're under-powered. The right move is independent confirmation — more queries, a fresh test set, or best of all a real online experiment, which measures the thing the offline labels were only approximating. That's the bridge into the online half of the lecture.
