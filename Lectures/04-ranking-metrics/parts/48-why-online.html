Why offline isn't enough

the proxy breaks down

Offline's blind spots

  • ·Proxy labels — an assessor's "relevant" \(\neq\) a user's satisfaction.
  • ·Drift — intents, freshness, trends shift; static qrels age.
  • ·No signal for UI, latency, presentation, personalization.

What online adds

  • ·Real behaviour — clicks, dwell time, conversions, re-queries.
  • ·Business outcomes — revenue, retention, the thing that pays salaries.
  • ·Catches what no label can: did the user actually get their answer?

Offline finds candidates fast; online confirms them on the only judge that matters — the user.

Offline metrics are indispensable for iteration speed, but they answer a question one step removed from the goal: does this match our labels, not does this satisfy users. Labels are a proxy created at one moment by assessors with a guideline; real intent drifts and is personal, and presentation, speed, and UI never show up in a qrel file. Online evaluation measures actual behaviour and ties to business outcomes. The mature workflow uses offline to generate and pre-filter candidates cheaply, then online to confirm the survivors on live traffic.
