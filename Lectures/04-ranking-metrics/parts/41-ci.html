The confidence interval says more than \(p\)

effect size, with uncertainty
$$ \overline{\Delta} \pm t_{0.975,\,n-1}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{sd}}{\sqrt{n}} = 0.0397 \pm 2.145\cdot\frac{0.0676}{\sqrt{15}} = [\,0.0023,\ 0.0772\,] $$
\(t_{0.975,14}\)
\(=2.145\)

SE
\(0.0676/\sqrt{15}=0.01747\)
standard error of the mean diff
half-width
\(2.145{\cdot}0.01747=0.0375\)
\(0.0397\pm0.0375\)

\(\Rightarrow [\,0.0397-0.0375,\ 0.0397+0.0375\,] \approx [\,0.0023,\ 0.0772\,]\). The \(t\)-critical \(2.145\) is a table value, not computed by hand.

The CI excludes \(0\) (so \(p<0.05\)) but reaches down to \(+0.002\): B is probably better, but the true gain might be negligible. A \(p\)-value alone hides this.

A p-value tells you the difference is unlikely to be zero; a confidence interval tells you how big it plausibly is, which is what actually matters for a ship decision. Here the 95% CI is 0.0023 to 0.0772 — it excludes zero, consistent with the significant p-values, but its lower edge is almost zero, so the real improvement could be trivially small. And it's wide because 15 queries is few. Always report the interval, not just the star: significant and large are different claims, and only the CI distinguishes them.
