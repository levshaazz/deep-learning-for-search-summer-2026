The precision–recall tradeoff & \(F_1\)

one curve, one summary number

The four points are plotted to scale — \(@1\,(0,0)\), \(@3\,(0.25,0.333)\), \(@5\,(0.5,0.4)\), \(@8\,(1.0,0.5)\) — and the curve runs through them. (On this particular ranking the hits cluster at even ranks, so precision actually rises with \(k\); the textbook curve usually falls.) \(F_1=\dfrac{2PR}{P+R}\) is their harmonic mean — punishing imbalance — and the 11-point interpolated curve and \(F_\beta\) (\(\beta>1\) favours recall) generalise it.

Precision and recall pull against each other, and the PR curve draws that tension: every cutoff is one operating point. F1 collapses the two into one number using the harmonic mean, which — unlike the arithmetic mean — refuses to be fooled by a system that maxes one and tanks the other. F-beta tilts the balance: beta above 1 weights recall more, useful when missing a relevant doc is costlier than showing a bad one. The 11-point interpolated precision is the classic smoothing TREC used to average PR curves across queries.
