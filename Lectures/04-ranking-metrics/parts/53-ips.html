Inverse Propensity Scoring: clicks are biased by what the old policy chose to show. Divide each reward by its propensity → an unbiased estimate of a new policy's value from logged data. Foundation of unbiased learning-to-rank.

The most modern idea here. Logged clicks are biased twice over — by position (Part 49) and by the logging policy itself, which only ever showed certain documents. Inverse Propensity Scoring corrects this: weight each observed reward by one over the probability that the old policy displayed it, so items the old system rarely showed but a user still clicked get up-weighted. The result is an unbiased estimate of how a new policy would have performed, computed entirely from logs without running a fresh experiment. Joachims and colleagues built unbiased learning-to-rank on exactly this — train and evaluate rankers from biased click logs. It needs accurate propensities, which is the hard part.
