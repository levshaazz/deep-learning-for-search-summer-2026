Inverse Propensity Scoring (оценка по обратной склонности): клики смещены тем, что старая политика решила показать. Разделите каждую награду на её склонность → несмещённая оценка ценности новой политики из логированных данных. Основа несмещённого learning-to-rank. Две загвоздки: малые \(p_i\) заставляют \(r_i/p_i\) взрываться (высокая дисперсия — обрезайте/клипуйте или используйте самонормированный IPS), и каждому оцениваемому элементу нужно \(p_i>0\) (поддержка), иначе оценка смещена, а не просто зашумлена.Inverse Propensity Scoring: clicks are biased by what the old policy chose to show. Divide each reward by its propensity → an unbiased estimate of a new policy's value from logged data. Foundation of unbiased learning-to-rank. Two catches: small \(p_i\) make \(r_i/p_i\) explode (high variance — cap/clip or use self-normalised IPS), and every evaluable item needs \(p_i>0\) (support), or the estimate is biased, not just noisy.

The most modern idea here. Logged clicks are biased twice over — by position (slide 51) and by the logging policy itself, which only ever showed certain documents. Inverse Propensity Scoring corrects this: weight each observed reward by one over the probability that the old policy displayed it, so items the old system rarely showed but a user still clicked get up-weighted. The result is an unbiased estimate of how a new policy would have performed, computed entirely from logs without running a fresh experiment. Joachims and colleagues built unbiased learning-to-rank on exactly this — train and evaluate rankers from biased click logs. It needs accurate propensities, which is the hard part. Two failure modes beyond that. Variance: a rarely-shown item has a tiny propensity, so r over p explodes — the estimator stays unbiased but can be uselessly noisy. The standard fixes are clipping/capping the propensities or self-normalised IPS (SNIPS), which divides by the sum of weights. And support (positivity): every action you want to evaluate must have had p greater than zero in the logs, or IPS is biased, not merely noisy, because some outcomes are never observed.
