DCG vs IDCG

Обе лестницы нарисованы в масштабе. Идеал складывает два самородка градации 3 на рангах 1–2, давая \(\mathrm{IDCG}=3{\cdot}1 + 3{\cdot}0.631 + 1{\cdot}0.5 + 1{\cdot}0.431 = 5.8235\); наше ранжирование BM25 сдвигает те же самородки вниз на ранги 2 и 4, платя более крутой дисконт, давая \(\mathrm{DCG}=3.8565\). Их отношение \(\mathrm{nDCG}=3.8565/5.8235\approx 0.662\) — около двух третей достижимого выигрыша (gain).Both ladders are drawn to scale. The ideal stacks the two grade-3 gems at ranks 1–2 for \(\mathrm{IDCG}=3{\cdot}1 + 3{\cdot}0.631 + 1{\cdot}0.5 + 1{\cdot}0.431 = 5.8235\); our BM25 ranking pushes the same gems down to ranks 2 and 4, paying a steeper discount, for \(\mathrm{DCG}=3.8565\). Their ratio \(\mathrm{nDCG}=3.8565/5.8235\approx 0.662\) — about two-thirds of the achievable gain.

The intuition behind normalization. IDCG is the score of a perfect ranker that puts every relevant doc — highest grade first — as high as possible; it is the ceiling for this query. Our ranker scattered the relevant docs down the list, paying the log discount each time, so it earns only 3.8565 of the available 5.8235 — about two-thirds. nDCG = 0.662 is therefore "two-thirds of the way to perfect", a phrase you can compare directly against any other system on the same query set, regardless of how many relevant docs each query has.
