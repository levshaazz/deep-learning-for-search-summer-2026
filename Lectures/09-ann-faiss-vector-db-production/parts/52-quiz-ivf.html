Quick check — the partition lane

nprobe and the boundary neighbour
In the 20-point IVF sweep, \(\textrm{nprobe}=1\) scanned 4 points for recall@5 \(=0.6\) and \(\textrm{nprobe}=3\) scanned 13 for \(1.0\). What does going from \(\textrm{nprobe}=3\) to \(5\) (scanning 17 then 20) buy you?
A Higher recall — more cells always means more true neighbours found
B Lower latency, because the work is spread over more cells
C Nothing — recall is already 1.0; the extra cells are pure overhead (diminishing returns)
Reveal explanation
C. All five true neighbours sat in the three nearest cells \(c_0, c_4, c_1\), so by \(\textrm{nprobe}=3\) recall is already \(1.0\). Probing cells four and five scans 17 then 20 points and finds no new neighbour — pure extra work. The knee is at \(\textrm{nprobe}=3\): the smallest setting that already gives full recall. (A is wrong — recall plateaus; B is wrong — more cells means more work, higher latency.)
