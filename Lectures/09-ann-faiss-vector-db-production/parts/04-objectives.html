By the end you can…

eight things you can do on paper
  • Explain why exact kNN dies at scale — both the \(O(N\cdot d)\) cost and high-dimensional distance concentration (the Wraith).
  • Choose a distance — L2 vs cosine vs inner product — and predict when they crown different top-1 results.
  • Trace HNSW greedy descent on a small-world graph and explain why the upper hub layer escapes a base-layer trap.
  • Tune the ef / M knobs and read a recall-vs-ef curve to its knee.
  • Compute IVF recall@k vs nprobe and explain the coarse-quantizer / boundary-neighbour tradeoff.
  • Derive the PQ memory math (subvectors, \(k=256\) codebooks, bytes/vector) and read an ADC distance off a table.
  • Compare flat / HNSW / IVF / IVF-PQ / HNSW-PQ and pick an index from a decision tree.
  • Lay out a production serving path and a latency budget against a \(p_{99}\) SLA — caching and quantization as the levers — and place FAISS vs vector DBs and the TurboQuant frontier.
Eight objectives mapping to the seven stops. The TurboQuant frontier is a nod, not the baseline. Each verb is a thing the student can reproduce on paper from this deck.
