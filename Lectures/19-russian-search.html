Часть 01 · Морфология и токен-налог Part 01 · Morphology & the token tax

Морфология наносит ответный удар Morphology strikes back

Английское слово почти монолитно: search — один токен. Русское слово несёт падеж, число, род и вид внутри себя — и тот же BPE, обученный в основном на английском, рубит «запроса» на семь кусков. An English word is nearly monolithic: search is one token. A Russian word carries case, number, gender and aspect inside itself — and the same BPE, trained mostly on English, shreds “запроса” into seven pieces.

Серёга и Токенозавр: динозавр легко откусывает английские слова целиком, но давится русскими — и выставляет счёт в токенах. Séréga and Tokenosaurus: the dinosaur bites English words whole, but chokes on Russian ones — and hands you the bill in tokens.

Divider, Act 1 — the token tax. The hook of the whole lecture: an English word is close to one token, but a Russian word packs case/number/gender/aspect into itself, and a BPE tokenizer trained on English-heavy data has no merges for those endings, so it falls back toward characters. Tokenosaurus eats English whole and chokes on Russian. This act builds the tax (measured on our toy), shows why it is unfair, and names when it does and does not bite.

Русский — флективный язык Russian is a fusional language

окончание несёт грамматику — и меняет строку the ending carries grammar — and rewrites the string
Что значит «флективный» What “fusional” means
  • ·Одно окончание — сразу несколько значений. «-ами» в «запросами» кодирует и творительный падеж, и множественное число одной морфемой.One ending, several meanings at once. “-ами” in “запросами” encodes both instrumental case and plural in a single morpheme.
  • ·Существительное = 6 падежей × 2 числа. Плюс чередования в основе (котёнок → котята): корень сам меняет буквы.A noun = 6 cases × 2 numbers. Plus stem alternations (котёнок → котята): the root itself changes letters.
  • ·Глагол — ещё богаче: лицо, число, время, вид, наклонение. Один глагол — десятки форм.A verb is richer still: person, number, tense, aspect, mood. One verb, dozens of forms.

Сравни с английским: cat / cats, play / plays / played / playing — горстка форм, общий видимый корень. У русского корень часто спрятан.Compare English: cat / cats, play / plays / played / playing — a handful of forms with a visible shared stem. In Russian the stem is often hidden.

The linguistic root cause. Fusional (as opposed to agglutinative or isolating): a single affix fuses several grammatical values, and stems alternate. A Russian noun has 6 cases × 2 numbers; a verb adds person/tense/aspect/mood. English is weakly inflected with a visible stem, which is why lexical search "mostly works" in English and fails in Russian. This motivates both normalization (Act 2) and the token tax (this act) — the endings the reader sees are exactly what the tokenizer and the index trip over.

The typology slide, and the one place the course's own trilingual setting pays off. Fusional (Russian): one affix fuses several grammatical values, so you cannot peel them apart — the whole ending has to be removed at once, which is why lemmatization beats naive suffix stripping. Agglutinative (Tatar, and the Turkic family generally): morphemes are strung in a fixed order, one meaning each, so a rule-based stripper works far better even though the form count is higher. Analytic (English): grammar sits in separate function words, which is exactly why lexical search "just works" in English. The 12.5 forms per lexeme is the OpenCorpora dictionary average — a lexeme's df is smeared across roughly a dozen index terms.

Почему BPE рвёт русское слово Why BPE shreds a Russian word

слияние выигрывает частота, а частоты считаны не на твоём корпусе frequency wins the merge — and it was counted on someone else’s corpus
Один тезис One claim

$$ (a,b)^{\star} \;=\; \arg\max_{(a,b)} \; \mathrm{count}_{\mathcal{C}}(a,b) $$

BPE (L2) на каждом шаге сливает пару, самую частую в корпусе обучения токенизатора \(\mathcal{C}\). Если \(\mathcal{C}\) перекошен к английскому — а веб перекошен, — кириллическая пара не выигрывает этот argmax ни разу. Слияний для русских основ и окончаний просто не появляется, и слово откатывается к отдельным символам.At every step BPE (L2) merges the pair that is most frequent in the tokenizer’s training corpus \(\mathcal{C}\). If \(\mathcal{C}\) is English-skewed — and the web is — a Cyrillic pair never wins that argmax even once. No merges appear for Russian stems and endings, and the word falls back to single characters.

Отсюда правило: словарь токенизатора — это слепок чужого корпуса, а не свойство языка. Дальше — счёт в байтах и настоящие цифры фертильности.Hence the rule: a tokenizer’s vocabulary is a cast of someone else’s corpus, not a property of the language. Next: the bill in bytes, then the real fertility numbers.

The formal cause, in one line. BPE's merge rule is an argmax over pair counts in the tokenizer's training corpus C — not in your data. English-skewed C means Cyrillic pairs never win, so no Russian merges are learned and Russian falls back toward characters. Two consequences that used to live on this slide have moved where they are actually demonstrated: the two-bytes-per-Cyrillic-character head start goes to the byte-count slide right after the toy, and "token boundaries ≠ morpheme boundaries" belongs to Act 2, where the Snowball cascade shows real morpheme boundaries being missed. Keep this slide short — it is the hinge, not the evidence.

Счёт в байтах The bill in bytes

Откуда фора у английского Where English’s head start comes from

Байтовый BPE (byte-level BPE) видит не буквы, а байты UTF-8. Латинская буква — один байт, кириллическая — два. Значит, на том же слове алгоритм стартует с вдвое большего числа кусков, и первые слияния уходят только на то, чтобы собрать обратно буквы.

A byte-level BPE sees bytes, not letters. A Latin letter is one UTF-8 byte, a Cyrillic letter is two. So on the same word the algorithm starts from twice as many pieces, and its first merges are spent merely reassembling letters.

Нет. BM25 алфавит-агностичен: он проверяет равенство строк, и какие в них байты — ему безразлично. The trap worth naming out loud, because it is the single most common thing people believe about Russian search — and it is the wrong diagnosis, which leads to the wrong fix (transliteration pipelines, forced ASCII folding). BM25 compares strings; Unicode is irrelevant to it. What actually breaks are two independent mechanisms already established in this act: a tokenizer whose merges were bought with English pair counts, and a fusional morphology that scatters one lexeme across ten index terms. The falsification test is cheap and memorable: romanize everything and watch the surface-form miss survive untouched. Приведи к корню — или промахнись Reduce to the root — or miss

BM25 (L3) считает совпадения строк. На флективном языке запрос и ответ делят ноль строк — пока ты не сведёшь формы к общему виду: стемминг отрезает окончание, лемматизация находит словарную форму. BM25 (L3) counts string matches. On a fusional language the query and the answer share zero strings — until you fold the forms to a common shape: stemming chops the ending, lemmatization finds the dictionary form.

Серёга у мельницы лемм: в воронку сыплются «котята, котёнок, котятам», из желоба выходит один «котёнок» — теперь индекс их видит одним словом. Séréga at the lemma mill: “котята, котёнок, котятам” pour into the hopper, one “котёнок” comes out the chute — now the index sees them as one word.

Divider, Act 2 — normalization. BM25 (L3) is a bag-of-strings scorer: it can only reward a term the query and document literally share. On a fusional language they often share none, so you must first fold inflected forms to a common shape. Two tools: stemming (crudely chop the ending to a stem) and lemmatization (map to the dictionary form, dictionary-aware). Séréga's lemma mill turns a family of forms into one lemma the index can match. This act measures the ranking failure and its fix on our toy, then contrasts the tools.

The mechanism of the failure. The algorithm, opened up. Snowball Russian first folds ё to е (a prelude added in 2018), then computes three regions: RV starts after the first vowel, R1 after the first non-vowel that follows a vowel, R2 the same rule applied inside R1. Everything else is four steps of "longest matching ending inside a region": step 1 removes a perfective gerund, or else a reflexive плюс one adjectival/verbal/nominal ending; step 2 a trailing и; step 3 a derivational ост/ость but only inside R2; step 4 undoubles нн, strips a superlative, or drops a soft sign. There are no morphemes anywhere in it. The formal reason dense retrieval sidesteps Act 2. Two mechanisms combine: sub-word tokenization means inflected forms share stem pieces (котёнок / котята overlap on "кот…"), so the fragmentation that was a tax in Act 1 becomes a soft match here; and contrastive pair training (L10) places different forms of one meaning near each other in the space, so retrieval matches by cosine, not string. Together they make explicit lemmatization unnecessary for dense retrieval — the morphology is absorbed into the vector. Same conclusion as the English L10 story, with the Russian twist that the token tax and the surface-form miss turn into an advantage.

The formal statement of the previous slide's first mechanism, and the act-to-act hinge of the whole lecture. BM25 sums over t ∈ q ∩ d, so when the query says "котята" and the document says "котёнок" the intersection is empty and the score is exactly zero — that was Act 2's worked example. A sub-word tokenizer maps both forms onto overlapping piece sets, so |sub(q) ∩ sub(d)| > 0 even while |q ∩ d| = 0, and the encoder has something to build a shared representation from. Note the reversal: the finer the fragmentation, the higher the token tax (Act 1) but also the likelier the sub-word overlap (Act 3). The same property, two signs. The hard negatives of L11 are usually mined with BM25: take the top of the ranking for a query, drop the gold document, call the rest negatives. So the quality of your training pairs is set by the lexical retriever.
  • ·Без нормализации майнить нечем. Акт 2: постинг-лист по форме «котята» пуст, df = 0 — BM25 возвращает не трудные негативы, а случайный шум. Модель учится отличать золотое от произвольного, то есть ничему.Without normalization there is nothing to mine. Act 2: the postings list for the surface form “котята” is empty, df = 0 — BM25 returns random noise instead of hard negatives. The model learns to tell gold from arbitrary, which is nothing.
  • ·С нормализацией приходят ложные негативы. Snowball склеивает «стал», «сталь» и «стали» в один стем «стал», а словарный лемматизатор без контекста выбирает «стать» и теряет «сталь». Snowball conflates “стал”, “сталь” and “стали” into one stem “стал”, while a context-blind dictionary lemmatizer picks “стать” and loses “сталь”. A document about the metal lands among the negatives of a query about the metal — and you are teaching the encoder to push the right answer away.

Порядок работ: сначала чинишь лексическое плечо, потом майнишь им негативы, и только потом дообучаешь ретривер (L11).Order of work: fix the lexical arm first, mine negatives with it second, fine-tune the retriever (L11) only third.

The bridge from Act 2 to L11 that makes the whole lecture load-bearing for anyone who fine-tunes. Hard negatives are mined with BM25, so the lexical arm silently authors your training set. In Russian this fails twice. Un-normalized, the postings list for an inflected query form is empty — df = 0, the Act 2 result — so BM25 returns noise and the "hard" negatives are trivially easy; the contrastive objective collapses. Normalized, the conflations of Act 2 turn into false negatives: Snowball maps стал / сталь / стали onto the single stem стал, and a context-blind lemmatizer resolves стали to стать, dropping the steel reading. A genuinely relevant document then enters the negative set and gradient descent pushes it away from its own query. So: fix the lexical arm, then mine, then train.

The counterexample that repairs the lecture's own thesis. Act 2 phrased symmetry as "normalize both arms the same way", which does not literally apply to dense retrieval — nothing is normalized there. The correct general form is: apply the same transformation to query and corpus. The E5 family makes that concrete, because it was trained with "query: " and "passage: " prefixes; drop the query prefix and you feed the encoder an input distribution it never saw. The practitioner's decision table, tying every act together. Keyword/exact-term search → BM25 + lemmatization (cheap, transparent, fixes forms). Semantic/paraphrase → multilingual-E5/BGE-M3 (meaning past morphology, no lemmatizer). Production best baseline → a hybrid of the lexical and the dense arm — exact terms and meaning together. The fusion RULE is deliberately not prescribed here: slide "Hybrid in Russian by the numbers" shows that MIRACL-ru's 0.532 comes from fusing BM25 and mDPR SCORES, and that L12's Reciprocal Rank Fusion has no published Russian evaluation at all — so recommending RRF by name would be claiming a number nobody has. Narrow Russian domain → a ru-specific model or fine-tuning. Cross-lingual ru↔en → a multilingual vector (BGE-M3 / mE5); LaBSE is a bitext-mining specialist and scores 31.53 on MTEB(rus, v1.1) Retrieval, so it is not the cross-lingual retriever to reach for.

Normalize in both arms with one analyzer and one code path. Keep не/ни/без out of the stop list so negation survives indexing. Put the ё→е char_filter before the stop filter, since the Lucene list has no ё forms. Normalize to NFC on input, because macOS serves NFD. Keep a case- and ё-preserving keyword field for entities.
