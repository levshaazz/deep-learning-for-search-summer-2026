The geometry is the point: in an encoder, this attention row runs across the chunk boundary. Cut the document before encoding and the “Berlin” column in this row does not exist — not “a small weight”, but no column at all.

Bridge to L6/L15 in one picture. One row of the encoder's attention matrix — the row belonging to the token «Its». Bar heights are ILLUSTRATIVE (say so out loud: the paper publishes no attention maps for this example); what is not illustrative is the structure. In the whole-document pass the row spans the chunk boundary and can put mass on «Berlin». In the naive pass the matrix is n_chunk × n_chunk, so the «Berlin» column is not small — it is absent. That is the difference between "the model under-weighted the referent" and "the referent was never in the input".
