My catchphrase for the day, and Sir Cosine speaks it. Until now words were symbols: the engine could check if two strings were equal, but had no notion of one word being close to another. Символы: каждая пара слов равноудаленаSymbols: every pair of words is equidistant

one-hot · ортогональные осиone-hot · orthogonal axes

В one-hot (и в BM25 на термах) разные слова ортогональны: косинус любой пары различных слов равен 0. Геометрии смысла нет — это домашняя территория Лексического Гремлина.In one-hot (and in term-based BM25) distinct words are orthogonal: the cosine of any two different words is 0. There is no geometry of meaning — the Lexical Gremlin's home turf.

State the problem cleanly. In a one-hot encoding each word is its own axis, so any two distinct words are orthogonal and their cosine is exactly zero. Cat is no closer to dog than to airplane — the representation knows nothing about meaning. This is the same lexical gap that beat BM25 last lecture: a string matcher sees couch and sofa as orthogonal too. The Gremlin lives here, in the zero off-diagonal of the identity matrix. To get nearness we need vectors that are dense, not one-hot.

В отличие от one-hot, оси больше не «по одному слову на ось». Похожие слова делят направления — у пространства появляется форма. Вопрос: откуда взять такие координаты? Ответ — следующая часть.Unlike one-hot, the axes are no longer one-word-per-axis. Related words share directions — the space gains a shape. The question: where do these coordinates come from? Next part answers it.

The wish, stated as a definition. We want a function from words to a dense vector of a few hundred real numbers, with one property above all: cosine similarity should track meaning. Cat and dog should land close; cat and airplane far apart. Dense is the key word — instead of one isolated axis per word, related words share directions, and the space acquires a geometry we can navigate. The obvious objection is that we cannot hand-author hundreds of numbers per word for a million-word vocabulary. We need to learn them, and the next part shows from what.
“
You shall know a word
by the company it keeps.
J. R. Firth, 1957 · the distributional hypothesis
The single idea that makes the whole lecture possible, from the linguist John Rupert Firth in 1957. You shall know a word by the company it keeps. If two words tend to appear in the same kinds of contexts — surrounded by the same neighbouring words — they tend to mean similar things. Doctor and physician share contexts; so do cat and dog. This converts an impossible problem, defining meaning, into a tractable one: count and model the contexts each word appears in. Everything from word2vec to modern language models is, at heart, a way to cash in this hypothesis.

Дистрибутивная гипотезаThe distributional hypothesis

смысл = распределение контекстовmeaning = distribution of contexts
ПринципPrinciple
Дистрибутивная гипотезаDistributional hypothesisHarris 1954 · Firth 1957

Слова, встречающиеся в похожих контекстах, имеют похожий смысл. Значит, смысл слова можно выучить из его соседей в большом корпусе — без словаря и без разметки.Words that occur in similar contexts have similar meanings. So a word's meaning can be learned from its neighbours in a large corpus — no dictionary, no labels.

«The ___ purred and chased the mouse» — пропуск почти наверняка cat. Контекст и есть надзор. Самоконтролируемое обучение: подпись бесплатна, она в самом тексте.“The ___ purred and chased the mouse” — the blank is almost surely cat. Context is the supervision. Self-supervised: the label is free, it lives in the text itself.

Make the hypothesis concrete and operational. The claim, due to Harris in 1954 and Firth in 1957, is that distributional similarity implies semantic similarity. The practical gift is self-supervision: we never need a human to label what a word means. The sentence the blank purred and chased the mouse tells us, for free, that the blank behaves like cat. Every context in a billion-word corpus is a tiny free training example. word2vec, next, turns exactly this into a learning objective with a sliding window over raw text.

Preview the two families so the structure is clear. Часть 02Part 02

From Words to Coordinates

Гипотезу мы приняли — теперь её надо заработать:We accepted the hypothesis — now we earn it: обучающая цель, которая превращает контексты в координаты.a training objective that turns contexts into coordinates.

My climb. The distributional hypothesis tells us where meaning comes from — contexts — but not how to turn that into numbers. word2vec, from Mikolov and colleagues in 2013, supplies the missing objective: predict a word's neighbours, and the act of getting good at that prediction forces semantically similar words to acquire similar vectors. The image is my metaphor: words pour through a sliding window and fall onto a coordinate grid. This is the precise moment text becomes geometry.

Set up the skip-gram model before the formula. We slide a window over the corpus. At each position one word is the centre and the words around it, within radius m, are its contexts. The task is to predict those context words from the centre. Crucially every word carries two vectors: one used when it is the centre, written v sub w, and one used when it is a context word, written u sub c. Keeping them separate makes the math clean and the dot products meaningful. One window position over brown yields four centre-context training pairs.

The word2vec punchline, derived as one moving story instead of three static cards. Step zero: the skip-gram objective is a softmax — the probability of context c given centre w is the exponentiated dot-product score over the exponentiated scores of every word in the vocabulary. Step one: the numerator is one cheap dot product, but watch the denominator light up red — it sums over all V words. Step two: that normaliser costs on the order of V, around a million, dot products on every single update, so we literally strike it out. Step three: we replace the whole multi-class softmax with a binary logistic test — make the true pair's sigmoid go toward one, and draw k random negatives, typically five to twenty, sampled in proportion to frequency to the three-quarters power, and push theirs toward zero. Cost drops from order V to order k, five orders of magnitude. Use the arrows to step through the algebra.

Часть 03Part 03

Meaning Becomes Arithmetic

Координаты заработаны — и тут сюрприз:The coordinates are earned — and then the surprise: направления в пространстве кодируют отношения. Можно складывать смыслы.directions in the space encode relations. You can do arithmetic on meaning.

The reveal. We trained only to predict neighbours, yet something we never asked for fell out: directions in the space turn out to encode relations. The vector that takes you from man to woman is, near enough, the same vector that takes you from king to queen. That means we can add and subtract meanings. king minus man plus woman lands next to queen. The picture is the parallelogram of arrows we are about to read with real numbers.

Линейка карты: косинус против углаThe ruler of the map: cosine vs angle

подвигайте угол \(\theta\) · Сэр Косинус, живьёмdrag the angle \(\theta\) · Sir Cosine, live

\(\cos(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{b}}{\lVert\mathbf{a}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert}\in[-1,1]\) смотрит на угол, не длину. \(\theta=0\Rightarrow\cos=1\) (синонимы), \(\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Rightarrow 0\) (не связаны), \(\theta=\pi\Rightarrow -1\). Сэр Косинус из L2 — линейка этой карты.\(\cos(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{b}}{\lVert\mathbf{a}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert}\in[-1,1]\) reads the angle, not the length. \(\theta=0\Rightarrow\cos=1\) (synonyms), \(\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\Rightarrow 0\) (unrelated), \(\theta=\pi\Rightarrow -1\). Sir Cosine from L2 is the ruler of this map.

Make the ruler tactile before any numbers. The curve is cosine of the angle theta as theta opens from zero to pi. Drag the slider: at theta zero the two vectors point the same way and the cosine reads one — that is what synonyms look like. At ninety degrees the cosine is zero — orthogonal, unrelated. At a hundred and eighty degrees it is minus one — opposite. Cosine similarity is the dot product normalised by both lengths, so it sees only the angle and ignores magnitude — exactly what we want for meaning, since a rare word and a frequent word can still be synonyms. This is Sir Cosine, the same ruler from Lecture 2, now ruling the whole map of meaning. The static table of real cosines comes next.

When the Map Curves

Линейной складки мало:A linear fold is not enough: данные лежат на изогнутом многообразии. Сохраняем соседей, а не глобальную геометрию.data lies on a curved manifold. Preserve neighbours, not global geometry.

PCA is linear: it can only fold along straight directions. But real embedding data often lies on a curved sheet — a manifold — twisting through the high-dimensional space, and a flat projection can crush distant-but-aligned points together or tear neighbours apart. The fix is a different goal entirely: instead of preserving global geometry, preserve each point's local neighbourhood. Keep friends next to friends, and let the global layout bend however it must. That is the philosophy behind t-SNE and UMAP, which we meet next.

t-SNE: сохранить соседейpreserve neighbours

Каждой паре точек в высокой размерности приписывается вероятность быть соседями. t-SNE ищет 2-D раскладку, в которой эти вероятности совпадают — близкое остаётся близким.Each pair of high-D points gets a probability of being neighbours. t-SNE finds a 2-D layout whose neighbour probabilities match — near stays near.

Ключевой рычаг — perplexity: сколько соседей считать «близкими». В наших данных perplexity \(= 14\). Тяжёлые хвосты \(t\)-распределения в 2-D разводят кластеры, борясь со скучиванием.The key dial is perplexity: how many neighbours count as “near”. In our data perplexity \(= 14\). The heavy-tailed \(t\)-distribution in 2-D spreads clusters apart, fighting crowding.

t-SNE, from van der Maaten and Hinton in 2008, takes the neighbour-preserving route. In the high-dimensional space it converts distances into probabilities: how likely is point j to be picked as a neighbour of point i. Then it lays points out in two dimensions and adjusts them until the low-dimensional neighbour probabilities match the high-dimensional ones, minimising a Kullback-Leibler divergence. Two knobs matter. Perplexity sets the effective number of neighbours each point pays attention to — fourteen in our run. And using a heavy-tailed Student-t distribution in the plane gives faraway points room, which is what produces those clean, well-separated clusters.

UMAP, from McInnes, Healy and Melville in 2018, is the sibling you will reach for most often in practice. It works on the same neighbour-graph principle but is grounded in a topological construction, runs much faster on large datasets, and tends to preserve more of the global structure than t-SNE. Its main dial, n_neighbors, plays the role perplexity plays in t-SNE. The practical guidance: use UMAP when you have a lot of points or care about the coarse layout, t-SNE when you want the finest local separation. And remember both are visualisation tools — you look at their output, you do not feed it into a downstream model.

Don't Over-read the Map

Красивая картинка умеет лгать:The pretty picture can mislead: на t-SNE расстояния, размеры кластеров и зазоры между ними — не глобальны.on a t-SNE plot, distances, cluster sizes and gaps are not global.

The catch beat, and it matters because t-SNE plots are so seductive. The cluster picture we just drew is honest about who is whose neighbour, but it is a mirage about almost everything else. The size of a cluster, the distance between two clusters, the empty space between them — none of that carries reliable meaning, because t-SNE is free to rescale locally to avoid crowding. Treat the plot as a statement about neighbourhoods, not a metric map. The next slide makes the three specific traps explicit.

The specific traps, the P7 anti-over-reading beat. Trap one: cluster sizes are meaningless — t-SNE expands dense regions and contracts sparse ones, so a big blob is not a high-variance group. Trap two: the distance between two clusters is not reliable — two clusters drawn far apart can be closer in the original space than two drawn near. Trap three, the big one: perplexity changes the picture qualitatively; at perplexity fourteen you may see seven islands, at fifty they may merge, and neither is the truth on its own. The discipline is simple — read it as a neighbourhood claim, run several perplexities, and never quote a distance off a t-SNE plot.

The Same Trick Everywhere

«Слова, которые встречаются вместе, близки» — это не про слова.“Things that occur together sit near” — this was never about words. Дай любой области последовательность токенов — и skip-gram строит карту.Give any field a sequence of tokens and skip-gram draws its map.

A short interlude before the payoff, and the headline transfer of the whole lecture. Nothing in word2vec knew that its tokens were words. The cross-domain table — the headline of this lecture's second half.
