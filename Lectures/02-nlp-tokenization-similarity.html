Пиксели интерполируются; символы — нетPixels interpolate; symbols do not

Препятствие 1 · дискретностьObstacle 1 · discreteness

Серый ровно между чёрным и белым осмыслен; «слово посередине между cat и dog» — нет. У символов нет собственной метрики — в этом и есть главная мотивация обучения представлений.A grey halfway between black and white is meaningful; a "word halfway between cat and dog" is not. Symbols have no inherent metric — that is the core motivation for representation learning.

This is the deepest obstacle. Pixel space is continuous and already has a metric; you can interpolate and the result is valid. Symbol space is discrete: "cat" and "dog" share no letters with their meanings, and there is no valid point "between" them. So we cannot read distance off the strings — we must manufacture a vector space where similar meanings sit close. Hold that thought; it is exactly Part 4.

Ambiguity is not an edge case; it is the normal state of language. The Winograd schema is the cleanest demo: a single adjective flip moves the referent. No purely lexical system can solve it — you need a model of the world (big things don't fit in small containers). This is why "understanding" is hard and why context-sensitive embeddings matter later.

Закон Ципфа: несколько слов делают всю работуZipf's law: a few words do all the work

Препятствие 3 · длинный хвостObstacle 3 · the long tail

Частота \(r\)-го по распространённости слова \(\propto r^{-1}\). На 20 Newsgroups (~3.7 M токенов) эмпирический наклон в log–log равен −1.02: топ‑10 слов покрывают 20.6% корпуса, топ‑100 — 47.3%, а топ‑1000 — 71.4% — оставляя огромный хвост слов, встреченных единожды (hapax legomena).The frequency of the \(r\)-th most common word is \(\propto r^{-1}\). On 20 Newsgroups (~3.7 M tokens), the empirical log–log slope is −1.02: the top 10 words cover 20.6% of the corpus, the top 100 cover 47.3%, and the top 1000 cover 71.4% — leaving a vast tail of words seen once (hapax legomena).

Zipf is steep. "the", "of", "and" eat enormous probability mass; then a power-law tail of rare words, most of which appear only once in a given corpus. The slope and coverage numbers cited here are not invented — they are computed by `_research/data/zipf.py` on sklearn's 20 Newsgroups corpus (public domain, headers/footers/quotes stripped). Empirical slope on the top-1000 is −1.018, which is textbook Zipf within rounding. Practical consequence: you can never collect enough text to see every word "enough" times. This is the empirical setup for the punchline two slides on.

Закон Хипса: словарь никогда не насыщаетсяHeaps' law: the vocabulary never saturates

Аргумент в пользу сабвордовThe argument for subwords

По мере чтения текста \(N\) число различных типов \(V\) продолжает расти как \(V \propto N^{\beta}\) — сублинейно, но неограниченно. Измерено на 3.66 M токенов 20 Newsgroups: \(\beta \approx 0.59\), всё ещё 94 287 различных типов и растёт (\(R^2=0.998\)). Никакой конечный список слов не покрывает всё.As you read more text \(N\), the number of distinct types \(V\) keeps growing as \(V \propto N^{\beta}\) — sublinear, but unbounded. Measured on 3.66 M tokens of 20 Newsgroups: \(\beta \approx 0.59\), still 94 287 distinct types and climbing (\(R^2=0.998\)). No finite word list ever covers everything.

Heaps and Zipf are two views of the same fact. The punchline — say it loud: a fixed WORD vocabulary can never cover real text. New names, typos, compounds, hashtags, code identifiers arrive forever. That is the direct, non-negotiable argument for SUBWORD tokenization: bound the vocabulary, and rebuild any unseen word from pieces. Everything in Part 2 follows from this slide. Numbers grounded by _research/data/heaps.py: β≈0.59, K≈12.7, R²=0.998, 94 287 types on 3.66 M tokens of 20 Newsgroups (same corpus as the Zipf slide — the type counts match).

The two classic ways to normalize word forms (after the character-level normalization on the previous slide). The payoff is the same — a search for "running" should match "ran" — which is exactly the lexical-gap problem from Lecture 1. Key modern caveat: deep models rarely stem or lemmatize; subword tokenization plus learned embeddings absorb morphology, so this lives mostly on the classical-IR/BM25 side of the course.

You cannot win on all three corners. Push toward characters: vocabulary shrinks to ~256 bytes and OOV vanishes, but sequences blow up ~4–5×, which is brutal under the O(n²) attention cost we define in the transformers lecture (L6). Push toward words: sequences are short, but the vocabulary is unbounded (Heaps) and OOV is severe. Subword sits in the interior — a bounded vocab of 30k–200k, moderate sequence length, near-zero OOV. Bigger vocab → fewer tokens per text → shorter sequences and lower cost, but a bigger embedding matrix and softmax.

A short, sharp coda. The measures are clean in 2D; in the hundreds-to-thousands of dimensions where real embeddings live, three effects quietly degrade them. The knights' nemesis appears here — the Curse-of-Dimensionality Wraith, who flattens every distance until far and near look the same. Knowing these three effects separates someone who can plug in cosine from someone who can debug a retrieval system.

Концентрация расстоянийDistance concentration

проклятие размерностиcurse of dimensionality

С ростом \(d\to\infty\) разброс попарных расстояний схлопывается: ближайшая и дальняя точки становятся почти равноудалёнными, и «ближайший сосед» теряет смысл (Beyer et al. 1999). Затрагивает все \(L_p\). Это для неструктурированных/i.i.d.-данных; реальные эмбеддинги лежат на низкоразмерном многообразии с кластерной структурой (низкая внутренняя размерность), где контраст сохраняется — поэтому ANN-поиск всё ещё работает.As \(d\to\infty\), the spread of pairwise distances collapses: nearest and farthest points become almost equidistant, so "nearest neighbour" loses meaning (Beyer et al. 1999). Affects all \(L_p\). This is for unstructured/i.i.d. data; real embeddings live on a low-dimensional manifold with cluster structure (low intrinsic dimension), where contrast survives — which is why ANN retrieval still works.

Concentration of measure: in very high dimensions, the histogram of distances from a query to all points narrows to a spike. The ratio (max−min)/min tends to zero, so the nearest neighbour is barely closer than the farthest — and tiny perturbations flip which point "wins". This is why high-d retrieval is fragile and why lower-p norms (Aggarwal 2001) retain a bit more contrast (though Mirkes 2020 shows fractional norms don't truly cure it).

ХабностьHubness

несколько точек в kNN-списке почти у всехa few points in everyone's kNN list

В высокой размерности \(d\) распределение k-вхождений становится право-скошенным: несколько хабов попадают в список ближайших соседей почти у всех; анти-хабы — ни у кого (Radovanović et al. 2010). Механизм — та же концентрация с прошлого слайда: точки ближе к среднему данных имеют систематически меньшее ожидаемое расстояние до всех остальных, поэтому с ростом \(d\) их избыточно засчитывают в соседи — именно поэтому помогают центрирование / отбеливание.In high \(d\), the distribution of k-occurrences becomes right-skewed: a few hubs appear in almost everyone's nearest-neighbour list; anti-hubs in none (Radovanović et al. 2010). The mechanism is the previous slide's concentration: points lying closer to the data mean have systematically smaller expected distance to all others, so as \(d\) grows they get over-counted as neighbours — which is exactly why centering / whitening helps.

A subtler high-d pathology than concentration. Some points become "popular" neighbours purely because of geometry — they sit in many kNN lists regardless of true relevance — while others are never retrieved. Mechanism (tie it to concentration): points nearer the global centroid have a smaller expected distance to every other point, so as dimension grows they win kNN membership disproportionately — hubness is a consequence of distance concentration, not an unrelated pathology, which is why centering/whitening the cloud reduces it. This skews kNN classification, retrieval, and recommendation. Fixes rescale local geometry: mutual proximity, local/global scaling, mutual-kNN graphs. Worth naming so students recognize the symptom (the same handful of documents keep getting returned).

Анизотропия контекстных эмбеддинговAnisotropy of contextual embeddings

узкий конус раздувает косинусa narrow cone inflates cosine

Представления BERT / GPT-2 занимают узкий конус — особенно в последних слоях (крайне выражено в финальном слое GPT-2, куда слабее в BERT и в нижних слоях) — так что два случайных токена уже имеют высокий косинус (Ethayarajh 2019). Важно, из какого слоя вы читаете эмбеддинги; сырой косинус завышен. Исправление: отбеливание / стандартизация или контрастное обучение.BERT / GPT-2 representations occupy a narrow cone — especially in the last layers (extreme in GPT-2's final layer, far milder in BERT and in lower layers) — so two random tokens already have high cosine (Ethayarajh 2019). Which layer you read embeddings from matters; raw cosine is inflated. Fix: whitening / standardization, or contrastive training. (Контекстные эмбеддинги из BERT/GPT-2 и контрастное обучение формально вводятся в L5–L6 — здесь мы лишь отмечаем их влияние на косинус.)(Contextual embeddings from BERT/GPT-2 and contrastive learning are formally introduced in L5–L6 — here we only flag their effect on cosine.)

The embeddings themselves are the problem here. Contextual reps from BERT/GPT-2 are squeezed into a narrow cone in space, so even unrelated tokens sit at a small angle and cosine reports them as "similar". The effect is layer-dependent: lower layers are fairly isotropic, anisotropy is severe mainly in the upper layers — extreme in GPT-2's final layer (average random-token cosine near 1.0), much milder in BERT — so the choice of which layer you pool from is itself a calibration decision. This makes raw cosine on out-of-the-box LM embeddings misleading. Remedies push the cloud toward isotropy: whitening, BERT-flow, standardization, or contrastive objectives (sentence-transformers). Practical lesson: don't trust raw cosine on hidden states without calibration.

«Косинус и евклидово расстояние всегда ранжируют одинаково — и косинус всегда „про близость“».“Cosine and Euclidean always rank the same — and cosine is always 'about similarity'.”

Они совпадают только на L2-нормированных векторах. Чистый контрпример: запрос \(q=(1,0)\); кандидаты \(a=(0.9,0)\) и \(c=(2,0)\). У обоих \(\cos=1\) с \(q\) (косинус не может их различить), но евклидово даёт \(0.1\) против \(1.0\) — оно строго предпочитает \(a\). И Steck et al. (2024): для определённых линейных / регуляризованных моделей матричной факторизации, чья целевая функция обладает симметрией масштабирования, диагональная «калибровка» оставляет предсказания скалярного произведения неизменными, но делает значения косинуса произвольными — так что косинус там зависит от калибровки. Это не «косинус бессмыслен для всех эмбеддингов»: модели, обученные напрямую под косинус (например, sentence-transformers), фиксируют калибровку и работают нормально. Исправление: обучать под косинус, либо предпочесть скалярное произведение, либо сначала отбелить.They agree only on L2-normalized vectors. Clean counterexample: query \(q=(1,0)\); candidates \(a=(0.9,0)\) and \(c=(2,0)\). Both have \(\cos=1\) with \(q\) (cosine cannot separate them), but Euclidean gives \(0.1\) vs \(1.0\) — it strictly prefers \(a\). And Steck et al. (2024): for certain linear / regularized matrix-factorization models whose objective has a rescaling symmetry, a diagonal "gauge" leaves dot-product predictions unchanged while making cosine values arbitrary — so cosine there is gauge-dependent. This is not "cosine is meaningless for all embeddings": models trained directly against cosine (e.g. sentence-transformers) fix the gauge and are fine. Fix: train against cosine, or prefer dot, or whiten first.

Two myths in one. First the ranking myth: q=(1,0), a=(0.9,0), c=(2,0) — same direction so cosine ties at 1.0, but Euclidean is 0.1 vs 1.0 and strictly prefers a. Magnitude, not angle, decided. Always L2-normalize first if you want them to agree. Second, the deeper Steck 2024 critique: in a regularized linear MF model whose objective has a rescaling symmetry, you can rescale dimensions (a diagonal gauge) without changing dot-product predictions, yet cosine similarities change arbitrarily — so "cosine of learned embeddings" is not well-defined unless you control the gauge. Scope it for students: this is about that family of linear/regularized models, NOT a blanket "never trust cosine" — models trained directly against a cosine objective (sentence-transformers) pin the gauge and are safe. Good class debate; the 2026 rebuttal argues normalization removes the freedom.

The deployment reality. FAISS returns squared L2 (cheaper, same ordering) and has no cosine metric — you L2-normalize and use inner product. The single most common production bug is forgetting to normalize, which silently turns your "cosine" search into dot-product search that rewards long vectors. And always match the index metric to the model's training objective; a cosine-trained sentence model behaves oddly under raw dot. Numerics: guard the zero vector with ε, smooth KL's zero bins.
