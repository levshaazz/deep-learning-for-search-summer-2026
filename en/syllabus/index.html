The Star Catalog · Build the inverted index so any record is found instantly; BM25 is the trusty old sextant; Rank Fusion (RRF) fuses two rankers' votes into one robust order. · Open slides 		Week 2 · session 1
04 Shannon Entropy — the entropy of language
How Much Is a Letter Worth? · From the telegraph to the language model. Information is surprise; entropy H = −Σ p log₂ p is expected surprise and the hard floor of compression. English carries ~1 bit per letter (Shannon's guessing game) — and that floor is exactly what a language model's cross-entropy loss chases. IDF is Shannon self-information. · Open slides 		Week 2 · session 2
05 Ranking Metrics (Recall@k, Precision@k, MRR, MAP, nDCG)
The Proving Grounds · Two systems — which is better? Build an honest scoreboard (relevance judgments + metrics); Goodhart shows how a metric, once a target, starts to lie. · Open slides 		Week 3 · session 1

The Map of Meaning · Words get coordinates — meaning becomes a place you can travel. Dimensionality reduction folds the great map small enough to carry. · Open slides 		Week 4 · session 2

The Council of Attention · The crew learns who to listen to (attention); contrastive learning finally cages the Lexical Gremlin. · Open slides 		Week 5 · session 1
08 BERT & other Transformers — from attention to RLHF
The Loom of Attention · The foundations under dense & contextual retrieval: the arc from RNN bottlenecks through attention and the Transformer block to BERT, GPT, decoding, efficient attention, and RLHF. Intuition, formulas, and worked examples — bilingual RU/EN. · Open slides 		Week 5 · session 2
09 The Wiring Diagram — neural circuit diagrams: the axes, and the bill
The Wiring Diagram · A formula tells you what to compute; it hides WHAT IT COSTS and WHICH AXIS IT EATS. A circuit diagram shows both: a wire is an axis, a cup is a contraction where an axis dies, a triangle is a softmax across an axis — and the cost of every glyph is one counting rule away. Read the bill off the picture: attention is only 10% of a BERT block at n=512, and 88% at n=32768, with the crossover at exactly n = 6d. Then meet the bug that no shape check can ever catch. · Open slides 		Week 6 · session 1
10 Bi-encoders (DPR, SBERT) · Cross-encoders & Reranking · Multi-stage Pipelines
Scouts and Judges · Fast scouts (bi-encoders) sweep wide; careful judges (cross-encoders) deliberate on the few. The neural cascade is born. · Open slides 		Week 6 · session 2
11 The Crucible of Negatives — hard negative mining in contrastive dense retrieval
The Crucible of Negatives · Same code, a different blade: a dense retriever is forged by the opponents it trains against. Easy negatives teach nothing, the hardest are often impostors — effectiveness lives in a narrow hard-but-honest band. · Open slides 		Week 8 · session 1
12 Late Interaction (ColBERT) · SPLADE · Hybrid · Learning to Rank
The Alliance · Sparse and dense armies unite under one banner (hybrid); Learning to Rank trains the captain to order the host. (Rank Fusion now lives in L3.) · Open slides 		Week 8 · session 2
13 ANN: HNSW, IVF, PQ · FAISS · Vector DBs · Production
Hyperspace Lanes · Linear scan dies at galaxy scale — open hyperspace lanes (HNSW); quantization compresses the star-maps (PQ, and the modern data-oblivious TurboQuant); production keeps the Ship fast. · Open slides 		Week 9 · session 2
14 The Curved Map — geometry of meaning: anisotropy & hubness
The Curved Map · Why "similar" vectors sometimes behave badly. Embedding spaces are distorted maps: contextual vectors crowd into a narrow cone (anisotropy — random words look 0.99-similar), and a few points sit in everyone's neighbour list (hubness). Cosine lies. The fix: re-project the map (center → whiten → contrastive isotropy) and tax the crowded hubs (CSLS). · Open slides 		Week 10 · session 2
15 RAG Fundamentals · Chunking · Query Understanding & Rewriting
The Oracle · The Ship stops merely finding and starts answering: retrieve, then speak. Query rewriting hears what the captain really asked. · Open slides 		Week 11 · session 1
16 RAG Evaluation (RAGAS, LLM-as-judge) · Agentic RAG (ReAct, Self-RAG, CRAG)
Judging the Oracle · How do you grade an Oracle that sounds confident either way? Goodhart returns; the agentic loop makes the Ship critique itself. · Open slides 		Week 11 · session 2
17 The Artificer's Quill — advanced query rewriting & decomposition
The Artificer's Quill · The user's words are almost never the archive's words. HyDE, step-back, multi-query & RRF, decomposition, and the trainable rewriter reshape the query to close three gaps — vocabulary, specificity, compositionality. Change the query, not the retriever. · Open slides 		Week 12 · session 2
18 Late Chunking — contextual chunk embeddings for long documents
Read the Whole Book First · Chunk-then-embed severs global context: a chunk whose subject is a pronoun loses its referent. Late chunking embeds the WHOLE document first (full-document attention resolves the coreference), then pools per chunk — one vector per chunk, free at query time. Change WHEN you pool, not the model. · Open slides 		Week 13 · session 1
19 Search in Russian — morphology strikes back
Морфология наносит ответный удар · English-tuned pipelines quietly break on a language where one lemma wears dozens of endings: stemming vs lemmatization, BM25 over rich morphology, tokenizer fertility, and what carries over to dense retrieval. · Open slides 		Week 13 · session 2
20 Advanced RAG (multi-hop, GraphRAG) · Multimodal (CLIP, ColPali) · Ethics & Safety
The Deep Field · The longest quest: multi-hop reasoning, GraphRAG's constellation map, learning to see (not only read), and the captain's responsibility. · Open slides 		Week 14 · session 1
21 Jev — ranking, calibrated probabilities and decisions
The Decision Desk · Supplementary module: typed judgments, expected gain, probability calibration and cost-sensitive RAG decisions. Worked synthetic examples and an offline lab; no invented model benchmark. · Open slides 		Supplementary · recommended after L16 · no scheduled date
