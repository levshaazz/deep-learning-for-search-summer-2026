Papers & references

The course bibliography · every cited work · grouped by area · with a real link and why it is here

Every work cited anywhere in the course — papers, textbooks and tools — with a real canonical link (arXiv, DOI or publisher) and a short note on why it earns its place. Grouped by area, ordered the way the course meets them. This is the bibliography the lectures, the Book and the syllabus reading list all draw from.

221 of 221 works

Classical IR

Probabilistic lineage, term weighting, fusion & link authority — the road to BM25.
  • Maron & Kuhns (1960) · On Relevance, Probabilistic Indexing and Information Retrieval · Journal of the ACM

    The founding paper of probabilistic IR — relevance as a probability to be estimated. It opens the lineage that L3 traces all the way to BM25.

  • Spärck Jones (1972) · A Statistical Interpretation of Term Specificity and Its Application in Retrieval · Journal of Documentation

    The origin of inverse document frequency (IDF): rare terms are more informative. Every TF·IDF and BM25 scorer in L3 inherits this idea.

  • Salton, Wong & Yang (1975) · A Vector Space Model for Automatic Indexing · Communications of the ACM

    Introduces the vector space model and cosine similarity for documents — the bridge from term counts (L3) to the geometric view of embeddings (L2).

  • Robertson & Spärck Jones (1976) · Relevance Weighting of Search Terms (the Binary Independence Model / RSJ weight) · Journal of the American Society for Information Science

    Formalises the Binary Independence Model and the RSJ term-weighting formula — the probabilistic backbone that BM25 later approximates.

  • Robertson, van Rijsbergen & Porter (1981) · Probabilistic Models of Indexing and Searching · Information Retrieval Research (eds. Oddy et al.), Butterworths, ch.4, pp.35–56

    The 2-Poisson / eliteness idea — a term's frequency behaves differently in documents that are 'about' it — is the conceptual seed of BM25's saturation curve taught in L3.

  • Robertson & Walker (1994) · Some Simple Effective Approximations to the 2-Poisson Model for Probabilistic Weighted Retrieval (Okapi BM25 at TREC-2/3) · SIGIR 1994

    Introduces Okapi BM25 — the ranking function you implement by hand in the Lab and Homework. The single most important classical scorer in the course.

  • Singhal, Buckley & Mitra (1996) · Pivoted Document Length Normalization · SIGIR 1996

    Shows why long documents need length normalization and how to pivot it — the direct motivation for BM25's b parameter studied in the Homework.

  • Page, Brin, Motwani & Winograd (1998) · The PageRank Citation Ranking: Bringing Order to the Web · Stanford InfoLab Technical Report

    Links as votes: query-independent authority from the web graph. L3 teaches PageRank as the canonical link-analysis signal alongside content scoring.

  • Lavrenko & Croft (2001) · Relevance-Based Language Models · SIGIR 2001

    Relevance-Based Language Models are the language-modeling foundation of pseudo-relevance feedback — a family parallel to (not descended from) vector-space Rocchio. L14's classical baseline in Act 1.

  • Zhai & Lafferty (2001) · A Study of Smoothing Methods for Language Models Applied to Ad Hoc Information Retrieval · SIGIR 2001, pp. 334–342

    Shows that in query-likelihood retrieval with collection smoothing the collection probability p(q|C) lands in the denominator and plays a role very similar to IDF — the IDF effect emerges from smoothing. Backs L4's KL-retrieval slide.

  • Abdul-Jaleel et al. (2004) · UMass at TREC 2004: Novelty and HARD (RM3) · TREC 2004

    RM3 — the canonical pseudo-relevance-feedback method (relevance model + query interpolation). The classical query-expansion baseline whose ceiling (it cannot invent missing vocabulary) L14 works by hand in Act 1.

  • Cormack, Clarke & Büttcher (2009) · Reciprocal Rank Fusion Outperforms Condorcet and Individual Rank Learning Methods · SIGIR 2009

    Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF): a parameter-light way to merge ranked lists that you implement in the Homework to fuse BM25 and TF·IDF.

  • Robertson & Zaragoza (2009) · The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond · Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval

    The definitive survey of BM25 and its variants. The reference text behind the L3 ranking material and a Homework A1 paper-reading option.

  • Thakur et al. (2021) · BEIR: A Heterogeneous Benchmark for Zero-shot Evaluation of Information Retrieval Models · NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks

    The zero-shot retrieval benchmark — and the source of nfcorpus/scifact used in the Lab and Homework. The standard way the course measures generalization.

Evaluation & online experimentation

Ranking metrics, test collections, significance, click bias & A/B testing.
  • Goodhart (1975) · Problems of Monetary Management: the U.K. Experience (origin of Goodhart's Law) · Papers in Monetary Economics, Reserve Bank of Australia

    Goodhart's Law — a measure that becomes a target stops being a good measure — is the recurring motif behind L1/L4's warnings about optimizing CTR or any single metric.

  • Strathern (1997) · ‘Improving Ratings’: Audit in the British University System (the crisp Goodhart's-Law phrasing) · European Review

    The source of the exact wording the course quotes for Goodhart's Law. Cited alongside Goodhart 1975 so the slogan is attributed correctly.

  • Voorhees (2000) · Variations in Relevance Judgments and the Measurement of Retrieval Effectiveness · Information Processing & Management

    Shows test-collection rankings are robust even when human judgments disagree — the empirical justification for TREC-style pooled evaluation taught in L4.

  • Järvelin & Kekäläinen (2002) · Cumulated Gain-Based Evaluation of IR Techniques (nDCG) · ACM Transactions on Information Systems

    Defines (n)DCG, the graded-relevance ranking metric used throughout L0/L3/L4 and computed by hand in the Lab and Homework.

  • Joachims (2002) · Optimizing Search Engines using Clickthrough Data · KDD 2002

    The seminal idea of mining clickthrough as implicit relevance feedback — the start of the click-as-signal thread that runs through L1 and L4.

  • Granka, Joachims & Gay (2004) · Eye-Tracking Analysis of User Behavior in WWW Search · SIGIR 2004

    The peer-reviewed eye-tracking evidence for the 'golden triangle' — users fixate the top-left of the results page. The citable academic backing the course uses in place of the Enquiro industry report.

  • Enquiro / Hotchkiss (2005) · Eye Tracking Study: ‘Google's Golden Triangle’ · Enquiro Search Solutions (industry report)

    The industry report that popularized the 'golden triangle' heat-map of search-results attention. Used in L1 as the visual; the peer-reviewed backing is Granka et al. 2004.

  • Smucker, Allan & Carterette (2007) · A Comparison of Statistical Significance Tests for Information Retrieval Evaluation · CIKM 2007

    Which significance test to trust when comparing rankers — the basis for the paired t-test / Wilcoxon / randomization choice in the Homework's significance task.

  • Craswell et al. (2008) · An Experimental Comparison of Click Position-Bias Models (the cascade model) · WSDM 2008

    Compares click models and introduces the cascade model — the formal account of why higher-ranked results get clicked more regardless of relevance (L1 position bias).

  • Moffat & Zobel (2008) · Rank-Biased Precision for Measurement of Retrieval Effectiveness (RBP) · ACM Transactions on Information Systems

    RBP models a user who keeps scanning with fixed persistence — a metric with an explicit user model, contrasted with nDCG and ERR in L4.

  • Chapelle et al. (2009) · Expected Reciprocal Rank for Graded Relevance (ERR) · CIKM 2009

    ERR: a cascade-based metric where a relevant top result discounts everything below it. Compared against nDCG in the L4 metrics zoo.

  • Sanderson (2010) · Test Collection Based Evaluation of Information Retrieval Systems · Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval

    The survey of offline IR evaluation — qrels, pooling, metrics and their pitfalls. The reference behind the L4 evaluation framework.

  • Chapelle & Chang (2011) · Yahoo! Learning to Rank Challenge Overview · JMLR Workshop and Conference Proceedings 14

    The overview of the Yahoo! LTR Challenge and its release of large web-search relevance datasets — the empirical backdrop for the learning-to-rank methods compared in L8.

  • Chapelle et al. (2012) · Large-Scale Validation and Analysis of Interleaved Search Evaluation · ACM Transactions on Information Systems

    The large-scale evidence that interleaving detects ranking differences far faster than A/B tests — the sensitivity argument L1/L4 make for interleaved evaluation.

  • Bajaj et al. (2016) · MS MARCO: A Human Generated MAchine Reading COmprehension Dataset · NeurIPS 2016 Workshop on Cognitive Computation (arXiv:1611.09268)

    The large-scale passage-ranking collection that became the standard neural-IR benchmark — its dev MRR@10 is the yardstick for almost every result in L1/L7/L8.

  • Joachims, Swaminathan & Schnabel (2017) · Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback (Inverse Propensity Scoring) · WSDM 2017

    How to learn to rank from click logs without inheriting position bias (IPS). The principled answer to the 'click logs lie' problem raised in L1/L4.

  • Kohavi, Tang & Xu (2020) · Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing · Cambridge University Press

    The practitioner's bible for online A/B testing — randomization, sample-ratio mismatch, novelty effects. The basis for L4's online-evaluation section.

  • Bonifacio et al. (2021) · mMARCO: A Multilingual Version of the MS MARCO Passage Ranking Dataset · arXiv 2021

    mMARCO is the translated MS MARCO used in L20 to train and measure Russian rankers without new annotation.

  • Zhang et al. (2023) · Making a MIRACL: Multilingual Information Retrieval Across a Continuum of Languages · TACL 2023

    MIRACL supplies human-annotated relevance for 18 languages including Russian — the evaluation ground L20 stands on.

  • Kovalev et al. (2025) · Building Russian Benchmark for Evaluation of Information Retrieval Models · arXiv 2025

    RusBEIR itself — the Russian BEIR whose BM25 baseline is a LEMMATIZED pipeline, which is L20's evidence that normalisation is the baseline, not an optimisation.

  • Kovalev et al. (2025) · Wikipedia-based Datasets in Russian Information Retrieval Benchmark RusBEIR · arXiv 2025

    The length-regime table L20 uses to overturn "dense always wins": the same content re-cut into longer windows flips the ranking between BM25 and the dense arm — a direct bridge to chunking in L10/L16.

  • Snegirev et al. (2025) · The Russian-focused embedders' exploration: ruMTEB benchmark and Russian embedding model design · NAACL 2025

    ruMTEB is the Russian embedding benchmark L20 uses to compare ru-specific and multilingual encoders on equal footing.

Search as system design

Why a model is not a system: the vocabulary gap, query intent, technical debt.
  • Furnas et al. (1987) · The Vocabulary Problem in Human-System Communication · Communications of the ACM

    Quantifies the vocabulary problem — two people pick the same word for one thing under ~20% of the time. The empirical root of L1's lexical gap that embeddings exist to close.

  • Broder (2002) · A Taxonomy of Web Search · SIGIR Forum

    The navigational / informational / transactional taxonomy of queries — the framing L1 uses to argue that intent, not keywords, defines the search problem.

  • Sculley et al. (2015) · Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems · NeurIPS 2015

    The 'ML model is the small box' paper — most of a real system is glue, data and serving, not modeling. The thesis behind L1's iceberg metaphor.

  • Covington, Adams & Sargin (2016) · Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Recommendations · RecSys 2016

    The canonical two-stage candidate-generation → ranking architecture at industrial scale — the retrieve-then-rank pattern L1 frames the whole course around.

  • Abbott (2024) · Neural Circuit Diagrams: Robust Diagrams for the Communication, Implementation, and Analysis of Deep Learning Architectures · TMLR

    The notation this course teaches once, in L6, and then simply uses: a wire is an axis, a cup is a contraction where an axis dies, a triangle is a softmax across an axis. It makes the axes — and therefore the memory bill and the shape-preserving bugs — visible in the figure instead of hidden in the prose.

NLP, tokenization & embedding geometry

Subword tokenizers, word & contextual embeddings, and high-dimensional geometry.
  • Firth (1957) · A Synopsis of Linguistic Theory, 1930–1955 ("you shall know a word by the company it keeps") · Studies in Linguistic Analysis (Special Volume of the Philological Society), Blackwell, pp. 1–32

    The origin of the distributional hypothesis — meaning is given by the contexts a word keeps. The catchphrase that opens the whole L5 embeddings arc.

  • Gage (1994) · A New Algorithm for Data Compression (the origin of Byte-Pair Encoding) · The C Users Journal

    The original Byte-Pair Encoding compression algorithm that subword tokenizers (L2) later borrowed. Cited as the historical root; Sennrich 2016 is the NLP-citable version.

  • Beyer et al. (1999) · When Is ‘Nearest Neighbor’ Meaningful? · ICDT 1999

    Proves that under broad conditions all points become nearly equidistant as dimension grows — the formal statement of the curse-of-dimensionality wraith in L2.

  • Aggarwal, Hinneburg & Keim (2001) · On the Surprising Behavior of Distance Metrics in High Dimensional Space · ICDT 2001

    Shows fractional and L1 metrics behave better than L2 in high dimensions — the quantitative warning behind L2's distance-concentration material.

  • van der Maaten & Hinton (2008) · Visualizing Data using t-SNE · Journal of Machine Learning Research

    t-SNE — the nonlinear projection that draws readable 'islands' from high-dimensional embeddings. The Cartographer's main folding tool in L5, taught with strict warnings against over-reading it.

  • Gutmann & Hyvärinen (2010) · Noise-Contrastive Estimation: A New Estimation Principle for Unnormalized Statistical Models · AISTATS 2010 (PMLR v9:297–304)

    The taproot of contrastive learning: estimate a model by learning to tell real data from noise. Every later objective L13 traces — word2vec negative sampling, InfoNCE, dense-retrieval contrastive loss — descends from this principle.

  • Radovanović et al. (2010) · Hubs in Space: Popular Nearest Neighbors in High-Dimensional Data · Journal of Machine Learning Research 11:2487–2531

    The seminal hubness paper: in high dimensions a few ‘hub’ points appear in almost everyone’s nearest-neighbour list while ‘anti-hubs’ appear in none — the L2 caption result on why high-d similarity misbehaves.

  • Schuster & Nakajima (2012) · Japanese and Korean Voice Search (the WordPiece tokenizer) · ICASSP 2012

    Introduces WordPiece — the likelihood-based subword tokenizer later used by BERT. One of the three tokenizer families compared in L2.

  • Mikolov et al. (2013) · Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space (word2vec) · ICLR 2013 (workshop)

    word2vec — the paper that made dense word embeddings mainstream and gave us 'king − man + woman ≈ queen'. The starting point of the L5 embeddings arc.

  • Mikolov et al. (2013) · Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases and their Compositionality (skip-gram with negative sampling) · NeurIPS 2013

    The companion word2vec paper that introduces negative sampling, subsampling of frequent words and the unigram^{3/4} noise distribution — the trick that made skip-gram trainable on billions of words, derived in L5.

  • Levy & Goldberg (2014) · Neural Word Embedding as Implicit Matrix Factorization · NeurIPS 2014

    Proves that skip-gram with negative sampling implicitly factorizes a shifted-PMI word-context matrix — the result that unifies L3's count-based LSA/SVD with L5's predictive word2vec.

  • Pennington, Socher & Manning (2014) · GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation · EMNLP 2014

    GloVe learns word vectors from global co-occurrence counts — the count-based counterpart to word2vec, taught alongside it in L5.

  • Perozzi, Al-Rfou & Skiena (2014) · DeepWalk: Online Learning of Social Representations · KDD 2014

    Cross-domain (graphs): treats random walks over a graph as 'sentences' and runs skip-gram to embed nodes — the cleanest transfer of the L5 word2vec idea beyond language.

  • Bahdanau et al. (2015) · Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate · ICLR

    Where attention was born: an additive alignment over encoder states lets the decoder look back at any input word. The seed of the whole L15 arc.

  • Korobov (2015) · Morphological Analyzer and Generator for Russian and Ukrainian Languages · AIST 2015

    pymorphy2 is the practical Russian morphological analyzer the L20 worked example leans on for lemmatization.

  • Schroff et al. (2015) · FaceNet: A Unified Embedding for Face Recognition and Clustering · CVPR 2015

    Triplet loss + semi-hard negative mining (margin α=0.2): the origin of 'hard negative' as a training lever, and the first warning that the hardest negatives can collapse training — L13's geometry act builds on both.

  • Ba, Kiros & Hinton (2016) · Layer Normalization · arXiv:1607.06450

    Normalizes across features within each token rather than across the batch — the normalization layer inside every Transformer block taught in L6.

  • Barkan & Koenigstein (2016) · Item2Vec: Neural Item Embedding for Collaborative Filtering · IEEE MLSP 2016

    Cross-domain (recsys): treats a basket/session of products as a 'sentence' of items and runs skip-gram to embed products — the search-adjacent transfer of word2vec featured in L5.

  • Bolukbasi et al. (2016) · Man is to Computer Programmer as Woman is to Homemaker? Debiasing Word Embeddings · NeurIPS 2016

    Shows word embeddings encode and amplify gender stereotypes through their analogy structure, and proposes a debiasing projection — the ethics hook behind L5's bias-in-embeddings material.

  • Grover & Leskovec (2016) · node2vec: Scalable Feature Learning for Networks · KDD 2016

    Cross-domain (graphs): generalizes DeepWalk with biased random walks (BFS/DFS interpolation) feeding skip-gram — the node-embedding work L5 uses to show 'a random walk is a sentence.'

  • He et al. (2016) · Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition (ResNet) · CVPR 2016

    Residual (skip) connections that let very deep networks train — the idea the Transformer reuses as add-&-norm around each sublayer, cited in L6.

  • Linzen (2016) · Issues in Evaluating Semantic Spaces Using Word Analogies · ACL 2016 RepEval Workshop

    Shows the famous 'king − man + woman ≈ queen' analogy is partly an artifact of excluding the query words, and that analogy accuracy overstates real structure — the honest counterpoint to L5's headline result.

  • Sennrich, Haddow & Birch (2016) · Neural Machine Translation of Rare Words with Subword Units (BPE for NLP) · ACL 2016

    Brings Byte-Pair Encoding into NLP as subword tokenization for rare words — the citable origin of the BPE tokenizer studied in L2 (and a tokenization-study option in the Homework).

  • Sohn (2016) · Improved Deep Metric Learning with Multi-class N-pair Loss Objective · NeurIPS 2016

    Generalises the triplet loss to one positive against N−1 negatives inside a single softmax — the formal ancestor of the in-batch-negatives trick that DPR and every modern dense retriever rely on.

  • Wattenberg, Viégas & Johnson (2016) · How to Use t-SNE Effectively · Distill

    The canonical warning against over-reading t-SNE plots — cluster sizes and distances are not meaningful and hyperparameters change everything. The anchor of L5’s ‘t-SNE mirage’ arc.

  • Wu et al. (2016) · Google's Neural Machine Translation System · arXiv

    Seq2seq + attention shipped at production scale; the single-context bottleneck it fought motivated the Transformer a year later.

  • Bojanowski et al. (2017) · Enriching Word Vectors with Subword Information (fastText) · Transactions of the ACL

    fastText represents a word as the sum of its character n-grams, so it can embed out-of-vocabulary and morphologically rich words — the bridge from L2 subword tokenization to L5 word embeddings.

  • Caliskan, Bryson & Narayanan (2017) · Semantics Derived Automatically from Language Corpora Contain Human-like Biases (WEAT) · Science

    The Word-Embedding Association Test (WEAT): a statistical measure showing embeddings reproduce documented human implicit biases. The quantitative backbone of L5's bias discussion.

  • Christiano et al. (2017) · Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences · NeurIPS

    The origin of RLHF: learn a reward model from human preference comparisons, then optimize a policy against it — the recipe L15 ends on.

  • Vaswani et al. (2017) · Attention Is All You Need (the Transformer) · NeurIPS 2017

    The Transformer architecture that underlies every modern embedding and ranking model in the second half of the course. Taught in L6.

  • Conneau et al. (2018) · Word Translation Without Parallel Data (MUSE; CSLS) · ICLR

    Introduces CSLS = 2·cos − r_T − r_S, a local-crowding tax that subtracts each point's neighbourhood density to defeat hubness; word-translation P@1 42.6 → 66.1. The hubness fix in L18.

  • Kudo (2018) · Subword Regularization: Improving NMT Models with Multiple Subword Candidates (Unigram LM) · ACL 2018

    The Unigram language-model tokenizer and subword regularization — the third major subword scheme in L2, contrasted with BPE and WordPiece.

  • Kudo & Richardson (2018) · SentencePiece: A Simple and Language-Independent Subword Tokenizer and Detokenizer for Neural Text Processing · EMNLP 2018 (system demonstrations)

    The tooling that made language-independent subword tokenization practical — the implementation L2 points students at when they want to run BPE/Unigram themselves.

  • McInnes, Healy & Melville (2018) · UMAP: Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection for Dimension Reduction · arXiv (also JOSS)

    UMAP — a faster manifold-learning projection (fuzzy simplicial sets, cross-entropy on a graph) that preserves more global structure than t-SNE. The companion dimensionality-reduction tool in L5.

  • Mu & Viswanath (2018) · All-but-the-Top: Simple and Effective Postprocessing for Word Representations · ICLR

    The cheapest anisotropy fix: subtract the mean and remove the top ~d/100 principal directions — which, they show, encode word FREQUENCY, not meaning. The conceptual parent of BERT-whitening (L18).

  • Peters et al. (2018) · Deep Contextualized Word Representations (ELMo) · NAACL

    Contextual embeddings from a bidirectional LM — the pre-BERT step where a word's vector finally depends on its sentence.

  • van den Oord, Li & Vinyals (2018) · Representation Learning with Contrastive Predictive Coding (InfoNCE) · arXiv:1807.03748

    Introduces the InfoNCE contrastive loss — pull a positive pair together, push in-batch negatives apart — the objective L6’s contrastive-retrieval section and L7’s bi-encoders are trained with.

  • Veličković et al. (2018) · Graph Attention Networks (GAT) · ICLR 2018

    Cross-domain (graphs): applies attention weights over a node's neighbours — the 'Council of Attention' on graph edges — illustrating L6's unifying frame that a Transformer is a GNN on a complete graph.

  • Devlin et al. (2019) · BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding · NAACL 2019

    BERT — bidirectional pre-trained contextual embeddings. The model whose anisotropy L2 dissects and the backbone of the neural rankers (SBERT, DPR, ColBERT) in L7-L8.

  • Ethayarajh (2019) · How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations? Comparing the Geometry of BERT, ELMo, and GPT-2 Embeddings (anisotropy) · EMNLP 2019

    Documents embedding anisotropy — contextual vectors crowd into a narrow cone, inflating similarities. The evidence behind L2's whitening/anisotropy discussion.

  • Gao et al. (2019) · Representation Degeneration Problem in Training Natural Language Generation Models · ICLR 2019

    Names the anisotropic 'narrow cone' that learned embeddings collapse into — exactly the degeneration that uniformity (and hard negatives) counteract. The problem the geometry deep-dive #4 expands on.

  • Gonen & Goldberg (2019) · Lipstick on a Pig: Debiasing Methods Cover up Systematic Gender Biases in Word Embeddings But do not Remove Them · NAACL 2019

    Shows that ‘debiasing’ word embeddings mostly hides bias rather than removing it — clustering still recovers gender. The cautionary result L5 cites alongside Bolukbasi and Caliskan.

  • Liu et al. (2019) · RoBERTa: A Robustly Optimized BERT Pretraining Approach · arXiv

    BERT was undertrained: more data, longer training, bigger batches, drop NSP — the recipe matters as much as the architecture.

  • Poole et al. (2019) · On Variational Bounds of Mutual Information · ICML 2019

    The careful account of InfoNCE's bound I ≥ log N − L_N: more negatives raise the mutual-information CEILING, but the bound saturates at log N — the caveat L13 states precisely instead of the loose 'InfoNCE maximises MI'.

  • Sanh et al. (2019) · DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT · arXiv

    Knowledge distillation: a smaller student mimics BERT — ~40% smaller, ~60% faster, ~97% of the performance.

  • Beltagy et al. (2020) · Longformer: The Long-Document Transformer · arXiv

    Sliding-window + a few global tokens make attention O(n) — long documents without the quadratic wall.

  • Brown et al. (2020) · Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3) · NeurIPS

    175B parameters, and in-context few-shot learning emerges: show a few examples in the prompt, no weight updates.

  • Chen et al. (2020) · A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations (SimCLR) · ICML 2020

    The NT-Xent in-batch contrastive recipe at scale: more in-batch negatives (bigger batches) help, and temperature τ matters. The visual-domain template L13's text in-batch negatives inherit.

  • Clark et al. (2020) · ELECTRA: Pre-training Text Encoders as Discriminators · ICLR

    Replaced-token detection instead of masked-LM — learn from every token, far more sample-efficient than BERT.

  • He et al. (2020) · Momentum Contrast for Unsupervised Visual Representation Learning (MoCo) · CVPR 2020

    A momentum-updated queue (K=65536) decouples the number of negatives from the batch size — the idea Contriever reuses for retrieval and GradCache makes affordable at 32K in-batch.

  • Kaplan et al. (2020) · Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models · arXiv

    Loss falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data, and compute — the empirical case for going big.

  • Lan et al. (2020) · ALBERT: A Lite BERT for Self-supervised Learning · ICLR

    Factorized embeddings + cross-layer parameter sharing shrink BERT dramatically at little accuracy cost.

  • Lewis et al. (2020) · BART: Denoising Sequence-to-Sequence Pre-training · ACL

    A denoising autoencoder encoder-decoder: corrupt text then reconstruct it — strong for summarization and generation.

  • Li et al. (2020) · On the Sentence Embeddings from Pre-trained Language Models (BERT-flow) · EMNLP

    Learns an invertible flow mapping BERT's anisotropic sentence-embedding distribution to an isotropic Gaussian (+8.16 avg STS); also shows raw cosine tracks edit-distance more than meaning. A core L18 fix.

  • Raffel et al. (2020) · Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5) · JMLR

    Cast every NLP task as text-to-text and one encoder-decoder does them all — the workhorse of the enc-dec family.

  • Stiennon et al. (2020) · Learning to Summarize from Human Feedback · NeurIPS

    RLHF applied to summarization: a reward model from human comparisons + PPO beats supervised fine-tuning.

  • Wang & Isola (2020) · Understanding Contrastive Representation Learning through Alignment and Uniformity on the Hypersphere · ICML 2020

    Decomposes the contrastive objective into alignment (positives close) and uniformity (features spread on the sphere) — the lens L13 uses to explain what hard negatives optimise, and the bridge to the geometry deep-dive on anisotropy/hubness.

  • Wang et al. (2020) · Linformer: Self-Attention with Linear Complexity · arXiv

    Project keys and values to a low rank — attention becomes linear in sequence length.

  • Zaheer et al. (2020) · Big Bird: Transformers for Longer Sequences · NeurIPS

    Window + global + random sparse attention — linear cost, keeps the theoretical power for long sequences.

  • Choromanski et al. (2021) · Rethinking Attention with Performers · ICLR

    FAVOR+ kernel features approximate softmax attention unbiasedly in linear time and memory.

  • Dosovitskiy et al. (2021) · An Image is Worth 16x16 Words: Transformers for Image Recognition at Scale (ViT) · ICLR 2021

    Cross-domain (vision): splits an image into 16x16 patches treated as tokens and runs the same transformer block — L6's flagship transfer, bridging to CLIP and ColPali in L12.

  • Gao, Yao & Chen (2021) · SimCSE: Simple Contrastive Learning of Sentence Embeddings · EMNLP 2021

    Dropout as a positive; the NLI contradiction sentence as a HARD negative (supervised STS 81.6). Also flattens the anisotropic spectrum — the cleanest worked case of a hand-labelled hard negative in L13's modern act.

  • Geva et al. (2021) · Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories · EMNLP 2021

    Reframes the Transformer's position-wise FFN as a key-value memory — the first matrix's rows are keys (pattern detectors), the second's are values retrieved when a key fires — explaining where much of a model's stored knowledge lives (L6 corner-case).

  • Rives et al. (2021) · Biological Structure and Function Emerge from Scaling Unsupervised Learning to 250 Million Protein Sequences (ESM) · PNAS

    Cross-domain (bioinformatics): pretrains a transformer on amino-acid sequences as 'sentences', so protein homology search becomes cosine nearest-neighbour over embeddings instead of BLAST alignment — the exact L5→retrieval move on proteins.

  • Su et al. (2021) · RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) · arXiv (later Neurocomputing 2024)

    RoPE rotates the query/key vectors by an angle proportional to position, so relative offset falls out of the dot product — the modern position encoding L6 contrasts with sinusoidal PE.

  • Su et al. (2021) · Whitening Sentence Representations for Better Semantics and Faster Retrieval (BERT-whitening) · arXiv

    Whitens embeddings — x̃ = (x−μ)W, W = U Λ^(−1/2) — so the covariance is identity (isotropy) and cosine is valid; STS-B 59.04 → 71.34, and whitening-256 shrinks the vector for faster retrieval. The central L18 fix.

  • Timkey & van Schijndel (2021) · All Bark and No Bite: Rogue Dimensions in Transformer Language Models Obscure Representational Quality · EMNLP 2021

    The independent replication L18 leans on: a handful of rogue dimensions dominate cosine, and standardizing them away moves a model's agreement with human similarity judgements — the reason the deck says which LAYER you read is a calibration decision.

  • Wang & Liu (2021) · Understanding the Behaviour of Contrastive Loss · CVPR 2021

    Shows the per-negative gradient is a Boltzmann distribution scaled by 1/τ — L13's central lens 'a negative is worth exactly its gradient'. τ→0 gives a nearest-negative hard-max; the uniformity–tolerance dilemma falls out of the same formula.

  • Dao et al. (2022) · FlashAttention: Fast and Memory-Efficient Exact Attention with IO-Awareness · NeurIPS 2022

    FlashAttention is an IO-aware, exact attention kernel that tiles the computation to avoid materializing the n×n matrix — the practical answer to L6's O(n²) cost wall.

  • Feng et al. (2022) · Language-agnostic BERT Sentence Embedding · ACL 2022

    LaBSE is the language-agnostic sentence encoder L20 cites as the cross-lingual baseline.

  • Kusupati et al. (2022) · Matryoshka Representation Learning · NeurIPS 2022

    Matryoshka embeddings train one model so the first k dimensions are themselves usable — truncate 768→64 for cheap ANN, keep full dim for rerank. Production-flavored, ties L6 to the L7 'search in ms' bridge.

  • Ouyang et al. (2022) · Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with Human Feedback (InstructGPT) · NeurIPS

    The SFT → reward model → PPO recipe that turned base LMs into assistants; a 1.3B InstructGPT was preferred over 175B GPT-3.

  • Rudman et al. (2022) · IsoScore: Measuring the Uniformity of Embedding Space Utilization · Findings of ACL 2022

    The measurement critique behind L18's act 02: average pairwise cosine is not an isotropy measure — one cloud read four ways gives 0.216 against 0.97-0.99 — so IsoScore is what the deck reports instead.

  • Günther et al. (2023) · Jina Embeddings 2: 8192-Token General-Purpose Text Embeddings for Long Documents · arXiv

    An 8192-token long-context embedding model (ALiBi) — the kind of encoder late chunking (L16) needs: full-document attention plus mean pooling.

  • Petrov et al. (2023) · Language Model Tokenizers Introduce Unfairness Between Languages · NeurIPS 2023

    Measures how many more tokens the same text costs in non-English languages — the token-tax result that opens L20.

  • Rafailov et al. (2023) · Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model (DPO) · NeurIPS

    Skip the RL loop: optimize human preferences directly with a simple classification loss — RLHF's simpler successor.

  • Valmeekam et al. (2023) · LLMZip: Lossless Text Compression using Large Language Models · arXiv:2306.04050

    Shannon's estimate of the entropy of English, redone with LLaMA-7B as the predictor: 0.709 bits per character on text8 — under his 1.3 upper bound, still above his 0.6 lower one. The authors' own caveat, that LLaMA has almost certainly read the Wikipedia text8 is made from, is part of the lesson.

  • Arnett et al. (2024) · A Bit of a Problem: Measurement Disparities in Dataset Sizes Across Languages · arXiv 2024

    The byte premium behind L20's token tax: the same content costs more bytes in Russian than in English before a tokenizer is even chosen, so part of the fragmentation starts in the encoding, not in the vocabulary.

  • BehnamGhader et al. (2024) · LLM2Vec: Large Language Models Are Secretly Powerful Text Encoders · COLM 2024

    LLM2Vec turns a decoder-only LLM into a text encoder with bidirectional attention plus unsupervised contrastive training — the recipe behind the decoder-based embedders at the top of MTEB (L7).

  • Chen et al. (2024) · M3-Embedding: Multi-Linguality, Multi-Functionality, Multi-Granularity Text Embeddings Through Self-Knowledge Distillation · ACL 2024 Findings

    BGE-M3 delivers dense, sparse and multi-vector retrieval from one multilingual model — the hybrid option L20 weighs for Russian.

  • Forooghi et al. (2024) · Whitening Not Recommended for Classification Tasks in LLMs · arXiv 2024

    The boundary L18 owes its whitening act: on classification the same transform degrades every model and dataset tested, and the damage grows with dimension — whitening is an STS/retrieval tool, not a universal one.

  • Godey et al. (2024) · Anisotropy Is Inherent to Self-Attention in Transformers · EACL 2024

    Kills the comfortable Zipfian explanation L18 starts from: anisotropy shows up in byte-level, character-level and speech models too, so it comes from self-attention, not from a rare-word tail.

  • Nussbaum et al. (2024) · Nomic Embed: Training a Reproducible Long Context Text Embedder · arXiv

    A fully reproducible 8192-token (RoPE) embedding model — the third long-context encoder in L16's late-chunking benchmark.

  • Rudman & Eickhoff (2024) · Stable Anisotropic Regularization · ICLR 2024

    I-STAR is L18's counterweight: tuning a regulariser over 27 settings, every optimum REDUCES isotropy — so isotropy is a diagnostic, not a target.

  • Shlegeris et al. (2024) · Language models are better than humans at next-token prediction · Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR) — arXiv:2212.11281

    Shannon's 1951 guessing game, re-run with a machine on the other side of the table: on next-token prediction the models already beat the human predictors. L17 uses it to date the moment the red line of the Fn staircase stopped belonging to us.

  • Steck, Ekanadham & Kallus (2024) · Is Cosine-Similarity of Embeddings Really About Similarity? · WWW 2024 Companion

    Shows that cosine similarity between learned embeddings can be arbitrary — regularization choices let it encode unrelated quantities — the L2 cautionary result on trusting cosine.

  • Sturua et al. (2024) · jina-embeddings-v3: Multilingual Embeddings with Task LoRA · arXiv

    A multilingual 8192-token embedder with task-specific LoRA adapters; one of the long-context models late chunking is evaluated on in L16.

  • Wang et al. (2024) · Multilingual E5 Text Embeddings: A Technical Report · arXiv 2024

    Multilingual E5 is the default multilingual encoder recommended in L20 for Russian retrieval.

  • Wang et al. (2024) · Improving Text Embeddings with Large Language Models · ACL 2024

    E5-mistral shows synthetic LLM-generated training data plus a 7B backbone is enough to top the embedding leaderboards — the cost/quality trade-off discussed in L7.

  • Zhou et al. (2024) · Length-Induced Embedding Collapse in Transformer-Based Models · arXiv

    Shows long-text embeddings collapse / over-compress — the empirical reason retrieval prefers SMALL chunks, which motivates late chunking (L16).

  • Diera et al. (2025) · Isotropy Matters: Soft-ZCA Whitening of Embeddings for Semantic Code Search · ESANN 2025

    The surviving niche for whitening in L18: soft ZCA (a shrinkage parameter between identity and full whitening) still helps semantic code search — the cure is not dead, it is dosed.

  • Günther et al. (2025) · jina-embeddings-v4: Universal Embeddings for Multimodal Multilingual Retrieval · arXiv:2506.18902

    The same team's later model, and L16's proof that the two 'lates' are not rivals: one 3.8B encoder emits BOTH a single vector and late-interaction multi-vectors, so late chunking and late interaction can ship in one checkpoint.

  • Lee et al. (2025) · NV-Embed: Improved Techniques for Training LLMs as Generalist Embedding Models · ICLR 2025

    NV-Embed's latent-attention pooling and two-stage instruction tuning are the concrete techniques that put LLM-based embedders ahead of BERT-class encoders (L7).

  • Lee et al. (2025) · Gemini Embedding: Generalizable Embeddings from Gemini · arXiv 2025

    Gemini Embedding is the proprietary end of the same trend — cited in L7's embedder roster to show the frontier is not only open-weights.

  • Lundin et al. (2025) · The Token Tax: Systematic Bias in Multilingual Tokenization · arXiv:2509.05486

    Quantifies how multilingual tokenizers fragment non-English text into more tokens — a systematic ‘tax’ on cost and quality. The L2 tokenization-fairness reference.

  • Ren et al. (2025) · Correcting Mean Bias in Text Embeddings: A Refined Renormalization with Training-Free Improvements on MMTEB · arXiv 2025 (preprint)

    The dose-response curve L18 quotes for modern encoders on MMTEB: removing the mean direction and one or two all-but-the-top components helps, full PCA whitening hurts. Cited as a PREPRINT, as the deck marks it.

  • Tsukagoshi & Sasano (2025) · Redundancy, Isotropy, and Intrinsic Dimensionality of Prompt-based Text Embeddings · Findings of ACL 2025

    The 2025 reading L18 ends its act on: deployed prompt-based embedders still score low on IsoScore and carry heavy redundancy, which is also the sharpest counterpoint to Matryoshka truncation.

  • Zhang et al. (2025) · Qwen3 Embedding: Advancing Text Embedding and Reranking Through Foundation Models · arXiv 2025

    Qwen3-Embedding pairs an embedder and a reranker trained from the same foundation model — the current open-weights reference in L7's roster.

Neural ranking & dense/sparse retrieval

Bi-encoders, late interaction, learned sparse retrieval and learning-to-rank.
  • Burges et al. (2005) · Learning to Rank using Gradient Descent (RankNet) · ICML 2005

    The original pairwise learning-to-rank loss — a neural net trained on which-of-two-is-better preferences. The starting point of the RankNet→LambdaRank→LambdaMART line in L8.

  • Cao et al. (2007) · Learning to Rank: From Pairwise Approach to Listwise Approach (ListNet) · ICML 2007

    Introduces the listwise learning-to-rank loss — optimize a probability over whole permutations rather than pairs — the ListNet method L8 contrasts with pairwise RankNet.

  • Xia et al. (2008) · Listwise Approach to Learning to Rank: Theory and Algorithm (ListMLE) · ICML 2008

    Gives listwise learning-to-rank a likelihood-based loss with consistency guarantees — the ListMLE algorithm L8 lists alongside ListNet in the listwise family.

  • Burges (2010) · From RankNet to LambdaRank to LambdaMART: An Overview · Microsoft Research Technical Report MSR-TR-2010-82

    The lineage of gradient-boosted learning-to-rank that optimizes nDCG directly — LambdaMART remains the strong classical reranker baseline in L8.

  • Nogueira & Cho (2019) · Passage Re-ranking with BERT (monoBERT) · arXiv:1901.04085

    The canonical Stage-2 cross-encoder re-ranker — feed [query, passage] through BERT and read a relevance score. It is the source of L1's MS MARCO MRR@10 0.167 → 0.365 lift and the monoBERT baseline L7 builds on.

  • Nogueira et al. (2019) · Document Expansion by Query Prediction (Doc2Query) · arXiv 2019 (no peer-reviewed venue)

    Document expansion by query prediction expands documents at index time — the mirror image of query expansion. The doc-side alternative L14 contrasts against query-time rewriting in Act 2.

  • Reimers & Gurevych (2019) · Sentence-BERT: Sentence Embeddings using Siamese BERT-Networks · EMNLP 2019

    SBERT — fine-tunes BERT with a siamese objective so sentence embeddings are directly comparable by cosine. The practical bi-encoder behind dense retrieval, taught in L7.

  • Hofstätter et al. (2020) · Improving Efficient Neural Ranking Models with Cross-Architecture Knowledge Distillation (Margin-MSE) · arXiv:2010.02666

    Introduces the Margin-MSE loss — distil a slow cross-encoder’s score margins into a fast bi-encoder — the cross-architecture distillation recipe L7 cites for cheap-yet-strong dense retrievers.

  • Karpukhin et al. (2020) · Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering (DPR) · EMNLP 2020

    DPR shows a dual-encoder trained with in-batch negatives beats BM25 on open-domain QA — the result that launched dense retrieval, central to L7.

  • Khattab & Zaharia (2020) · ColBERT: Efficient and Effective Passage Search via Contextualized Late Interaction over BERT · SIGIR 2020

    Late interaction: per-token embeddings with a MaxSim operator buy much of a cross-encoder's accuracy at near-bi-encoder cost. The L8 'ColBERT' lecture is built on it.

  • Nogueira, Jiang & Lin (2020) · Document Ranking with a Pretrained Sequence-to-Sequence Model (monoT5) · Findings of EMNLP 2020

    Reframes re-ranking as text generation: a T5 model emits ‘true’/‘false’ for a (query, document) pair and its softmax becomes the score. The monoT5 cross-encoder L7 contrasts with monoBERT.

  • Formal et al. (2021) · SPLADE v2: Sparse Lexical and Expansion Model for Information Retrieval · arXiv:2109.10086

    The second SPLADE iteration — max pooling, distillation, harder negatives — that pushed learned sparse retrieval to competitive effectiveness, part of L8’s sparse-neural lineage.

  • Formal, Piwowarski & Clinchant (2021) · SPLADE: Sparse Lexical and Expansion Model for First Stage Ranking · SIGIR 2021 (short)

    SPLADE learns sparse, inverted-index-friendly term expansions — the 'learned sparse' bridge between BM25 and dense retrieval taught in L8.

  • Gao et al. (2021) · Scaling Deep Contrastive Learning Batch Size under Memory Limited Setup (GradCache) · RepL4NLP @ ACL 2021

    Gradient caching makes batches of tens of thousands of in-batch negatives fit on a single GPU — the systems trick behind E5/BGE's large-batch uniformity, and the practical answer to 'how do you afford 32K negatives?'

  • Hofstätter et al. (2021) · Efficiently Teaching an Effective Dense Retriever with Balanced Topic Aware Sampling (TAS-B) · SIGIR 2021

    Topic-aware balanced sampling + dual-teacher distillation that trains a strong dense retriever cheaply — a key efficiency recipe in L7’s bi-encoder training story.

  • Qu et al. (2021) · RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · NAACL 2021

    The false-negative discovery and its cure: cross-batch negatives + cross-encoder DENOISING + augmentation. Its killer ablation (undenoised hard negatives DROP MRR below in-batch, 32.39→26.03; denoising recovers to 36.38) is L13's climax.

  • Xiong et al. (2021) · Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Learning for Dense Text Retrieval (ANCE) · ICLR 2021 (arXiv 2020)

    Shows that mining hard negatives from the model’s own ANN index during training is what makes a dense retriever strong — the negative-sampling lesson at the heart of L7’s bi-encoder training.

  • Zhan et al. (2021) · Optimizing Dense Retrieval Model Training with Hard Negatives (STAR/ADORE) · SIGIR 2021

    STAR (stable static negatives) + ADORE (per-step DYNAMIC hard negatives by freezing the doc index and training the query encoder): the peak of dynamic mining — MRR@10 0.347, 179× faster than full re-indexing.

  • Bogolin et al. (2022) · Cross Modal Retrieval with Querybank Normalisation · CVPR 2022

    QB-Norm is the production form of the crowding toll in L18: the query-side normalisation that charges a hub for its own dense neighbourhood, applied at retrieval time with a bank of held-out queries.

  • Formal et al. (2022) · From Distillation to Hard Negative Sampling: Making Sparse Neural IR Models More Effective (SPLADE++) · SIGIR 2022

    SPLADE++ — the distillation + hard-negative recipe that makes learned sparse retrieval state-of-the-art; L8 cites its 38.0 / 50.7 effectiveness figures.

  • Izacard et al. (2022) · Unsupervised Dense Information Retrieval with Contrastive Learning (Contriever) · TMLR 2022

    Unsupervised contrastive dense retrieval — a MoCo queue of negatives plus cropping-based positives, no relevance labels at all. Evidence that well-chosen negatives alone can train a competitive retriever (the unsupervised end of L13's modern act).

  • Santhanam et al. (2022) · ColBERTv2: Effective and Efficient Retrieval via Lightweight Late Interaction · NAACL 2022

    Compresses ColBERT’s per-token vectors with residual centroid quantization and denoised supervision — the L8 late-interaction result behind the 39.7 MS MARCO MRR figure.

  • Santhanam et al. (2022) · PLAID: An Efficient Engine for Late Interaction Retrieval · CIKM 2022

    The serving engine that makes ColBERTv2 fast — centroid pruning prunes candidate documents 6,8× and cuts latency ≈45×, the efficiency story L8 tells about late interaction in production. (Venue from CIKM 2022 proceedings, not the arXiv landing page.)

  • Wang et al. (2022) · Text Embeddings by Weakly-Supervised Contrastive Pre-training (E5) · arXiv:2212.03533

    The E5 family of general-purpose text embeddings, weakly-supervised on web text pairs — a strong open bi-encoder L7 names as a modern off-the-shelf retriever.

  • Zhang et al. (2022) · Adversarial Retriever-Ranker for Dense Text Retrieval (AR2) · ICLR 2022

    A minimax retriever–ranker game where the noise-robust ranker damps false-negative harm (MRR@10 39.5; +SimANS 40.9) — the 'co-train' cure for false negatives in L13's arc, alongside filter (RocketQA) and sample (SimANS).

  • Zhou et al. (2022) · SimANS: Simple Ambiguous Negatives Sampling for Dense Text Retrieval · EMNLP 2022 (Industry)

    A gradient analysis (easy→~0, too-hard→high variance from false negatives, ambiguous=sweet) motivates a Gaussian sampler centred at the positive's score. The 'sweet-spot sampling' cure — softens false-neg dominance but cannot, at equal hardness, separate a true from a false negative.

  • Li et al. (2023) · Towards General Text Embeddings with Multi-stage Contrastive Learning (GTE) · arXiv:2308.03281 (2023)

    A modern multi-stage embedding recipe whose headline finding L13 closes on: 'with hard negatives, a large batch is unnecessary' — making explicit the in-batch (uniformity) ↔ mined-hard-neg (boundary) trade-off.

  • Muennighoff et al. (2023) · MTEB: Massive Text Embedding Benchmark · EACL 2023 (arXiv 2022)

    The 8-task, 58-dataset benchmark and public leaderboard that turned ‘which embedding model?’ into a measurable question — L7 points students at it to compare bi-encoders.

  • Sun et al. (2023) · Is ChatGPT Good at Search? Investigating Large Language Models as Re-Ranking Agents (RankGPT) · EMNLP 2023

    Shows an LLM can listwise-rerank passages by permutation prompting — the RankGPT result L7 cites for the rise of LLM-as-reranker.

  • Ma et al. (2024) · Fine-Tuning LLaMA for Multi-Stage Text Retrieval · SIGIR 2024

    RepLLaMA/RankLLaMA fine-tunes LLaMA for both retrieval and reranking, giving the course one reference point for the decoder-as-retriever tier (L7).

  • Xiao et al. (2024) · C-Pack: Packed Resources For General Chinese Embeddings (BGE) · SIGIR 2024

    The BGE/C-Pack recipe — large in-batch contrastive pretraining, then fine-tuning with one ANN-mined hard negative per query. The canonical open instance of L13's 'massive in-batch + a few mined-and-filtered hard negatives' pattern.

  • Zhang et al. (2024) · Adversarial Hubness in Multi-Modal Retrieval · arXiv 2024 · IEEE S&P 2026

    The security edge of L18's hubness: a hub can be MANUFACTURED — a crafted item made near-neighbour to thousands of unrelated queries — which is why N_k measures centrality, never importance.

ANN, vector indexing & quantization

Graph indexes, product quantization and the engineering of billion-scale search.
  • Jégou, Douze & Schmid (2011) · Product Quantization for Nearest Neighbor Search · IEEE TPAMI

    Product quantization — compress vectors into subspace codebooks for fast approximate distances. The compression half of L9's ANN story.

  • Guo et al. (2020) · Accelerating Large-Scale Inference with Anisotropic Vector Quantization (ScaNN) · ICML 2020

    ScaNN's anisotropic quantization loss optimizes the quantizer for inner-product search specifically — a refinement over plain PQ relevant to the L9 quantization material.

  • Malkov & Yashunin (2020) · Efficient and Robust Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search using Hierarchical Navigable Small World Graphs (HNSW) · IEEE TPAMI

    HNSW — the graph index behind nearly every production vector database. The core ANN algorithm of L9.

  • Johnson, Douze & Jégou (2021) · Billion-Scale Similarity Search with GPUs (FAISS) · IEEE Transactions on Big Data

    FAISS — the library and the GPU engineering that made billion-vector similarity search routine. The tooling students use for the L9 ANN lab.

  • Gao & Long (2024) · RaBitQ: Quantizing High-Dimensional Vectors with a Theoretical Error Bound for Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search · SIGMOD 2024

    The counterweight to PQ in L9: a quantizer whose codebook is NOT learned — rotated hypercube vertices — and which therefore carries a sharp error bound. Names the failure PQ hides: on MSong, PQ passes 50% average relative error and cannot reach 60% recall even after re-ranking.

  • Munyampirwa et al. (2024) · Down with the Hierarchy: The 'H' in HNSW Stands for "Hubs" · arXiv 2024

    The bridge from L18's hubness to L9's index: the Hub Highway Hypothesis says hubs form a connected sub-network that already does the job of HNSW's hierarchy — so a hub is not only a defect, it is sometimes the routing layer.

  • Zandieh et al. (2025) · TurboQuant: Online Vector Quantization with Near-optimal Distortion Rate · arXiv (Google Research)

    A recent data-oblivious quantizer with near-optimal distortion — the modern ANN reading paired with the turbovec lab tool in L9.

  • Shi et al. (2026) · GPU-Native Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search with IVF-RaBitQ: Fast Index Build and Search · arXiv (NVIDIA cuVS)

    Where RaBitQ landed in production: integrated into NVIDIA cuVS as IVF-RaBitQ. At recall ≈ 0.95 it gives 3.0× the QPS of the graph method CAGRA and builds indexes 14.7× faster; against IVF-PQ, 4.5× the throughput without touching raw vectors for re-ranking.

  • Xu et al. (2026) · Through the Lens of Hubness: A Revisit on Graph-Based Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search · PACMMOD 4(3) / SIGMOD 2026

    The other side of L18's hubness coin, and the bridge back to L9: hubness skews the proximity graph so badly that the greedy walk strands on isolated anti-hubs — which is why graph-based ANN has a long-tail latency problem at all.

  • Codrai · turbovec — a TurboQuant-style ANN index · GitHub (L9 lab tool)

    An open-source community demo of a TurboQuant-style ANN index — the hands-on tool for the L9 vector-search lab.

RAG, generation & multimodal retrieval

Retrieval-augmented generation, its evaluation, agentic & multimodal variants.
  • Yang et al. (2018) · HotpotQA: A Dataset for Diverse, Explainable Multi-hop Question Answering · EMNLP 2018

    The multi-hop QA benchmark requiring reasoning across several documents — the dataset L12 cites to motivate query decomposition and multi-step retrieval.

  • Lewis et al. (2020) · Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks · NeurIPS 2020

    The paper that named RAG — couple a retriever with a generator so the model cites real sources. The whole second arc of the course (L10-L12) is built on it.

  • Mao et al. (2021) · Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering (GAR) · ACL 2021

    Generation-Augmented Retrieval generates and appends contexts to the query for sparse retrieval + reader. The antecedent of the expansion branch (→ Query2doc) — L14's Act-1 bridge from classical PRF to the LLM quill.

  • Radford et al. (2021) · Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision (CLIP) · ICML 2021

    CLIP aligns images and text in one embedding space — the foundation of multimodal and cross-modal retrieval introduced in L12.

  • Khot et al. (2022) · Decomposed Prompting: A Modular Approach for Solving Complex Tasks (DecomP) · ICLR 2023 (arXiv 2022)

    Decomposed Prompting (DecomP) frames decomposition as a modular library of reusable, possibly-recursive sub-task programs. The architecture flavor L14 contrasts against least-to-most and self-ask in Act 5.

  • Press et al. (2022) · Measuring and Narrowing the Compositionality Gap in Language Models (Self-Ask) · Findings of EMNLP 2023 (arXiv 2022)

    Self-ask has the model emit explicit follow-up questions before answering, narrowing the compositionality gap. The elicitation flavor of decomposition in L14's Act 5.

  • Zhou et al. (2022) · Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models · ICLR 2023 (arXiv 2022)

    Least-to-most prompting solves the simplest sub-problem first and feeds the answer forward. The ordering flavor of decomposition L14 contrasts in Act 5 (SCAN 99.7% vs 16.2% chain-of-thought).

  • Es et al. (2023) · RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation · EACL 2024 (system demonstrations)

    RAGAS — reference-free metrics (faithfulness, answer/context relevance) for RAG pipelines. How L11 measures whether a RAG system is actually grounded.

  • Gao et al. (2023) · Precise Zero-Shot Dense Retrieval without Relevance Labels (HyDE) · ACL 2023

    HyDE — let an LLM hallucinate a hypothetical answer, then retrieve with its embedding. A query-transformation trick taught in the L10 RAG-retrieval material.

  • Greshake et al. (2023) · Not what you've signed up for: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection · AISec 2023

    The paper that named indirect prompt injection: retrieved content is untrusted input — the security frame for L12's RAG-security act.

  • Jagerman et al. (2023) · Query Expansion by Prompting Large Language Models · arXiv 2023 (no peer-reviewed venue)

    Query expansion by prompting an LLM (Q2D / chain-of-thought / few-shot) to generate expansion terms; CoT-style expansion is strong. An arXiv-only companion to Query2doc in L14's Act 2.

  • Ma et al. (2023) · Query Rewriting for Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models (Rewrite-Retrieve-Read) · EMNLP 2023

    Rewrite-Retrieve-Read (RRR) trains a small language-model rewriter by reinforcement learning on the downstream answer reward. The 'trainable quill' that headlines L14's Act 6 — the one axis L10 lacks entirely.

  • Saito et al. (2023) · Verbosity Bias in Preference Labeling by Large Language Models · arXiv (NeurIPS 2023 workshop)

    Names the verbosity bias L11's judge example hits: LLM judges systematically prefer longer answers — the failure behind the perfect-score finding.

  • Wang, Yang & Wei (2023) · Query2doc: Query Expansion with Large Language Models · EMNLP 2023

    Query2doc expands the query with an LLM-generated pseudo-document — the sparse-index twin of HyDE. The Act-2 technique L14 uses to close the vocabulary gap on BM25 (+3–15%, largest on sparse / zero-shot).

  • Yao et al. (2023) · ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models · ICLR 2023

    ReAct interleaves chain-of-thought reasoning with tool calls — the agent loop behind the agentic-RAG material in L11.

  • Zheng et al. (2023) · Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena · NeurIPS 2023 (Datasets & Benchmarks)

    MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena established LLM-as-a-judge — using a strong model to score model outputs — plus its known biases. The methodology L11 builds its judging material on.

  • Zheng et al. (2023) · Take a Step Back: Evoking Reasoning via Abstraction in Large Language Models · ICLR 2024 (arXiv 2023)

    Step-Back prompting: derive a higher-level question first, retrieve against that, then answer — the query-abstraction technique L10 cites for query understanding.

  • Asai et al. (2024) · Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection · ICLR 2024

    Self-RAG trains the model to decide when to retrieve and to critique its own output with reflection tokens — the adaptive-retrieval idea in L11.

  • Chen et al. (2024) · Dense X Retrieval: What Retrieval Granularity Should We Use? · EMNLP

    Argues for proposition-level retrieval units — an alternative answer to the chunk-granularity problem late chunking (L16) tackles by pooling late instead.

  • Edge et al. (2024) · From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization · arXiv (Microsoft Research)

    GraphRAG builds a knowledge graph and community summaries so RAG can answer global, corpus-wide questions — the graph-structured retrieval taught in L12.

  • Faysse et al. (2024) · ColPali: Efficient Document Retrieval with Vision Language Models · ICLR 2025

    ColPali retrieves over document page-images with late interaction, skipping OCR entirely — the visual-document retrieval frontier in L12.

  • Günther et al. (2024) · Late Chunking: Contextual Chunk Embeddings Using Long-Context Embedding Models · arXiv:2409.04701

    Embed the whole long document first, then pool per-chunk vectors — so each chunk’s embedding carries document context. The chunking-strategy result L10 cites for retrieval quality.

  • Jeong et al. (2024) · Adaptive-RAG: Learning to Adapt Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models through Question Complexity · NAACL 2024

    Adaptive-RAG routes queries by complexity — no-retrieval, single-step, or multi-step — the adaptive-retrieval strategy in L11's agentic material.

  • Liu et al. (2024) · Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts · TACL 2024

    Shows that LLMs attend most reliably to the start and end of a long context and skim the middle — the empirical basis for L10's 'lost in the middle' argument that a bigger window does not replace ranking.

  • Sarthi et al. (2024) · RAPTOR: Recursive Abstractive Processing for Tree-Organized Retrieval · ICLR 2024

    Builds a tree of recursively-summarized chunks so retrieval can pull either fine detail or a high-level abstract — the hierarchical-chunking idea cited in L10 and L12.

  • Yan et al. (2024) · Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation (CRAG) · arXiv

    CRAG adds a lightweight retrieval evaluator that triggers correction or web search when retrieved context is weak — the robustness layer of L11's RAG variants.

  • Merola & Singh (2025) · Reconstructing Context: Evaluating Advanced Chunking Strategies for Retrieval-Augmented Generation · arXiv:2504.19754 — 2nd Workshop on Knowledge-Enhanced Information Retrieval (KEIR @ ECIR 2025)

    The INDEPENDENT replication L16 needed: late chunking and contextual retrieval, re-evaluated by neither method's authors. Its verdict is that late chunking does not consistently beat early pooling across models and datasets — on BGE-M3/NFCorpus it collapses, and head-to-head against contextual retrieval it loses.

  • Zou et al. (2025) · PoisonedRAG: Knowledge Corruption Attacks to Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Large Language Models · USENIX Security 2025

    PoisonedRAG shows a handful of injected passages can steer a RAG answer — the corpus-poisoning threat model taught in L12.

Textbooks & course reading

The book-length references the syllabus reading list is built from.
  • Manning, Raghavan & Schütze (2008) · Introduction to Information Retrieval · Cambridge University Press (free online edition)

    The standard IR textbook — inverted indexes, scoring, evaluation. The chapter-level reference behind L1, L3 and L4.

  • Croft, Metzler & Strohman (2009) · Search Engines: Information Retrieval in Practice · Addison-Wesley / Pearson

    A practice-oriented IR textbook covering crawling, indexing and ranking end-to-end — the applied companion to Manning et al. on L1’s reading list.

  • Turnbull & Berryman (2016) · Relevant Search: With Applications for Solr and Elasticsearch · Manning Publications

    The practitioner's guide to relevance engineering with Solr/Elasticsearch — the hands-on counterpart to the classical-IR theory in L3.

  • Zinkevich (2017) · Rules of Machine Learning: Best Practices for ML Engineering · Google Developers

    Google’s field guide of 43 ML-engineering rules — launch without ML first, get the infrastructure right, make metrics a priority — the engineering-discipline spine of L1’s system-design half.

  • Teofili (2019) · Deep Learning for Search · Manning Publications

    The course's namesake textbook — neural methods applied to search, end to end. A primary reading for the second half of the course.

  • Lin, Nogueira & Yates (2021) · Pretrained Transformers for Text Ranking: BERT and Beyond · Springer / Morgan & Claypool (also arXiv)

    The definitive survey of transformer-based text ranking — cross-encoders, dense retrieval, late interaction. The reference text for L7-L8.

  • Huyen (2022) · Designing Machine Learning Systems · O'Reilly

    The practical reference for productionizing ML — data, training pipelines, deployment, monitoring — that anchors L1’s ML-system-design half.

  • Babushkin & Kravchenko (2025) · Machine Learning System Design: With End-to-End Examples · Manning Publications

    How to design ML systems end to end — the system-thinking reference behind L1's 'a model is not a system' theme.

foundations

  • Shannon (1950) · Programming a Computer for Playing Chess · Philosophical Magazine, ser. 7, 41(314)

    The first technical paper on computer chess: the move tree, the evaluation function and bounded search depth — the same man who defined information started the line that leads to AI.

  • McCarthy et al. (1955) · A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence · AI Magazine 27(4), 2006

    The 31 August 1955 proposal where the phrase 'artificial intelligence' is written for the first time — and Shannon signs it alongside McCarthy, Minsky and Rochester.

models

  • Warner et al. (2024) · Smarter, Better, Faster, Longer: A Modern Bidirectional Encoder for Fast, Memory Efficient, and Long Context Finetuning and Inference · arXiv (ModernBERT)

    What happened to the encoder in the six years after BERT: the skeleton stayed, the parts changed — RoPE instead of absolute positions, GeGLU, a 128-token local window alternating with a global layer every third block, and unpadding. Result: a native 8192 context at a comparable size (149M base, 395M large), trained on 2T tokens.

  • Marone et al. (2025) · mmBERT: A Modern Multilingual Encoder with Annealed Language Learning · arXiv (mmBERT)

    The multilingual branch of the same generation, and the one that matters for a course taught in three languages: 3T tokens over 1800+ languages, with 1700 low-resource ones mixed in ONLY during the decay phase — a small amount of data placed late, which lifts exactly the languages that usually get nothing.
