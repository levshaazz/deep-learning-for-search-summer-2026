How Much Is a Letter Worth?

Shannon Entropy — the entropy of language

From the telegraph to the language model. Information is surprise; entropy H = −Σ p log₂ p is expected surprise and the hard floor of compression. English carries ~1 bit per letter (Shannon's guessing game) — and that floor is exactly what a language model's cross-entropy loss chases. IDF is Shannon self-information.

The Wiring Diagram

The Wiring Diagram — neural circuit diagrams: the axes, and the bill

A formula tells you what to compute; it hides WHAT IT COSTS and WHICH AXIS IT EATS. A circuit diagram shows both: a wire is an axis, a cup is a contraction where an axis dies, a triangle is a softmax across an axis — and the cost of every glyph is one counting rule away. Read the bill off the picture: attention is only 10% of a BERT block at n=512, and 88% at n=32768, with the crossover at exactly n = 6d. Then meet the bug that no shape check can ever catch.

The Curved Map

The Curved Map — geometry of meaning: anisotropy & hubness

Why "similar" vectors sometimes behave badly. Embedding spaces are distorted maps: contextual vectors crowd into a narrow cone (anisotropy — random words look 0.99-similar), and a few points sit in everyone's neighbour list (hubness). Cosine lies. The fix: re-project the map (center → whiten → contrastive isotropy) and tax the crowded hubs (CSLS).

Read the Whole Book First

Late Chunking — contextual chunk embeddings for long documents

Chunk-then-embed severs global context: a chunk whose subject is a pronoun loses its referent. Late chunking embeds the WHOLE document first (full-document attention resolves the coreference), then pools per chunk — one vector per chunk, free at query time. Change WHEN you pool, not the model.
