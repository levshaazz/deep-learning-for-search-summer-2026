Homeworks A3

Build the RAG retriever, then judge it

Chunk an enterprise corpus and measure the recall floor; lift retrieval with multi-query, HyDE and RAG-Fusion; then evaluate it by hand (context precision & recall from gold answer-facts) and expose an LLM-judge’s position, verbosity and Goodhart biases — plus one paper essay.

Builds on the EnterpriseRAG-Bench setup from Lab 3 — you reuse your Lab-1 BM25 and Lab-2 dense-retriever CODE, re-indexed on this corpus (not the Lab-3 ANN index; exact search is fine at this scale). Part 1 builds the retrieval half of a RAG system on a real enterprise corpus and learns not to trust it blindly; Part 2 is a close reading of one paper with a hand-drawn re-interpretation of its hero figure. The central frame is the recall floor: if the answer is not in the retrieved context, no generator can recover it — so the retriever and its evaluation are where quality is won or lost.

Dataset & setup

Use EnterpriseRAG-Bench over a fixed ≥200-question subset (state which; e.g. the first 200 by question_id). Each question carries expected_doc_ids (ground-truth documents — relevance is BINARY here, so nDCG uses gains 0/1 and any un-retrieved doc counts as rel 0), a gold_answer, and answer_facts (a list of atomic facts the answer must contain) — the answer_facts are what make context recall hand-computable without a full generator. STREAM the ~500K-doc corpus and keep a laptop subset = the source_type slices your questions reference (seed-20260605 sample if large) UNION every expected_doc_id of your evaluated questions, so no gold document is ever dropped (a dropped gold doc silently caps recall). Reuse your Lab-1 BM25 and Lab-2 dense-retriever CODE, re-indexed on this corpus. Retrieval unit: rank CHUNKS; a chunk is relevant iff its parent doc_id ∈ expected_doc_ids. Slice every result by question_type (values are lowercase, e.g. "basic", "conflicting_info", "info_not_found", …) — the categories are the whole point of this benchmark.

Part 1 — Coding & experiments (60%)

  1. Chunking study (by hand, pure string ops): implement fixed-size and recursive (paragraph → sentence → word) chunking, sizes/overlaps in tokens. On the long enterprise documents, sweep chunk size × overlap and measure recall as answer-fact containment — is a gold answer_fact "present" in some chunk of its expected_doc? Fix ONE match rule and reuse it everywhere: a fact is present in a chunk iff cosine(fact, chunk) ≥ 0.6 under your Lab-2 encoder (state your threshold; a fact rarely appears verbatim, so exact string match is too strict). Treat this as a proxy, not semantic ground truth. Report the chunk-count formula n = 0 for L=0, otherwise max(1, ⌈(L−overlap)/(size−overlap)⌉); require integer L≥0 and 0≤overlap<size, exhibit one fact split across a chunk boundary at overlap 0 (present 0) that a small overlap rescues (present 1), and report proxy fact coverage: the fraction of answer_facts that pass this match rule. This is NOT a lower or upper bound on generator accuracy: similarity does not establish entailment, and multiple chunks may jointly supply a fact. Validate a hand-labelled sample (including negations and split facts) and discuss false matches/misses.
  2. Query rewriting (retrieval lift): implement three techniques that edit the query and leave the retriever untouched — multi-query (retrieve several paraphrases, then merge into ONE ranking by scoring each document by the BEST (max) similarity it got across the paraphrase runs, so MRR/nDCG are well-defined — a bare set-union is not a ranking), HyDE (embed a hypothetical answer and retrieve near it), and RAG-Fusion (merge the paraphrase runs with RRF, k=60, by hand). Generate the paraphrases/hypotheticals with a small local generator, or hand-write a fixed set and commit it to your repo for reproducibility. Measure recall@k, MRR and nDCG lift over the plain query, and explain each result through the vocabulary gap between a short question and a long jargon-rich enterprise document.
  3. RAG evaluation by hand (no RAGAS library): context precision = mean AP over questions (divide per-query AP by ALL relevant parent docs in qrels), but DEDUP chunks to their parent document first (collapse the chunk ranking to the first-seen rank of each distinct doc_id, then average precision@k at the ranks of the relevant docs) — otherwise several near-duplicate chunks of one gold doc pin precision near 1 and the metric drifts with chunk size. Proxy context recall = (# gold answer_facts matched in at least one retrieved chunk) / (# answer_facts), using the SAME cosine ≥ 0.6 match rule as the chunking study — generator-free. Report both per question_type. For the info_not_found category the metrics are N/A (no relevant docs, empty answer_facts → 0/0): instead report an ABSTENTION metric — the fraction of those questions whose top-1 chunk similarity falls below a stated threshold τ (correct "retrieve nothing confident" behaviour) — and contrast it with a control category.
  4. LLM-judge bias lab: this needs a small frozen local judge (an ~8B instruct model, temperature 0, fixed seed) — the position and verbosity biases are empirical rates a judge must emit, they cannot be done "by hand." First build ~15–20 answer PAIRS from the data: for each of several questions take its gold_answer as answer A, and construct B two ways — (i) a paraphrase of A (a genuine tie, for position) and (ii) A padded with on-topic filler that adds no new fact (same content, longer, for verbosity). Give the judge a fixed 1–5 rubric (relevance, grounding, completeness). Measure: position — run each tie in BOTH A/B orderings, report the position-follow rate; verbosity — report how often the longer answer wins. Then, PURELY BY HAND (no model), reproduce the Goodhart weight-flip: honest A = mean[5,5,3] = 4.3333 beats padded C = mean[4,3,5] = 4.0, but re-weight completeness ×2 and C = (4+3+2·5)/4 = 4.25 wins with no word changed — the rubric weights are the attack surface. Finally implement swap-and-average (count a win only if the same answer wins both orderings) and re-measure the position rate; report the reduction.
  5. Metrics & significance: over your best rewrite vs the BM25 baseline, run a paired test (t-test and/or Wilcoxon signed-rank) on per-query context precision — mean difference, 95% CI, p-value, and an honest interpretation (necessary-not-sufficient; the multiple-comparisons caveat once you sweep many configs).
  6. Report: plots + tables + a discussion tying proxy fact coverage, the rewrite lift, the by-hand RAG metrics, and the judge-bias findings into one story — where quality is actually won in a RAG pipeline, and why you cannot optimise hard against an unaudited judge.

Stepik autograder — control numbers

The by-hand RAG logic is checked by a deterministic autograder (stepik/graders/grader_a3_rag.py): a 52-token mini-memo with gold answer-facts, a frozen chunk-ranking and two frozen judge-verdict lists are embedded in the checker. One substitution: the cosine >= 0.6 fact-match rule of the full homework becomes a token-overlap rule (>= 0.8 of the fact’s token set) — no encoder on the runner. Tolerance 1e-4; the EnterpriseRAG-Bench study and the live LLM-judge rates go into the report (peer-reviewed).

Part 2 — Paper reading & hand-written essay (40%)

Pick one of the papers below, read it closely, and write a two-page A4 hand-written essay in your own words — your interpretation, not a summary. Then complete the hero-figure sub-task.

Hero-figure sub-task. Identify the paper’s hero figure — the one diagram that carries its central idea. Redesign it your own way (hand-drawn is fine) to expose the concept more deeply than the original: change the encoding, add the missing axis or annotation, show the failure case, or re-draw it using your own Homework numbers (your recall-floor curve, your rewrite lift, your judge-bias rates). Add a short caption stating what your version makes visible that the original did not.

Submission

A photo/scan of the two-page hand-written essay, the redesigned hero figure (hand-drawn or digital), and one paragraph naming the figure you chose and why it is the hero figure.

Choose one paper

  1. Lewis, P. et al. (2020). Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks. NeurIPS.The original RAG. Hero figure: the retrieve-then-generate architecture with non-parametric memory — redesign it to show the recall floor (what generation can and cannot recover).
  2. Gao, L., Ma, X., Lin, J. & Callan, J. (2023). Precise Zero-Shot Dense Retrieval without Relevance Labels (HyDE). ACL.Search with an embedded hypothetical answer. Hero figure: the question→hypothetical-answer→embedding→retrieve flow — redraw it on your own before/after ranks.
  3. Günther, M. et al. (Jina AI, 2024). Late Chunking: Contextual Chunk Embeddings Using Long-Context Embedding Models.Embed the whole document first, chunk after the transformer. Hero figure: the isolation defect vs contextual chunk embeddings — show what context is restored.
  4. Liu, N. F. et al. (2024). Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts. TACL.Long-context position bias. Hero figure: the U-shaped accuracy-vs-position curve — redraw it to argue why ranking still matters even with huge context windows.
  5. Es, S., James, J., Espinosa-Anke, L. & Schockaert, S. (2023). RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation.Reference-free decomposition into faithfulness / answer relevance / context precision / context recall. Hero element: the four-metric 2×2 (answer vs context) — redesign it as a diagnostic that localises blame between retriever and generator.
  6. Zheng, L. et al. (2023). Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena. NeurIPS.The LLM-judge and its biases. Hero element: the position / verbosity / self-enhancement bias table — redraw it with your own measured bias rates and the swap-and-average correction.
  7. Yao, S. et al. (2023). ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models. ICLR.Interleaved reasoning + acting for multi-hop retrieval. Hero figure: the thought→action→observation loop — redraw it as a recall-by-hop curve (single-shot recall 0 → multi-hop recall 1).
